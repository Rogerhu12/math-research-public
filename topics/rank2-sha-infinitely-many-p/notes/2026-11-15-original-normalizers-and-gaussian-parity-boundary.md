# Original normalizers, Gaussian CM bases, and the exact parity boundary

This is a supplement to [R736](../results/R736-original-fixed-brandt-vector-and-complete-first-wild-incidence.md). It retains its primitive integral vector $f_{\rm int}$ and all its local conditions.

## 1. The full right-$j$ operator

Right multiplication by the global $j$ normalizes the original order. In the finite model of R736 it is

$$
\mathcal J(z_2,z_{17},k_3)
=(\bar z_2,\bar z_{17},k_3[j]).
\tag{1}
$$

The conjugations at $2$ and $17$ are right normalizer actions, not the left fractional-linear actions of the matrix of $j$. The factor $[j]$ at $3$ is retained.

The operator commutes with all good Hecke operators. The original rational eigenline is one-dimensional, so its eigenvalue is $+1$ or $-1$. The complete fixed-vector computation gives

$$
f_{\rm int}\circ\mathcal J=f_{\rm int}.
\tag{2}
$$

This is an equality on all 368 classes, not a claim inferred from the root sign.

## 2. Gaussian square bases and actual inversion

Suppose $p\equiv5\pmod {12}$. Choose a Gaussian prime
$\varpi=a+bj\in\mathbb Z[j]$ of norm $p$, and put

$$
\beta=\varpi^2,\qquad \operatorname{nrd}(\beta)=p^2.
\tag{3}
$$

It is primitive in $\mathcal O$, and $\beta$ commutes with $j$.

Choose the conductor-$p^2$ base whose local cyclic image is the image of $\beta$. This is a translation of the same complete CM orbit, not a change of the universal vector. More explicitly, take the adele with component $\beta$ at $p$ and component $1$ elsewhere. For a toric parameter $\alpha$ at $p$, right multiplication by global $j$ and left multiplication by global $j^{-1}$ give

$$
(\alpha\beta)_p\longmapsto(\bar\alpha\beta)_p
\tag{4}
$$

and return every other finite component to $1$. Equation (4) uses the actual commutation in (3). Hence (2) makes the function on this packet invariant under inversion of the ring-class parameter.

The class $d_0=[\delta]$ is the original order-two toric class. It fixes $f_{\rm int}$, lies in the tame group $\Delta_p$, and has zero wild digit. Thus the row through the Gaussian base descends to a function

$$
F:\Delta_p/\langle d_0\rangle\longrightarrow\mathbb Z,
\qquad F(x^{-1})=F(x).
\tag{5}
$$

The quotient is cyclic of odd order $(p+1)/6$. Its only inversion-fixed element is the identity. If $\mathcal P$ chooses one element from each nonidentity inversion pair, the complete row is

$$
\frac{A_0^{\rm int}}2
=f_{\rm int}(\bar\beta\cdot1)
+2\sum_{x\in\mathcal P}F(x).
\tag{6}
$$

The subscript zero refers to the translated wild labels of this actual base. In previously fixed labels it is the one row obtained by the recorded ring-class translation. The assertion that every first-wild row vanishes is unchanged.

## 3. The fixed Gaussian residues are all even

For the bad-place maps in R736, the class of $\bar\beta$ depends only on
$(a,b)\pmod {102}$. Conditions needed here are

$$
(a^2+b^2,102)=1,\qquad
a^2+b^2\equiv2\pmod3,\qquad a+b\equiv1\pmod2.
\tag{7}
$$

The producer checks every such pair. There are 2048 records, and their actual original vector values are

$$
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
f_{\rm int}(\bar\beta\cdot1)&0&2&\text{any odd value}\\ \hline
\text{number of records}&512&1536&0.
\end{array}
\tag{8}
$$

Thus the simplest parity witness does not occur. This is not an omission of a prime residue class: (8) covers the entire fixed residue set (7).

In particular (6) gives the genuine divisibility

$$
A_0^{\rm int}\in4\mathbb Z
\tag{9}
$$

for these bases. If $v=f_{\rm int}(\bar\beta\cdot1)\in\{0,2\}$, then the exact next condition is

$$
\frac{A_0^{\rm int}}4
=\frac v2+\sum_{x\in\mathcal P}F(x).
\tag{10}
$$

Reduction modulo two of the final sum is the parity of the number of pairs in $\mathcal P$ at which the original vector is odd. Those pairs are still selected by the full anti-Fermat matching of R736. Therefore (8) does not determine (10) modulo two.

In particular, a zero first-wild family forces that parity to equal $v/2$, but (8) alone does not contradict it. No modulo-four or modulo-eight nonvanishing is being asserted.

## 4. Which further normalizer fields preserve the same vector?

An element normalizing $K$ has the form $\alpha$ or $\alpha j$, with
$\alpha\in K^\times$. If $\alpha j$ is to normalize the same order at every place, then at a good split rational prime $\ell$ its two valuations must agree: the normalizer of $M_2(\mathbb Z_\ell)$ is
$\mathbb Q_\ell^\times\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb Z_\ell)$.

After removing a rational scalar, the only allowed valuations are therefore at the original bad primes. The primes $2,17$ are inert in $K$ and their uniformizers are rational; the only remaining nonrational valuation is that of $\delta$ at $3$. Since $K$ has class number one,

$$
\alpha\in \mathbb Q^\times\mu_6\delta^{\mathbb Z}.
\tag{11}
$$

Consequently the quadratic field generated by $\alpha j$ is, up to a rational square in its defining norm,

$$
\mathbb Q(i)\quad\text{or}\quad\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3}).
\tag{12}
$$

The embeddings $\omega^i j$ of the first field are conjugate by original global units, so their square-orbit values are already covered by (8).

For the second field the prime $p$ is inert. Any integral element of norm $p^2$ is $p$ times a unit in its maximal order; if it lies in $\mathcal O$, the same unit lies in $\mathcal O$. Thus it is not a primitive element of the norm-$p^2$ packet. This second field supplies no omitted primitive base of the kind (3).

Allowing a new split valuation of $\alpha$ changes the local level and the vector. Such a change might be useful, but its actual local translation or projection has to be paid. It is not another free normalizer of the present universal vector.
