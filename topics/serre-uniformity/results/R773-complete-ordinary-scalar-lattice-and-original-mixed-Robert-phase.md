# R773: the complete ordinary scalar-layer lattice and the original mixed Robert phase

Date: 2026-11-14. **Status: proved.** The original mixed scalar
Robert unit has the exact marked q phase $r_\mu(c_s)=9$.
This follows from reduction of the specified q-torsion product,
without enumerating that torsion or replacing the ordinary unit.

If the original $b$ is nonzero, the whole ordinary character unit
lattice in $V=LF_s=M^T$ is generated by the original $u_s,c_s,y$.
An integral presentation and its complete invariant mod-p
subspace are given below. The true dual class descended in R768
has an explicit form in this presentation; in particular its
full direct q phase is zero. This last statement concerns a unit
representative over $V$, and does not evaluate R755's rational
regularized cochain pairing.

## 1. Actual units and the scalar field

Keep all original markings, $p=43$, $\mathcal O=\mathbf Z_p[i]$,
$k=\mathcal O/p$, $\Gamma=\langle\gamma\rangle$,
$S=\langle s\rangle$, $T=\langle t\rangle$, and
$M=LB$. Put $V=M^T=LF_s$, $\pi=\gamma-1$, and

$$
D=D_\Gamma^{\rm bal},\qquad
D_S=\sum_{j=0}^{42}js^j,\qquad
H_{11}(s)=\sum_{j=0}^{10}s^j,\qquad a=15i/7.
$$

The augmentation of $H_{11}$ is $11$, so it is a unit
of the local ring $\mathcal O[S]$.
The actual ordinary units of R561/R613 satisfy

$$
\begin{gathered}
N_Su_s=u,\qquad N_\Gamma y=u,\\
N_Sc_s=c_q,\qquad
N_\Gamma c_s=(1-s^{11})u_s=-(s-1)H_{11}u_s,\\
py=u+aDc_q,\qquad
\pi y=a\gamma^{22}c_q,\qquad
\pi D=p\gamma^{22}-N_\Gamma.
\end{gathered}
\tag{773.1}
$$

Here $u_s$ is Gamma fixed, $y$ is S fixed, and all norms
are norms of the specified ordinary units. The original
unaveraged $P_+$ is unchanged.

The exact coefficient comparison already proved in R720 is

$$
C=9ib,\qquad r_\mu(y)=25+9b,\qquad r_\mu(u_s)=25.
\tag{773.2}
$$

The value of $b$ is not assumed in the unconditional
phase calculation of §2. Sections 3--8 assume $b\ne0$.
This includes the original possible obstruction value $24$.

## 2. The actual q-torsion product gives r_mu(c_s) = 9

R613 §4 defines, in the actual torsion field,

$$
H_q=(\mathbf F_{q^2}^{\times})^{43},
\qquad m_q=|H_q|=\frac{q^2-1}{43},
$$
$$
c_{B,\rm raw}
=P_+\left[\prod_{k\in\mu_{42}}\prod_{h\in H_q}
 v(P_2+[k]R+[h]Q_q)\right],
\qquad c_B=s^{11}c_{B,\rm raw},
\tag{773.3}
$$

where $pR=P$, $Q_q$ is the same nonzero q-torsion point,
$P_2=(i,1-i)$, and
$v(Q)=64^{10}/\psi_{11,A}(Q)$.
The corresponding pure-p product is $u_B$, and
$c_s=N_Tc_B$, $u_s=N_Tu_B$.
In particular the actual subgroup size in (773.3) is
$(q^2-1)/43$, with no extra division by $4$.

The curve has good supersingular reduction at the original
inert prime q. Concretely $q\equiv3\pmod4$, and pairing
$x$ and $-x$ in the quadratic-character count of
$y^2=x^3-x$ gives trace zero. The usual Frobenius
equation is therefore $\operatorname{Fr}_q^2=[-q]$,
as used with this same curve in R686.
On the reduced curve, multiplication by q is thus purely
inseparable. Its geometric q-torsion has only the identity.
Every q-torsion point in characteristic zero consequently
specializes to the identity at a q-adic place.

The point $P_2+[k]R$ has exact order $4p^2$, prime to q.
Good reduction preserves that order. In particular it is
neither the identity nor a nonzero 11-torsion point.
The function $v$ is regular and nonzero at its reduction:
its discriminant numerator is a q-unit and its denominator
has no zero at that point. Thus specialization of every
individual factor in (773.3) gives

$$
\overline{v(P_2+[k]R+[h]Q_q)}
=\overline{v(P_2+[k]R)}.
$$

Taking the actual products, character projection, and
the specified $s^{11}$ shift yields the full residue identity

$$
\boxed{\overline{c_B}
       =\bigl(\overline{s^{11}u_B}\bigr)^{m_q}.}
\tag{773.4}
$$

It is an equality in the residue multiplicative module,
before extracting an order-p phase. It holds at every
original T and Delta translate by the same specialization.
Consequently the original T norm gives

$$
\overline{c_s}=\bigl(\overline{s^{11}u_s}\bigr)^{m_q}.
\tag{773.5}
$$

The entire p-primary residue of $u_s$ is in $\mu_p$,
by the actual norm relation $N_Su_s=u$ and the zero
p-primary residue of $u$. The marked scalar action
$s=1+2p$ fixes this $\mu_p$ subgroup. The original q
congruence and R561's actual finite input give

$$
m_q\equiv21\pmod{43},\qquad r_\mu(u_s)=25.
$$

Hence (773.5) proves

$$
\boxed{r_\mu(c_s)=21\cdot25=9\pmod{43}.}
\tag{773.6}
$$

No new numerical window or large q-torsion calculation was run.
The exact number follows from the already published q congruence
and scalar Robert evaluation, together with the displayed
factor-by-factor reduction proof. Neither a q-inverse shift,
a tame weight, nor a normalizing root of unity has been changed.

## 3. For nonzero b the exact ordinary Gamma norm image

Assume $b\ne0$. R684 and R715 give

$$
A_V=A_M/(t-1)A_M=k,\qquad
[P_+Q_V]=0,\qquad j_{M/V}=0.
\tag{773.7}
$$

The first equality also makes Gamma act trivially on $A_V$.
The ordinary class group of $F_s$ is zero: the class norm
$A_B\to A_{F_s}$ is onto because $B/F_s$ is ramified
of prime degree at p, while $A_B=0$.
The same primitive-norm argument as R657 gives the
entire saturated lattice

$$
E_{F_s}=\mathcal O[S]u_s.
\tag{773.8}
$$

Indeed $N_Su_s=u$ is primitive in $E_F$, so the map from
the regular group ring is nonzero on its mod-p socle;
Dirichlet rank and Nakayama prove (773.8).

In the Gamma extension $V/F_s$, the ramified character
divisor quotient is the single line $k$ generated by the
marked q prime. Its image in $A_V$ is zero by (773.7).
Every strongly ambiguous class is generated by these
ramified divisors, since $A_{F_s}=0$. Thus

$$
A_V^{\rm st}=0,\qquad A_V^\Gamma=k,\qquad
H^1(\Gamma,\mathscr P_V)=k.
\tag{773.9}
$$

Here $\mathscr P_V$ is the module of all principal divisors.
The full ordinary multiplicative sequence identifies (773.9)
with the difference between field norms of ordinary base
units and norms of ordinary units.

Every unit of $F_s$ in the present character is a field norm
from $V$. At q its tame norm obstruction is zero:
$u_s$ has p-primary residue in $\mu_p$ inside the upper
residue group $\mu_{p^2}$, hence that residue is a p-th power.
All its S translates have the same property.
At p and 2 the Gamma extension is split; elsewhere it is
unramified and the input is a local unit.
The cyclic norm theorem therefore applies.
It follows from (773.9) that the ordinary norm index is $|k|$.

The actual norms of $u_s$ and $c_s$ in (773.1) already
generate $(p,s-1)\mathcal O[S]u_s$, also of index $|k|$.
Consequently

$$
\boxed{
N_\Gamma E_V=(p,s-1)\mathcal O[S]u_s.
}
\tag{773.10}
$$

This retains the genuine ordinary norm obstruction:
the fact that all base units are field norms has not
been substituted for (773.10).

## 4. Two original units generate the entire Gamma norm kernel

Set $K_\Gamma^V=\ker(N_\Gamma:E_V\to E_{F_s})$ and
$R_\Gamma=\mathcal O[\Gamma]/(N_\Gamma)$.
As in R633, this kernel is a torsion-free module over the
DVR $R_\Gamma$, of rank p. Hence

$$
\dim_k K_\Gamma^V/\pi K_\Gamma^V=43.
\tag{773.11}
$$

The same actual primitive norm $N_\Gamma y=u$ proves

$$
c_q\notin\pi K_\Gamma^V.
\tag{773.12}
$$

For if $c_q=\pi h$, then
$y-a\gamma^{22}h$ would be Gamma fixed and have norm $u$,
forcing $u\in pE_{F_s}$. Since u is primitive and S fixed,
this is impossible.

Define the two actual ordinary kernel elements

$$
k_s=\pi c_s,\qquad
k_v=u_s-y+(aD+D_SH_{11}^{-1})c_s.
\tag{773.13}
$$

The second has Gamma norm zero because

$$
N_\Gamma k_v
=pu_s-u-D_S(s-1)u_s
=pu_s-u-(p-N_S)u_s=0.
$$

We will use the integral inclusions

$$
DE_V\subset\pi K_\Gamma^V,\qquad
(p-N_\Gamma)E_V\subset\pi^2K_\Gamma^V.
\tag{773.14}
$$

To prove them, use the representatives of degree at most 42.
Each of $B=D$ and $B=D_\Gamma-21N_\Gamma$ has augmentation
zero and may be divided integrally by $\pi=\gamma-1$:
write $B=\pi C$. The two derivative augmentations are,
respectively,

$$
\operatorname{aug}C
=-\frac{p(p^2-1)}{24}=p(-77)
\quad\text{and}\quad
\operatorname{aug}C
=\frac{p(p^2-1)}{12}=p(154).
$$

For D, its degree-at-most-42 coefficients are r for
$0\le r\le21$ and $r-43$ for $22\le r\le42$;
summing r times these coefficients gives the first value.
The second follows by summing $r(r-21)$.
Only divisibility by p is needed.
In either case write $\operatorname{aug}C=p\kappa$.
For $e\in E_V$, the element
$Ce-\kappa N_\Gamma e$ is in $K_\Gamma^V$,
and applying $\pi$ gives $Be$.
Since
$p-N_\Gamma=\pi D_\Gamma
=\pi(D_\Gamma-21N_\Gamma)$ as an operator,
both inclusions follow. No p-division of an ordinary
unit was assumed.

Now (773.1) gives the exact identity

$$
\pi k_v
=-a\gamma^{22}c_q+
 a\gamma^{22}(p-N_\Gamma)c_s+
 D_SH_{11}^{-1}k_s.
$$

Reduce modulo $\pi K_\Gamma^V$ and use (773.14):

$$
D_SH_{11}^{-1}\bar k_s=a\bar c_q.
\tag{773.15}
$$

Write $z=s-1$ in characteristic p.
One has $N_S=z^{42}$ and
$D_S=-z^{41}-z^{42}$.
Also $N_Sk_s=\pi c_q$, so $z^{42}\bar k_s=0$.
The constant term of $H_{11}^{-1}$ is $1/11$.
Equation (773.15) therefore says

$$
\boxed{z^{41}\bar k_s=-11a\bar c_q\ne0,\qquad
       z^{42}\bar k_s=0.}
\tag{773.16}
$$

Thus $\bar k_s$ generates a 42-dimensional submodule.

For any element of $K_\Gamma^V$, the full p-primary residue
belongs to $\mu_p$, because its Gamma norm is zero.
Its direct phase gives a linear map on
$K_\Gamma^V/\pi K_\Gamma^V$.
This map is zero on $k_s$ and all its S translates,
since Gamma is inertia.
On the second element in (773.13) it has the actual value

$$
r_\mu(k_v)=r_\mu(u_s)-r_\mu(y)=-9b\ne0.
\tag{773.17}
$$

The balanced derivative contributes zero by its augmentation.
The other derivative contributes zero because $c_s$ has
residue in $\mu_p$ and
$\operatorname{aug}(D_SH_{11}^{-1})=p\cdot21/11$.
This also follows from the exact phase (773.6).

Equations (773.11), (773.16), and (773.17) give 43
independent dimensions. Nakayama over $R_\Gamma$
therefore proves the full integral assertion

$$
\boxed{
K_\Gamma^V=R_\Gamma[S]k_s+R_\Gamma[S]k_v.
}
\tag{773.18}
$$

Together with (773.10), whose two generators have the
actual ordinary preimages $u_s$ and $-H_{11}^{-1}c_s$,
this proves

$$
\boxed{E_V=\mathcal O[\Gamma,S]c_s+
              \mathcal O[S]u_s+\mathcal O[\Gamma]y.}
\tag{773.19}
$$

It is the entire ordinary lattice, not a finite-index
Robert subgroup. In particular, since all three generators
have residue in $\mu_p$, so does every ordinary unit of V.
The full direct phase thus descends to a linear map
$E_V/pE_V\to k$, with generator values

$$
\boxed{r_\mu(c_s)=9,\quad r_\mu(u_s)=25,\quad
       r_\mu(y)=25+9b.}
\tag{773.20}
$$

## 5. An explicit torsion-free integral presentation

The result has a presentation using only the same actual units.
Take the free $\mathcal O$ module on

$$
\mathcal O[S]\{c,\gamma c,\ldots,\gamma^{41}c\}
\ \oplus\ \mathcal O[S]U_s\ \oplus\ \mathcal OY
$$

and impose the one relation

$$
\boxed{pY-N_SU_s-aDN_Sc=0.}
\tag{773.21}
$$

The symbols map to $c_s,u_s,y$.
S acts regularly on the first two summands and fixes Y.
Gamma fixes $U_s$, shifts the first 41 powers of c,
and its remaining actions are

$$
\gamma(\gamma^{41}c)
=(1-s^{11})U_s-\sum_{j=0}^{41}\gamma^jc,
\qquad
\gamma Y=Y+a\gamma^{22}N_Sc.
\tag{773.22}
$$

In (773.21), $DN_Sc$ is reduced by (773.22).
No $U_s$ term appears from this reduction, because
$N_S(1-s^{11})U_s=0$.
Hence the coefficient of the $s^0U_s$ basis vector in
(773.21) is exactly $-1$.
The relation is primitive, and the quotient is free
over $\mathcal O$ of rank $p^2$.

The formulas give $\gamma^p=1$, $s^p=1$, and commuting
actions. For Y, $\gamma^pY=Y$ uses $N_\Gamma N_Sc=0$.
Applying gamma to (773.21) changes it by zero using
$\pi D=p\gamma^{22}-N_\Gamma$.
Moreover
$\gamma^{-22}D\pi=p-N_\Gamma$ makes (773.21)
equivalent to $N_\Gamma Y=N_SU_s$.
Thus every relation in (773.1) is respected.

The actual map from this free quotient to $E_V$ is onto
by (773.19). Its source and target have the same rank
$p^2$, and its kernel is a torsion-free module of rank
zero. Therefore it is an isomorphism.
This proves completeness of both the generators and
the integral relation module.

## 6. The complete two-variable socle modulo p

In $E_V/pE_V$ put $x=\gamma-1$, $z=s-1$, and continue
to write $c$ for $c_s$. The actual coefficient is
$a=-4i$ modulo p. The integral presentation gives

$$
x^{42}c=-zH_{11}U_s,\qquad
z^{42}c=a^{-1}(1+x)^{-22}xY,\qquad
x^{42}Y=z^{42}U_s=u.
\tag{773.23}
$$

It follows that the cyclic c submodule has exactly the presentation

$$
\boxed{
k[x,z]c/
(x^{43},z^{43},x^{42}z^{42},
 a x^{41}z^{42}+(1/11)x^{42}z^{41})c.
}
\tag{773.24}
$$

For completeness, the displayed relations follow directly
from (773.23). They leave dimension $p^2-2$: the top corner
monomial is zero and the two next corner monomials satisfy
one relation; multiplying that relation by x or z produces
only the already zero top corner.
The whole unit module is generated by this cyclic submodule
and the two additional vectors $U_s,Y$, since every positive
z derivative of $U_s$ and every positive x derivative of Y
is in the c submodule by (773.23).
Its dimension is $p^2$ by §5. The surjection from the
displayed $p^2$-dimensional space is therefore an isomorphism.
This proves that (773.24) has no hidden relations.

Put

$$
e=x^{42}z^{41}c,\qquad
n=x^{40}z^{42}c+i x^{41}z^{41}c-x^{42}z^{40}c.
\tag{773.25}
$$

The corner relation is $x^{41}z^{42}c=-ie$, and
(773.23) gives

$$
u=-\frac1{11}e=-4e.
\tag{773.26}
$$

The complete subspace killed by both x and z is

$$
\boxed{(E_V/pE_V)^{\Gamma\times S}=ku\oplus kn.}
\tag{773.27}
$$

Here is a direct check of completeness. A nonzero coefficient
of $U_s$ in an invariant vector would give the uncancelled
term $x^{42}c$ under z, and a nonzero coefficient of Y would
give the uncancelled term $z^{42}c$ under x.
Thus every invariant is in the cyclic c submodule.
In its highest two degrees the only candidates are
$x^{40}z^{42}c,x^{41}z^{41}c,x^{42}z^{40}c,e$.
Their two boundaries impose the two independent equations
whose one-dimensional kernel in degree 82 is n.
The degree-83 vector e is killed by both.
Every lower monomial has a derivative outside these corner
relations, so no further invariant occurs.

This proof uses the actual integral presentation and
its dimension, not an abstract choice of a socle origin.

## 7. The original target has coefficient 4/C on the specified vector n

Let $U_V$ be the genuine ordinary restriction of $d_q$
provided by R768 when $b\ne0$.
Both x and z kill it. Therefore (773.27) gives a unique
$\eta\in k$ and some coefficient of n.

The coefficient of n is fixed by the original ordinary
capitulation cocycle, including its sign.
Define the actual ordinary unit

$$
U_{\rm can}=D_Sv_s-11aD_\Gamma y,\qquad
v_s=u_s-y+aDc_s,\qquad
D_\Gamma=\sum_{j=0}^{42}j\gamma^j.
\tag{773.28}
$$

Modulo p it is invariant under both groups.
For the z derivative this follows from $N_Sv_s=0$.
For the x derivative use R695's exact
$\pi v_s=(s-1)V_s$ and
$N_SV_s=ap\cdot21\gamma^{22}c_q+11au$:
one obtains
$xD_Sv_s=-11au$ modulo p, whereas
$xD_\Gamma y=-u$.
Thus the two terms in (773.28) cancel.

The same formula gives its ordinary scalar Bockstein:

$$
\beta_S[U_{\rm can}]=[v_s].
\tag{773.29}
$$

Indeed $(s-1)U_{\rm can}=pv_s$ exactly.
In the presentation of §6, its leading corner terms are

$$
U_{\rm can}
=11a^2x^{40}z^{42}c+
 ax^{41}z^{41}c+
 (1/11)x^{42}z^{40}c
+\text{a multiple of }e.
$$

Since $a=-4i$, these first three terms are exactly $-4n$.
The multiple of e has zero scalar Bockstein because
e is a scalar multiple of the actual invariant unit u.
Consequently

$$
\beta_S(n)=-\frac14[v_s].
\tag{773.30}
$$

On the other hand, the original ideal defining $A_\alpha$
has class $e_L$ and is principalized in V when $b\ne0$.
For any actual principalization $Z_V$ of that full divisor,
$A_\alpha-pZ_V$ represents $U_V$. The same ordinary
multiplication-by-p connecting map as R768 gives

$$
\beta_S(U_V)=-e_L=-\frac1C[v_s].
\tag{773.31}
$$

The last equality is precisely R561's original
$[v_s]=Ce_L$, with $C=9ib\ne0$.
Equations (773.27), (773.30), and (773.31) therefore prove

$$
\boxed{U_V=\frac4C\,n+\eta u.}
\tag{773.32}
$$

This determines the coefficient on the specified n.
It leaves a genuine scalar $\eta$ unknown; it does not
choose an origin for the finite-flat quotient.

For the original flat target $A_\alpha-(8i/1848)u$,
the coefficient of n is the same and

$$
\eta_{\rm fl}=\eta-\frac{8i}{1848}=\eta+8i
\quad\text{in }k.
\tag{773.33}
$$

The original correction has not been replaced by a free
choice of a unit or by a new first-moment convention.

## 8. All ordinary solutions of the actual two-direction equations

The genuine dual restriction $e_V$ in R768 satisfies

$$
xe_V=-2iU_V,\qquad ze_V=2U_V.
\tag{773.34}
$$

There are explicit particular solutions for the two basis
targets in (773.27). Put

$$
\begin{aligned}
e_n={}&-2i x^{39}z^{42}c+2x^{40}z^{41}c
       +2i x^{41}z^{40}c-2x^{42}z^{39}c,\\
e_u={}&-8(x^{40}z^{42}c+x^{42}z^{40}c).
\end{aligned}
\tag{773.35}
$$

Multiplying by x and z and using the one corner relation
in (773.24) verifies

$$
xe_n=-2in,\quad ze_n=2n,\qquad
xe_u=-2iu,\quad ze_u=2u.
\tag{773.36}
$$

For example $x(x^{40}z^{42}c)=-ie$,
$z(x^{42}z^{40}c)=e$, and $u=-4e$;
these give both equalities for $e_u$ with their displayed signs.

By subtracting the particular solution and using the
complete kernel (773.27), every solution of (773.34)
has exactly the form

$$
\boxed{
e_V=\frac4C e_n+\eta e_u+A n+B u,\qquad A,B\in k.
}
\tag{773.37}
$$

This is a description of solutions in the ordinary module.
For true supported global primitives, the allowed differences
are only the actual rational classes
$\mathbf F_p d_0+\mathbf F_p d_q$ proved in R768.
Equation (773.37) does not enlarge that global ambiguity or
certify an arbitrary pair $A,B$ as a supported descent.

Every displayed monomial in $e_n,e_u,n$ has positive
Gamma and S augmentation degree. All units in $E_V$
have residue in $\mu_p$ by §4; both Gamma and S act
trivially on this residue. Hence these monomials have
zero full direct q phase. The actual u has zero q phase
as well. Since multiplication by p kills the phase of
every ordinary unit of V, the phase is independent of
the representative in $E_V/pE_V$.
Equation (773.37) therefore proves the stronger local fact

$$
\boxed{r_\mu(e_V)=0}
\tag{773.38}
$$

for the genuine ordinary restricted dual class.
It holds at every original Delta transport.
It is more precise than saying that its local Kummer class
vanishes in the larger $\mu_{p^2}$ residue group.

The representatives (773.35) have Gamma and S norms
zero integrally, since both augmentation operators occur
with positive powers. The same is true for n; u has norms
pu, hence zero modulo p. Thus (773.37) also retains the
two true norm constraints of R768.

## 9. Remaining cochain comparison

The result supplies the entire ordinary scalar lattice,
its exact q-phase map, a specified two-dimensional invariant
subspace, the original target coefficient $4/C$, and all
solutions of the true restricted derivative equations.

The remaining coefficient $\eta$ has not been evaluated.
Nor has the passage from an ordinary class over V to
the original rational supported cochain been replaced by
a choice of local p-th root. In particular (773.38) does
not say that the regularized class $\mathscr S_\alpha$
of R755 is zero. Its construction still involves the
original q quadratic cochain, the actual division cochains
for the two generators, and their global descent.

Thus $I_\alpha=-\langle w_q,\mathscr S_\alpha\rangle$,
$\sigma_2$, $J_\ell$, and $b$ remain unevaluated.
When $b=0$, the conditional scalar-lattice argument is not
asserted; the original obstruction is already
$\Omega_2=22\varepsilon\ne0$ in that case.
For $b=24$, all the proven scalar-lattice conclusions
apply, but no contradiction has yet been inferred.
