# R686: all pure-p levels and the exact ordinary character class growth law

**Status: proved.** Keep the original $p=43$, $q$, CM, Weil and positive
Artin markings. There is a single

$$d\in\mathbf Z_{\ge1}\cup\{\infty\}$$

such that in every full pure-p layer

$$
B_n=K(A_0[p^{n+1}])^{\mu_{42}},\qquad
M_n=LB_n,\qquad n\ge0,
$$

the ordinary character class module is

$$
\boxed{A_{M_n}\simeq R_\Gamma/\pi^{m_n},\qquad
m_n=\min\{d,42n+1\}.}
\tag{686.1}
$$

Here $B_0=F$, $M_0=L$, $m_0=1$, and $R_\Gamma,\pi$ are the same
actual ring and uniformizer as in R657/R684. For $d=\infty$, the
minimum in (686.1) is $42n+1$. The isomorphisms can be chosen
compatible with the ordinary class norms.

In particular, if the original $m=m_1$ of R657 is less than $43$,
then

$$
\boxed{m_n=m\quad\hbox{for every }n\ge1.}
\tag{686.2}
$$

Thus going to higher full pure-p layers cannot by itself repair a
failure of the original ordinary scalar unit-Norm condition. The
alternative $m=43$, the actual $d$, and the original $C,J,b,\mathcal L$
are not evaluated here. This result is not obtained by assuming that
the level-one unit lattice or residue derivative persists: both
facts are proved at every level below.

$$
\operatorname{Gal}(B_n/F)=S_n\times T_n\simeq

\tag{686.4}

For clarity, its full principal-unit image holds at every level:

\tag{686.5}

The first spans the rational principal units; the second spans

## 2. Why q splits in every anti-p layer

Here $q$ is exactly R171's $776$-bit prime
$q=-89460^{44}P(1/89460)$, with the same degree-$44$
polynomial $P$ and marked root $89460y_0=-1$; no new prime or
root is selected. The original exact input of R171/R198 satisfies

$$
q\equiv-1\pmod{172},\qquad v_p(q+1)=1,
\qquad -q\equiv1+32p\pmod{p^2}.
\tag{686.7}
$$

In particular $q\equiv3\pmod4$. On $A_0/\mathbf F_q$, the terms
in the quadratic-character count for $x$ and $-x$ cancel because
$(-1/q)=-1$ and $(-x)^3-(-x)=-(x^3-x)$. Thus $a_q=0$. The exact
Frobenius characteristic equation is consequently

$$
\operatorname{Fr}_q^2=[-q].
\tag{686.8}
$$

Since $q$ is inert in $K$, the positive arithmetic Frobenius of
$K_q$ acts on every prime-to-$q$ torsion group of $A_0$ by this
same endomorphism $[-q]$. This is an equality of endomorphisms,
not a congruence only on $p^2$ torsion.

The Gaussian number $-q$ is a rational principal unit at $p$.
Therefore in the full tower it has the form

$$
\operatorname{Fr}_{K_q}=s_\infty^{a_q^{\rm sc}},
\qquad
a_q^{\rm sc}=\frac{\log(-q)}{\log(1+p)}\in\mathbf Z_p^\times,
\qquad a_q^{\rm sc}\equiv32\pmod p,
\tag{686.9}
$$

and has zero $T_\infty$ component **exactly**. Write
$F_{t,n}=B_n^{S_n}$. Then $q$ splits completely in every
$F_{t,n}/F$, whereas its residue degree in $B_n/F$ is $p^n$.
The original $\tau$-fixed prime of $F_{t,1}$ extends uniquely to
a $\tau$-fixed prime in each $F_{t,n}$, because the odd cyclic
group $T_n$ is inverted by $\tau$. These primes are compatible
under restriction and keep the same Weil residue root.

This proves the higher splitting statement from the actual
endomorphism (686.8); a zero $T$ coefficient modulo $p^2$ alone
would not prove it.

### 3.1 Ordinary-unit status

At a finite prime different from $2,p,11$, the curve has good
reduction, and $P_2+R_n$ has exact prime-to-residue-characteristic
order $4p^{n+1}$. Both this point and its image under $[11]$ avoid
the identity after reduction. The divisor of $v$ is
$121[O]-A_0[11]$, so the value is a local unit there.
At the auxiliary prime $11$ the same assertion can be checked
without inverting $11$: the usual division-polynomial recurrence
on $y^2=x^3-x$ gives

$$\psi_{11,A_0}(x,y)\equiv-1\pmod{11},$$

while $64^{10}\equiv1\pmod{11}$. Thus the value is an $11$-unit
as well. This is a fixed small polynomial identity, independent
of $n$.

Consequently $U_n$ is at least a $\{2,p\}$-unit. The divisor modules
over these two primes have zero $\chi^-$ summand: at $p$ the full
$\Delta$ is inertial, and at $2$ the existing inertia has nontrivial
$\chi^-$ character and is unchanged by the principal pro-$p$
layers. Hence its projected divisor is zero. Flatness of
$\mathcal O$ and the integral $\Delta$ idempotent give

$$u_n\in E_{B_n}.
\tag{686.12}$$

This proves ordinary-unit membership of the completed character
element; it does not rely on a heuristic integrality observation
from the first layer.

### 3.2 Exact distribution, including the sign

R198, equation (198.4), is the exact odd-isogeny norm identity for
the function (686.10). Its normalization is fixed by
$\Delta^{10}/\psi_{11}$; there is no scalar or root-of-unity
factor. For the last principal layer, the conjugates of $R_n$
are exactly $R_n+A_0[p]$. Applying the identity to $[p]$ gives

$$
N_{T^{\rm full}_{n+1}/T^{\rm full}_n}v(P_2+R_n)
=v([p]P_2+R_{n-1})=v(-P_2+R_{n-1}).
\tag{686.13}
$$

The function is even. The rational Teichmüller norm contains
$-1$, so reindexing that product by $k\mapsto-k$ changes
$v(-P_2+[k]R_{n-1})$ to $v(P_2+[k]R_{n-1})$ exactly. Applying
the unchanged $P_+$ therefore proves

$$
\boxed{N_{B_n/B_{n-1}}u_n=u_{n-1},\qquad
N_{B_n/F}u_n=u.}
\tag{686.14}
$$

In particular no unspecified $p$-adic unit factor, sign, or
projection scale is inserted at higher levels.

Set $u_{t,n}=N_{S_n}u_n\in E_{F_{t,n}}$. The same commuting norms

$$
N_{T_n}u_{t,n}=u,\qquad
N_{F_{t,n}/F_{t,1}}u_{t,n}=u_{t,1}=u_t.
\tag{686.15}
$$

## 4. Complete ordinary unit lattices and pure-p class groups

We now prove, for each $n$, the integral statements

$$
\boxed{E_{B_n}=\mathcal O[S_n,T_n]u_n,\quad A_{B_n}=0,}
\tag{686.16}
$$
$$
\boxed{E_{F_{t,n}}=\mathcal O[T_n]u_{t,n},\quad A_{F_{t,n}}=0.}
\tag{686.17}
$$

There is no $p$-power root of unity in these fields, since
$p-1=42$ does not divide their absolute degrees $88p^{2n}$ or
$88p^n$. Thus the character unit lattices are torsion-free over
$\mathcal O$. Dirichlet's representation gives their respective
ranks $p^{2n}$ and $p^n$, and their full-group invariant lattices
are $E_F=\mathcal O u$.

The primitive $u$ is not divisible by $p$ in either upper unit
lattice. If $u=pw$, torsion-freeness makes $w$ invariant under
the whole group, contradicting its primitivity in $E_F$.

Modulo $p$, put $x=s-1$, $z=t-1$. The group ring is

$$
k[S_n,T_n]=k[x,z]/(x^{p^n},z^{p^n}).
\tag{686.18}
$$

Its socle is the one-dimensional line
$kx^{p^n-1}z^{p^n-1}=kN_{S_n}N_{T_n}$, and every nonzero ideal
meets that line. For example, take the last nonzero power of the
maximal ideal acting on a nonzero ideal; it is killed by both
$x,z$ and hence lies in the displayed socle.

The map from (686.18) to $E_{B_n}/pE_{B_n}$ sending $1$ to
$\bar u_n$ is nonzero on that socle by (686.14) and primitivity
of $u$. Its kernel is therefore zero. Source and target both
have dimension $p^{2n}$ over $k$, so it is an isomorphism.
The integral map $\mathcal O[S_n,T_n]\to E_{B_n}$ has unit
determinant and is an isomorphism. This proves its **entire
saturated lattice**, not just the rank of the Robert subgroup.
The identical one-variable socle argument using (686.15) proves
the unit assertion in (686.17).

For $F_{t,n}/F$, all invariant character divisors descend because
the only ramification is at $p$ and its character divisor module
is zero. Since the unit lattice just proved is $T_n$-free,
$H^2(T_n,E_{F_{t,n}})=0$. Hilbert 90 and the principal/divisor
exact sequences imply that $A_{F_{t,n}}^{T_n}$ is generated by
divisors from $F$, whose character class group is zero. A nonzero
finite $p$-group module has nonzero invariants, so
$A_{F_{t,n}}=0$. Apply the same argument to the cyclic extension
$B_n/F_{t,n}$ and its $S_n$-free unit lattice to obtain
$A_{B_n}=0$. This completes (686.16)--(686.17).

\tag{686.19}

The norm in (686.15) is a sum over the kernel of

$$
\boxed{A_n(T)\bmod(T^p-1)=A_1(T).}
\tag{686.20}
$$

This uses the same marked prime, unit, generator, and logarithm.
In characteristic $p$, evaluation at $T=1$ and the first

A_n(1)=0,\qquad A_n'(1)=-13i\ne0,
\qquad (A_n(T))=(T-1)\subset k[T_n].

The logarithm is $\ell$, not the direct root exponent

## 6. Every mixed q layer has a cyclic ordinary Gamma module

Consider $M_n/B_n$, whose group is the fixed cyclic $\Gamma$ of
order $p$. At $q$ it is totally ramified. There are $p^n$ primes
after taking the $\Delta$ character summand, indexed by $T_n$.
At a $q$ completion of $B_n$, the local $\Gamma$ norm character
on a unit is obtained by first taking its unramified $S_n$ norm
to the corresponding completion of $F_{t,n}$. Applied to $u_n$
this gives $u_{t,n}$ exactly. The action of $S_n$ on this local
order-$p$ obstruction is trivial. By (686.16), the image of all
ordinary units in the local obstruction module $k[T_n]$ is thus
the ideal $(A_n)=(T-1)$, of length $p^n-1$.

At $p$ and $2$ the $\Gamma$ extension is split; elsewhere it is
unramified and local units are norms. The cyclic Hasse Norm
theorem gives

$$
\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal O}
\frac{E_{B_n}}{E_{B_n}\cap N_\Gamma(M_n^\times\otimes\mathcal O)_{\chi^-}}
=p^n-1.
\tag{686.22}
$$

The full invariant divisor quotient is $k[T_n]$, of length $p^n$.
Since $A_{B_n}=0$, the ordinary ambiguity sequences of R619 give

$$
0\to H^1(\Gamma,E_{M_n})\to k[T_n]\to A_{M_n}^{\rm st}\to0,
$$
$$
0\to A_{M_n}^{\rm st}\to A_{M_n}^{\Gamma}\to
\frac{E_{B_n}\cap N_\Gamma(M_n^\times\otimes\mathcal O)_{\chi^-}}
     {N_\Gamma E_{M_n}}\to0.
\tag{686.23}
$$

The rational unit representation is $p^{2n}$ copies of the
regular $\Gamma$ representation, so its character Herbrand
quotient is one. Both ordinary unit-cohomology lengths in
(686.23) cancel, exactly as at level one, and (686.22) gives

$$
\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal O} A_{M_n}^{\Gamma}
=p^n-(p^n-1)=1.
\tag{686.24}
$$

The group-ring norm $N_\Gamma$ kills $A_{M_n}$, since the class
norm to $B_n$ is zero. Consequently this is a finite module over
the DVR $R_\Gamma$, and its $\pi$-socle has dimension one by
(686.24). Thus

$$A_{M_n}\simeq R_\Gamma/\pi^{m_n},\qquad m_n\ge1.
\tag{686.25}$$

No ordinary unit-Norm surjectivity is assumed in deriving this.

## 7. The marked scalar coefficient and a sharp bound at every level

The strict-p genus field $H/F$ of R561/R657 is disjoint from every
$M_n/F$: its nontrivial $\Delta$ type has no common quotient with
the $\Delta$-trivial group $\Gamma\times S_n\times T_n$.
The base change $HM_n/M_n$ is unramified, by the same tame
exponent-$p$ argument at $q$ and splitting at $p,2$ as in R657.
It gives a surjective quotient

$$\varphi_n:A_{M_n}\to k$$

which is killed by $\pi$. The actual marked inertia and positive
$K_q$ Frobenius coordinates are still $-i$ and $32$.

In the Hilbert class extension over $K$, lift $\Delta$ by
Schur--Zassenhaus. Its nontrivial character kernel has zero
$\Delta$ invariants. Averaging gives a unique $\Delta$-fixed
lift of each element of the abelian quotient
$\Gamma\times S_n\times T_n$; these lifts commute and have
their stated orders. Write the actual tame inertia and positive
Frobenius as

$$
x=(a,\gamma_0),\qquad
f=(c,s_0^{a_q^{\rm sc}}),\qquad
\varphi_n(a)=-i,\quad\varphi_n(c)=32.
\tag{686.26}
$$

The Hilbert extension is unramified above $M_n$, so $x$ has
order $p$. Since $q^2\equiv1\pmod p$, tame commutation gives

$$
(s^{a_q^{\rm sc}}-1)a=\pi c.
\tag{686.27}
$$

All the ordinary class actions are scalars over $R_\Gamma$.
If $m_n\ge2$, compare (686.27) modulo $\pi^2$ and use
$a_q^{\rm sc}\equiv32$: the coefficient is forced to be

$$s=1+i\pi\pmod{\pi^2}.
\tag{686.28}$$

The same semilinear conjugation used in R657 conjugates
coefficients, sends $\pi$ to $-\pi$ modulo $\pi^2$, and has
$\tau t\tau=t^{-1}$. Consequently $\bar b_n=b_n$, and we obtain

$$t=1+b_n\pi\pmod{\pi^2},\qquad b_n\in\mathbf F_p.
\tag{686.29}$$

We do not assign $b_n$ when $m_n=1$, where no first coefficient
is defined. Under norm maps the defined coefficients agree.

If $v=1+i\pi+O(\pi^2)$ is any scalar lift to $R_\Gamma$, then

$$
v^p-1\equiv(i^p-i)\pi^p=-2i\pi^{43}\pmod{\pi^{44}}.
\tag{686.30}
$$

Thereafter the linear term in the next $p$th power has uniquely
lowest valuation, increasing that valuation by $p-1=42$ each
time. Hence

$$v_\pi(v^{p^n}-1)=42n+1.
\tag{686.31}$$

Since the action of $s$ has order dividing $p^n$ on $A_{M_n}$,
(686.31) proves

$$\boxed{m_n\le42n+1.}
\tag{686.32}$$

For $m_n=1$ this inequality holds directly. No value of a
higher coefficient of $s$ was needed.

## 8. Exact norm control between adjacent full levels

The relative group $M_{n+1}/M_n$ is generated by
$s^{p^n}$ and $t^{p^n}$ and has order $p^2$. Split it into two
cyclic steps. Each step is genuinely ramified at $p$ and
unramified elsewhere, with zero character divisor module at
the ramified primes. Its local multiplicative norm cokernel at
$p$ is $\Delta$-trivial. Thus the complete descent calculation
of R684, Section 2, applies to each step:

- invariant full character divisors descend;
- every base ordinary character unit is a field norm;
- the ordinary capitulation kernel and unit-Norm cokernel
  have equal lengths by the Herbrand quotient;
- the class norm is onto, since a ramified prime-degree step
  is disjoint from the base Hilbert class field;
- equality of lengths then makes the norm from coinvariants
  an isomorphism.

Applying the two actual norm maps therefore proves

$$
\boxed{
A_{M_{n+1}}/
(s^{p^n}-1,t^{p^n}-1)A_{M_{n+1}}
\xrightarrow{\sim}A_{M_n}.
}
\tag{686.33}
$$

This includes all ordinary unit terms; it is not an unproved
Iwasawa control assertion.

For $n\ge1$, the scalar action of $s^{p^n}-1$ has exact
$\pi$ valuation $42n+1$, up to truncation by the class length,
by (686.31). For a lift $w=1+b\pi+O(\pi^2)$ of $t$ with
$b\in\mathbf F_p$, the terms at valuation $p$ cancel:

$$w^p-1\equiv(b^p-b)\pi^p=0\pmod{\pi^{p+1}}.$$

The subsequent $p$ powers increase the valuation by at least
$42$. Consequently

$$v_\pi(w^{p^n}-1)\ge42n+2\qquad(n\ge1).
\tag{686.34}$$

If the class length is one, compatible scalar lifts with these
coefficients may simply be chosen; the truncation of either
ideal is already zero. Thus (686.33), (686.31), and (686.34)
give the exact recurrence

$$
\boxed{m_n=\min\{m_{n+1},42n+1\}\qquad(n\ge1).}
\tag{686.35}
$$

For $n=0$ the same equality follows from $m_0=1$ and the
nonzero scalar first coefficient when relevant; it is also
immediate from the already known $A_L=k$.

## 9. A single parameter, and the permanent-stabilization alternative

The norms are onto, so $(m_n)$ is nondecreasing. If at some
level $m_n<42n+1$, then (686.35) forces $m_{n+1}=m_n$; repeating
the argument forces all subsequent values to equal it.

Set $d=\sup_n m_n$, allowing $\infty$. If the sequence is
unbounded, no strict inequality can ever occur, so
$m_n=42n+1$ at every level. If it is bounded, its final constant
value is $d$, and (686.35), read backwards, gives
$m_n=\min(d,42n+1)$ at each level. This proves (686.1) and
its consequence (686.2).

Because the norms are surjective and $R_\Gamma$-linear, choose
a generator at each level lifting the preceding one. These
choices identify the norms with the natural quotient maps.
Thus the actual ordinary inverse-limit module has the form

$$
\boxed{
\varprojlim_n A_{M_n}\simeq
\begin{cases}
R_\Gamma/\pi^d,&d<\infty,\\
R_\Gamma,&d=\infty.
\end{cases}}
\tag{686.36}
$$

This is a theorem about the original tower and norm maps;
the isomorphism does not provide a numerical value of $d$.
It assumes neither an Iwasawa main conjecture nor a
vanishing statement for finite Iwasawa submodules.

## 10. Scope and the remaining original arithmetic

The base $n=1$ class module of R657 is now the first
truncation $R_\Gamma/\pi^{\min(d,43)}$. If $m<43$, no later
full pure-p level increases this ordinary character class
length. In particular the missing scalar ordinary-unit Norm
cannot be forced by a mere assertion that sufficiently high
pure-p levels have larger class length. If $m=43$, the later
lengths still depend on $d$ and may stabilize at any permitted
later stage.

The actual original $d/m$, the marked scalar $C$, the
principalization first moment $J$, and $\mathcal L$ remain
unknown. Nothing here identifies the auxiliary $C_t=9$,
a pure-p residue, or the direct root exponent with those
original quantities.
