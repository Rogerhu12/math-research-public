# R863 — The original nonsplit cuspidal extension and its full first ramified phase

Date: 2026-11-16. Status: **proved**. This identifies the full reduction of the particular integral finite-type lattice of R434/R592, including its nonsplit extension, and computes the original CM-torus phase through degree $p-2$ in $1-\zeta_p$. It does not identify that cusp expansion with the original Drinfeld normal coefficient.

## 1. The exact lattice to be identified

Let $p\ge41$, $p\equiv2\pmod3$, let $k=\mathbf F_{p^2}$, and use a compatible unramified coefficient ring $\mathcal O$ containing the original character values. Put
$$
G=\operatorname{PGL}_2(\mathbf F_p),\qquad
\theta(u)=[u]^{-(p-1)},\qquad
\Lambda=\mathcal O^{\mathbf F_p}/\mathcal O\mathbf1,
\qquad \overline\Lambda=\Lambda/p\Lambda.
\tag{863.1}
$$
The basis classes $e_r$, $r\in\mathbf F_p$, satisfy $\sum_re_r=0$. The original Borel acts by affine permutations. The original Weyl action is exactly R592(592.6):
$$
w e_r=-\frac1p\sum_{y\in\mathbf F_{p^2}^{\times}}
\theta(y)e_{r\mathrm N(y)+\operatorname{Tr}(y)}.
\tag{863.2}
$$
R434(434.10–16) and R592(592.7–10) prove that this is integral and that
$$
\lambda:\overline\Lambda\longrightarrow k,
\qquad \lambda(e_r)=1,
\tag{863.3}
$$
is a $G$-invariant quotient. They also prove that the original nonsplit Cartan average is integral, has a one-dimensional invariant space, and maps each $e_r$ to a vector with $\lambda=1$. Those statements are used as established inputs.

Both $\theta$ and its inverse give the same Weyl sums: the substitution $y\mapsto y^p$ preserves norm and trace. No opposite integral lattice is substituted for (863.1).

## 2. An explicit polynomial extension

The action convention is important. On homogeneous polynomials of degree $p+1$ use the following **right** action:
$$
(F\mid g)(x,y)=\det(g)^{-1}F\bigl(g(x,y)^t\bigr).
\tag{863.4}
$$
Here $g(x,y)^t$ denotes the product of $g$ with the column $(x,y)^t$, used as the two arguments of $F$; it is not an additional transpose of $g$. The dual left action is $(g\ell)(F)=\ell(F\mid g)$. A scalar matrix acts trivially, since its factor is $a^{p-1}=1$.

Let
$$
W=k[x,y]_{p+1}\otimes\det^{-1},\qquad
W_3=\langle x^{p+1},\ x^py+xy^p,\ y^{p+1}\rangle,
\qquad U=W/W_3.
\tag{863.5}
$$
The notation for the twist in $W$ records the action already specified in (863.4); it is not a second determinant factor. The subspace $W_3$ is stable. Indeed the four-dimensional subspace
$$
W_4=\langle x^{p+1},x^py,xy^p,y^{p+1}\rangle
\tag{863.6}
$$
is the finite-group module $V^{(p)}\otimes V\otimes\det^{-1}$, with $V^{(p)}=V$ on $G(\mathbf F_p)$. Since $p\ne2$, it decomposes into its symmetric square $W_3$ and the line generated by
$$
D=x^py-xy^p.
\tag{863.7}
$$
The latter is trivial under (863.4).

The quotient $W/W_4$ is the simple module
$$
S=\operatorname{Sym}^{p-3}V\otimes\det.
\tag{863.8}
$$
One can check this directly, without a composition-factor table. For $2\le i\le p-1$, send the class of $x^iy^{p+1-i}$ to
$i(i-1)X^{i-2}Y^{p-1-i}$ with the determinant twist in (863.8). Translation in $x$ uses
$$
\binom{i}{j}j(j-1)=i(i-1)\binom{i-2}{j-2}.
\tag{863.9}
$$
For translation in $y$ the same identity holds modulo $p$, since
$(p+1-i)(p-i)\equiv i(i-1)$. Diagonal matrices give matching powers. These elements generate the group, proving the displayed isomorphism. Simplicity of $\operatorname{Sym}^{p-3}$ follows, for example, by taking the truncated logarithms of the two unipotent root groups: their raising and lowering operators and their commutator connect all monomials, and the commutator has distinct weights modulo $p$.

Thus, for left modules on dualizing,
$$
0\longrightarrow S\longrightarrow U^*\longrightarrow k\longrightarrow0,
\qquad \ell\longmapsto\ell(D).
\tag{863.10}
$$
Here $S$ is self-dual as a finite $G$ module: its dual determinant power differs by $p-1$.

For
$$
F(x,y)=\sum_{i=0}^{p+1}f_i x^iy^{p+1-i},
\tag{863.11}
$$
define the following actual functionals on $U$:
$$
\boxed{\mathcal E_r(F)
=-\frac12\left(\frac{d}{dx}F(x,1)\bigg|_{x=r}
-r f_{p+1}-f_p\right),\qquad r\in\mathbf F_p.}
\tag{863.12}
$$
They annihilate $W_3$, satisfy $\mathcal E_r(D)=1$, and have the sole relation $\sum_r\mathcal E_r=0$. For the last assertions, their explicit polynomial dependence on $r$ is
$$
\mathcal E_r(F)
=-\frac12\left(f_1-f_p+
\sum_{i=2}^{p-1}i f_i r^{i-1}\right).
\tag{863.13}
$$
Evaluation of polynomials of degree at most $p-2$ on $\mathbf F_p$ has rank $p-1$, and all their power sums are zero. This proves that
$$
\Phi:\overline\Lambda\xrightarrow{\sim}U^*,
\qquad e_r\longmapsto\mathcal E_r
\tag{863.14}
$$
is an isomorphism of vector spaces. Direct substitution in (863.12) gives
$$
n(t)\mathcal E_r=\mathcal E_{r+t},\qquad
\operatorname{diag}(a,1)\mathcal E_r=\mathcal E_{ar}.
\tag{863.15}
$$
Thus it is precisely the original Borel identification, with no rescaling of $r$. Also $w\mathcal E_0=\mathcal E_0$, as in the original model. These checks alone would not identify the whole representation; the next section proves the full Weyl compatibility.

## 3. Why this is the original full representation

First compute the original cuspidal character on every $p$-regular class. On a noncentral split semisimple element, the affine permutation model has one fixed point, and quotienting the constant line gives trace zero. On a nonsplit semisimple class with eigenvalues $u,u^p$, put $t=\operatorname{Tr}(u)$ and $N=\operatorname N(u)$. A representative has fractional transformation $r\mapsto t-N/r$, and factors as translation, dilation and the Weyl element.

Use (863.2) on the full $p$-dimensional function space as a lift. This lift kills the constant vector because $\sum_y\theta(y)=0$, so its trace is the trace on the quotient. The diagonal condition is
$$
r(1-N\operatorname N(y))=t+N\operatorname{Tr}(y).
\tag{863.16}
$$
If the coefficient of $r$ is nonzero there is one solution; if both sides are zero there are $p$ solutions. The sum of $\theta$ over the norm circle $\operatorname N(y)=1/N$ is zero, since its restriction to the norm-one group is nontrivial. The only remaining two terms are $y=-1/u,-1/u^p$. Hence the exact characteristic-zero trace is
$$
-\theta(u)-\theta(u)^{-1}.
\tag{863.17}
$$
At the identity the trace is $p-1$. This derivation uses the original Weyl formula, including its factor $-1/p$.

These are also the Brauer character values of $U^*$. For a nonsplit element write $q=u^{p-1}$. On $W$ the eigenvalue sum is $\sum_{i=0}^{p+1}q^i=1$; on $W_3$ it is $q^{-1}+1+q$. Their difference is $-q-q^{-1}$, exactly (863.17). For a split semisimple element the two sums agree, giving zero, and the dimensions agree. All these equalities are equalities of Teichmüller-lifted character values, not merely traces reduced modulo $p$. Consequently the semisimplification of $\overline\Lambda$ is $k\oplus S$. Its existing quotient (863.3) has simple kernel $S$.

Both this extension and (863.10) are nonsplit. In fact the original Borel permutation quotient has no nonzero fixed vector: a translation-invariant class has a representative $c_0+c_1r$, and invariance under every dilation forces $c_1=0$. A split extension would contain a trivial submodule.

There is at most one nonzero extension class up to scalar. Here is a short proof in this precise group. Restriction from $G$ to its Borel is injective on $H^1(G,S)$, because $[G:B]=p+1$ is a unit; equivalently a Borel-equivariant splitting can be averaged over $G/B$ to obtain a $G$ splitting. The diagonal subgroup of the Borel has prime-to-$p$ order, so restriction bounds $H^1(B,S)$ by $H^1(C_p,S)$. On $S$, a generator of the unipotent $C_p$ acts as a single Jordan block of size $p-2$. Its norm is zero and
$$
\dim_k H^1(C_p,S)
=\dim_k S/(u-1)S=1.
\tag{863.18}
$$
The two nonsplit extensions are therefore isomorphic as $G$ modules.

Finally, the original Borel permutation quotient has only scalar Borel endomorphisms. Its unipotent generator is one Jordan block of length $p-1$, so its commutant is the truncated polynomial ring in $L=\log(u)$, of degrees $0,\ldots,p-2$. This logarithm is legitimate because all the required denominators are smaller than $p$. Dilation by $a\in\mathbf F_p^\times$ sends $L$ to $aL$; the only invariant polynomial in this degree range is constant. Thus any $G$ isomorphism between the two nonsplit extensions is, in the Borel identification (863.14), a scalar multiple of that identification. It follows that (863.14) itself intertwines the **full** group, including its original Weyl operator:
$$
\boxed{\Phi\text{ is the exact }G\text{-isomorphism, and }
\lambda(e_r)=\mathcal E_r(D)=1.}
\tag{863.19}
$$
This step is why neither a dimension count nor the Borel checks alone were used to assert the match.

## 4. Every low moment of the original CM Cartan average

Use the actual torus of R727 in the period basis $(\zeta,1)$, with
$$
\zeta=(-1+s)/2,\quad s^2=-3,\quad s=\zeta-\zeta^2,
\qquad
T_\zeta=\left\{
\begin{pmatrix}a&-b\\b&a+b\end{pmatrix}
\right\}/\mathbf F_p^\times.
\tag{863.20}
$$
Its quadratic form is $x^2+xy+y^2$. This torus is nonsplit since $p\equiv2\pmod3$. Put
$$
A=x-\zeta y,\qquad B=x-\zeta^2y.
\tag{863.21}
$$
The right $T_\zeta$ action on $A^{p+1-i}B^i\otimes\det^{-1}$ has character $q^i$, where $q$ ranges over $\mu_{p+1}$. Its average therefore retains just the $A^{p+1}$ and $B^{p+1}$ coefficients. Moreover
$$
D=\frac{B^{p+1}-A^{p+1}}s,
\qquad A^{p+1}+B^{p+1}\in W_3.
\tag{863.22}
$$
If those retained coefficients of $F$ are $c_A,c_B$, its averaged class in $U$ is $(s/2)(c_B-c_A)D$.

Let
$$
v_T=\frac1{p+1}\sum_{t\in T_\zeta}t e_0\in\Lambda,
\qquad \bar v_T=\sum_{r\in\mathbf F_p}\bar c_r e_r.
\tag{863.23}
$$
The coefficients $\bar c_r$ are defined up to a common constant. For $0\le k\le p-2$ the moment
$$
\mu_k=\sum_{r\in\mathbf F_p}\bar c_r r^k
\tag{863.24}
$$
is independent of that ambiguity, because the corresponding power sum is zero. In particular $\mu_0=\lambda(\bar v_T)=1$.

For $F_i=x^iy^{p+1-i}$, the inverse substitution is
$$
x=(\zeta B-\zeta^2A)/s,\qquad y=(B-A)/s.
\tag{863.25}
$$
Its two extreme coefficients are $c_A=\zeta^{2i}/s^{p+1}$ and $c_B=\zeta^i/s^{p+1}$. Since $s^p=-s$, (863.22) gives
$$
\bar v_T(F_i)
=-\frac{\zeta^i-\zeta^{2i}}{2s}.
\tag{863.26}
$$
For $2\le i\le p-1$, (863.12) gives $\bar v_T(F_i)=-i\mu_{i-1}/2$; the case $i=1$ is the already fixed normalization. Hence the complete formula is
$$
\boxed{\mu_k=
\frac{\zeta^{k+1}-\zeta^{2k+2}}{(k+1)s},
\qquad 0\le k\le p-2.}
\tag{863.27}
$$
No denominator in this range is divisible by $p$. The numerator divided by $s$ is respectively $1,-1,0$ as $k+1\equiv1,2,0\pmod3$, so all these moments actually lie in $\mathbf F_p$.

The torus average of every $e_r$ has the same reduction, as already follows from R434. Formula (863.27) concerns this entire original lattice vector, including the extension below its trivial quotient. It is not obtained by discarding the radical of R808's Gram form.

## 5. The entire first ramified cusp phase

Set
$$
\pi=1-\zeta_p,\qquad
\ell=\log(1-\pi)=-\sum_{j=1}^{p-2}\frac{\pi^j}{j}
\quad\text{in }\mathcal O[\zeta_p]/(\pi^{p-1}).
\tag{863.28}
$$
In this quotient $p=0$, and it is identified with $k[\pi]/(\pi^{p-1})$. Thus reducing the integral coefficients of $v_T$ modulo $p$ is sufficient for this exact precision.

Define a polynomial of degree at most $p-2$ by
$$
\mathcal C(T)=\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}
\frac{\zeta^j-\zeta^{2j}}{s\,j!}T^{j-1}.
\tag{863.29}
$$
Equivalently it is
$\{E_{p-1}(\zeta T)-E_{p-1}(\zeta^2T)\}/(sT)$, where
$E_{p-1}(T)=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}T^j/j!$ and the division is performed as a polynomial identity **before** evaluating a nilpotent argument. This convention retains the final $T^{p-2}$ coefficient; no division by $\pi$ or by an operator is taken in the quotient ring.

For every integer $n$, the original finite phase satisfies
$$
\boxed{\sum_r c_r\zeta_p^{-nr}
\equiv\mathcal C(-\bar n\ell)
\pmod{\pi^{p-1}}.}
\tag{863.30}
$$
Indeed expand $(1-\pi)^{-nr}=\exp(-nr\ell)$ through degree $p-2$ and apply (863.27). All factorials used are units. A different lift of $r$ changes the exponent by a multiple of $p$ and has no effect at this precision. Changing the representative $(c_r)$ adds a constant multiple of $\sum_r\zeta_p^{-nr}$; for $p\nmid n$ this is exactly zero, and for $p\mid n$ it is $p$, also zero in the stated quotient.

Let $f(Q)=\sum_{n\ge1}a_nQ^n$ be the original split-cusp vector in R592(592.11), with its specified phase convention
$$
e_r\longmapsto f_r(Q)=\sum_n a_n\zeta_p^{-nr}Q^n,
\qquad a_{pn}=0.
\tag{863.31}
$$
Then its **original** torus average has the full coefficientwise congruence
$$
\boxed{f_{v_T}(Q)
\equiv\mathcal C(-\ell\Theta_Q)\,\bar f(Q)
\pmod{\pi^{p-1}},\qquad
\Theta_Q=Q\frac d{dQ}.}
\tag{863.32}
$$
The right side is the polynomial operator (863.29), so it is defined even on indices divisible by $p$. There is no extra division by $p$ or $p+1$: the normalization is the average in (863.23), precisely as in R434/R592. The unnormalized trace is obtained by multiplying by the original integer $p+1$.

Equation (863.32) is stated in the fixed split-cusp frame (863.31). The tensor product with the actual tame representation is allowed, since the finite factors commute. When a tame degeneracy changes the displayed cusp variable or the affine label, its actual phase convention must be transported as well; the formula does not silently replace every such convention by a single $\Theta$ on an unrelated variable. In particular the torus and $e_0$ have not been conjugated independently.

## 6. The source comparison still required

This gives the whole first ramified phase, not only its constant term $\lambda=1$. It includes every coefficient through $\pi^{p-2}$, with the original integral cuspidal extension and CM torus fixed.

It is still a cusp statement. To obtain a Drinfeld normal coefficient one must transport this actual section, with its tame phase and Hodge normalization, through the original bad-$p$ model and its blow-up. The terms killed on the ordinary Igusa fiber cannot simply be erased on that blow-up. R859/R861 identify the target Néron lattice and its normal coordinates, but do not supply this missing calculation of the pullback by $q$. Accordingly no first-normal unit, first-block same-$h$ product, or height nonzero conclusion is asserted here.
