# R729 — Uniform nonvanishing and effective valuation bounds for the original full Abel coefficient

Date: 2026-11-14. **Status: proved.** For every sufficiently large prime
$p\equiv2,5\pmod9$, the original characteristic-zero coefficient $M_2$
is nonzero. Its valuation is an integer and has an effective bound
linear in the degree of the original real coefficient field.
This is not a claim that $C_p$, $C_{p-3}$, or $M_2$ is a unit.

The new nonvanishing uses the original weight-five and weight-seven
characters of R717, with their full tame factors and periods. It does
not replace them by the fixed-conductor values that define $C_k$.

## 1. Objects and statements

Let

$$
p\ge41,\quad p\equiv2,5\pmod9,\quad
n=p+1=3M,\quad
F=\mathbf Q(\zeta_n)^+,\quad g=[F:\mathbf Q]=\varphi(n)/2.
\tag{729.1}
$$

Keep the original $E_0:y^2=x^3-1$, differential, six-torsion marking,
$\theta=\theta_{-1}$, and selected $p$-adic embedding of R588/R717.
Thus $\theta(a)=\operatorname{Teich}(a/\bar a)$,
$\theta(\zeta)=\zeta^{-1}$, and $\theta(\sqrt{-3})=-1$.
For $h\equiv1\pmod3$ and $h$ odd, define

$$
\xi_{5,h}((a))=a^5\chi_6(a)\theta(a)^{-h},\qquad
\xi_{7,h}((a))=a^7\chi_6(a)\theta(a)^h.
\tag{729.2}
$$

These are the same characters as R717 for primitive $h$.
Write

$$
\mathcal H=\{h\bmod n:h\equiv1\pmod3,\ h\ {\rm odd}\},\qquad
\mathcal H^*=\{h\in\mathcal H:(h,n)=1\}.
$$

Let $A_5^{\rm full},A_7^{\rm full}$ be the averages of
$L(\xi_{5,h},3),L(\xi_{7,h},4)$ over $\mathcal H$, respectively,
and let $A_5^{\rm prim},A_7^{\rm prim}$ be their averages over
$\mathcal H^*$. All averages are divided by the cardinality of
their indicated set.

**Theorem 729.1.** Uniformly through all primes in (729.1),

$$
\boxed{
 A_5^{\rm full},\ A_7^{\rm full},\
 A_5^{\rm prim},\ A_7^{\rm prim}
 \longrightarrow \frac{\pi}{\sqrt3}.
}
\tag{729.3}
$$

In particular, for every sufficiently large such prime,

$$
\boxed{
S_2(\theta)S_3(\theta^{-1})\ne0,\qquad M_2\ne0.
}
\tag{729.4}
$$

There is no condition on the prime factorization of $p+1$.

**Theorem 729.2.** For any prime as in (729.1) for which $M_2\ne0$,
put $h_p=v_p(M_2)$. Then $h_p\in\mathbf Z$, $h_p\ge-1$, and

$$
\boxed{
5+2h_p<
 g\left(9+\frac{115\log2}{\log p}\right).
}
\tag{729.5}
$$

Consequently $h_p\le16g-3$ for all such $p\ge41$.
For $p\ge2^{115}$ the sharper conclusion is

$$
\boxed{h_p\le5g-3.}
\tag{729.6}
$$

Combining Theorem 729.1 with R726 shows that the original common
coarse Abel order in R682 is $m=1$ for all sufficiently large
primes in (729.1). Section 7 also gives an original Abel isometry
on an explicit disc of depth at most $5g+2$, without a $C_p$-unit
hypothesis. This depth grows with $p$ and does not contain the
whole required candidate disc of depth four.

## 2. The original AFE also covers the full odd family

Set

$$
c=\frac{\pi}{3\sqrt3},\qquad t=c/p,\qquad
K_r(x)=e^{-x}\sum_{j=0}^r\frac{x^j}{j!}.
$$

For $h\in\mathcal H$, the unit conditions on $-1$ and $\zeta$ in
(729.2) hold exactly. The character $\theta^h$ is nontrivial,
since its restriction to $\mu_3$ is the fixed nontrivial character.
Its conductor at $p$ is therefore $(p)$, even when $h$ is not
primitive of order $n$. The conductor of $\chi_6$ remains $(6)$.

R717's Gauss-sum proof needs only that this $p$-character is
nontrivial and trivial on rational scalars. It gives the same
$\tau_p=p$, $\tau_6=-6$, CRT factor $-1$, and
$\tau_{6p}=6p$ for every $h\in\mathcal H$. Its Poisson calculation
therefore gives root number $+1$ and the complete identities

$$
\begin{aligned}
L(\xi_{5,h},3)
 &=2\sum_{(\mathfrak a,6p)=1}
       \frac{\xi_{5,h}(\mathfrak a)}{N\mathfrak a^3}
       K_2(tN\mathfrak a),\\
L(\xi_{7,h},4)
 &=2\sum_{(\mathfrak a,6p)=1}
       \frac{\xi_{7,h}(\mathfrak a)}{N\mathfrak a^4}
       K_3(tN\mathfrak a).
\end{aligned}
\tag{729.7}
$$

These sums are absolutely convergent. Conjugating ideals conjugates
their coefficients and leaves the sums invariant, so the central
values and all four averages in (729.3) are real.

To retain the exact fixed cubic phase, choose the unique
$a_0\in\{M,2M\}$ with $a_0\equiv1\pmod3$. It exists because
$(M,3)=1$, and it is even. Every $h\in\mathcal H$ has the unique
form

$$
h=a_0+3k\pmod n,\qquad k\bmod M\ {\rm odd}.
\tag{729.8}
$$

Moreover $(h,n)=1$ if and only if $(k,M)=1$.
On ideals generated by primary elements, put

$$
\nu_k=(\theta^3)^k,\qquad
\lambda_5((a))=a^5\chi_6(a)\theta(a)^{-a_0},\qquad
\lambda_7((a))=a^7\chi_6(a)\theta(a)^{a_0}.
$$

Then, with no change of original character,

$$
\xi_{5,h}=\lambda_5\nu_k^{-1},\qquad
\xi_{7,h}=\lambda_7\nu_k.
\tag{729.9}
$$

The factors $\theta^{\pm a_0}$ have not been deleted.
They are exactly the fixed cubic phases in this parametrization.
Also $\chi_6(\zeta)=1$; the exponents $5+a_0$ and $7-a_0$
are divisible by three. Together with
$\chi_6(-1)=-1$ this verifies that each $\lambda_\ell$ is
well defined on ideals.

## 3. The full signed scalar kernel

Use the same visible primary set $\mathcal V$ as R714/R726.
Each primary generator of an ideal prime to $6p$ decomposes
uniquely as

$$
a=\chi_{-3}(q)\,q\beta,\qquad
q\ge1,\quad(q,6p)=1,\quad \beta\in\mathcal V.
\tag{729.10}
$$

The rational scalar $\chi_{-3}(q)q$ is $1\bmod3$.
For an odd such scalar, its $\chi_6$ value is one, and its
$\theta$ value is one. Since both infinity types in (729.2)
are odd, the contribution of that scalar to the normalized
central summand is exactly $\chi_{-3}(q)/q$.

For $r=2,3$, define

$$
F_r(u)=\sum_{q\ge1}\frac{\chi_{-3}(q)}q K_r(uq^2),\qquad
F_{r,2p}(u)=
 \sum_{\substack{q\ge1\\(q,2p)=1}}
       \frac{\chi_{-3}(q)}q K_r(uq^2).
\tag{729.11}
$$

The terms with $3\mid q$ vanish. The function $K_r$ is positive,
decreasing, bounded by one, and tends to zero at infinity.
The nonzero signs of $\chi_{-3}$ alternate, and the absolute
summands in $F_r$ decrease. Therefore

$$
0\le F_r(u)\le K_r(u).
$$

Because $\chi_{-3}(2)=\chi_{-3}(p)=-1$, exact inclusion-exclusion
gives

$$
F_{r,2p}(u)
 =F_r(u)+\tfrac12F_r(4u)
       +p^{-1}F_r(p^2u)+(2p)^{-1}F_r(4p^2u).
$$

Consequently

$$
\boxed{
0\le F_{r,2p}(u)\le S K_r(u),\qquad
S=\tfrac32(1+1/p)<2.
}
\tag{729.12}
$$

This is a bound after summing all signed integer multiples.
There is no absolute-value replacement by a divergent or larger
unsigned harmonic sum.

The tails of $F_r(u)$ beyond $q\ge Q$ are bounded in absolute value
by $2/Q$, uniformly in $u>0$, by partial summation of the bounded
partial sums of $\chi_{-3}$. Taking a fixed finite prefix and then
letting $Q$ increase proves

$$
F_r(u)\longrightarrow L(1,\chi_{-3})
 =\int_0^1\frac{dx}{1+x+x^2}
 =\frac{\pi}{3\sqrt3}=c
 \qquad(u\downarrow0).
\tag{729.13}
$$

The two terms with coefficients $1/p$ in (729.12) are bounded
by $3/(2p)$ in total. Thus, for $t=c/p$,

$$
2F_{r,2p}(t)\longrightarrow3c=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt3}.
\tag{729.14}
$$

## 4. Full means: every nonunit direction is exponentially remote

For $\beta\in\mathcal V$, let

$$
o(\beta)=\operatorname{ord}\bigl((\bar\beta/\beta)^3\bmod p\bigr).
$$

The odd-character kernel of the cyclic group of order $M$ is
$1$ at order one, $-1$ at order two, and zero at every other
order. Inversion of $\nu_k$ does not change this kernel.

The primary unit $\beta=1$ has order one and contributes
exactly $2F_{r,2p}(t)$ to the relevant full average.
Suppose that a nonunit visible primary $\beta$ contributes.
Then $o(\beta)=1$ or $2$, so
$(\bar\beta/\beta)^6=1\bmod p$.
All six roots of this equation are reductions of the actual
$\mu_6\subset K$. Hence, for some $\epsilon\in\mu_6$,

$$
\bar\beta-\epsilon\beta\in p\mathcal O_K.
$$

This difference is not zero. Otherwise the ideal of $\beta$
would equal its conjugate. A visible element cannot be divisible
by an inert rational prime or by both primes above a split
rational prime, while the ramified prime over three is excluded
by $(N\beta,6p)=1$. The ideal would then be trivial, making
$\beta$ a unit, contrary to assumption.

A nonzero element of $p\mathcal O_K$ has absolute value at least
$p$, and $|\bar\beta-\epsilon\beta|\le2|\beta|$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{|\beta|\ge p/2}
\tag{729.15}
$$

for every nonunit visible direction that occurs in a full average.

For $x\ge0$ one has the simple bound

$$
K_r(x)\le(2^{r+1}-1)e^{-x/2}.
\tag{729.16}
$$

Indeed $x^j/j!\le2^je^{x/2}$ for each $0\le j\le r$.
Use the global packing count $N(T)\le9T^2$ and
$f(T)=e^{-cT^2/(2p)}/T$. For $R_0=p/2$,
partial summation gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{|\beta|\ge R_0}f(|\beta|)
&\le9R_0e^{-cR_0^2/(2p)}
      +18\int_{R_0}^\infty e^{-cT^2/(2p)}\,dT\\
&\le9\left(R_0+\frac{2p}{cR_0}\right)
          e^{-cR_0^2/(2p)}
 =O\!\left(p e^{-cp/8}\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{729.17}
$$

The inequality includes possible points at the lower endpoint:
one may apply partial summation from just below $R_0$.
The Gaussian integral bound follows from $T/R_0\ge1$.
Multiplication by the fixed constants in (729.12) and (729.16)
therefore gives

$$
A_{2r+1}^{\rm full}
 =2F_{r,2p}(c/p)+O\!\left(p e^{-cp/8}\right)
 \longrightarrow\frac{\pi}{\sqrt3}
 \qquad(r=2,3).
\tag{729.18}
$$

No angular phase of $\lambda_5$ or $\lambda_7$ has been replaced
by its value at the unit; absolute values were used only to bound
the nonunit remainder.

## 5. Primitive means: the same uniform count applies

Let $R=\operatorname{rad}(M)$ and
$\mathcal D=\{o\mid R:o>2\}$.
The primitive character kernel equals $\mu(o)/\varphi(o)$ at
an element of order $o$. It agrees with the full odd kernel at
orders one and two. Its difference from that kernel is supported
exactly on $\mathcal D$.

The actual expressions (729.7)–(729.12), with
$|\lambda_{2r+1}(\beta)|/N\beta^{r+1}=|\beta|^{-1}$, yield

$$
\left|A_{2r+1}^{\rm prim}-A_{2r+1}^{\rm full}\right|
 \le2S
 \sum_{o\in\mathcal D}\frac1{\varphi(o)}
 \sum_{\substack{\beta\in\mathcal V\\o(\beta)=o}}
       \frac{K_r(c|\beta|^2/p)}{|\beta|}.
\tag{729.19}
$$

R726 proves that the same weighted Gaussian sum tends to zero
with exponent $-c|\beta|^2/p$.
Its proof holds, without any other change, for every fixed
positive constant $c'$ in place of $c$: the counting lemmas,
prime products, and small/intermediate-radius estimates are
independent of $c'$, and the full lattice-tail constant in
(726.7) is merely replaced by $36\sqrt{\pi/c'}$.
Its large-order and large-radius errors still tend to zero.
In particular

$$
\mathcal B_p(c/2):=
 \sum_{o\in\mathcal D}\frac1{\varphi(o)}
 \sum_{\substack{\beta\in\mathcal V\\o(\beta)=o}}
       \frac{e^{-c|\beta|^2/(2p)}}{|\beta|}
 \longrightarrow0.
\tag{729.20}
$$

Combining (729.16), (729.19), and (729.20) bounds the two errors by
$2S(2^{r+1}-1)\mathcal B_p(c/2)=o(1)$.
Together with (729.18), this proves all four limits in (729.3).
The proof is effective and uniform in the factorization of $p+1$.

## 6. Transfer to the same primitive and to the actual M2

It is not enough to find possibly different nonzero characters
in the two averages. Here the exact finite CM sums provide the
required algebraic transfer.

R717 proves, with the original completed-zeta values and full
translated kernels, that

$$
H_2/C_0,\ H_3/C_0\in\mathbf Q(x,y,E),
\qquad
C_0=72\sqrt3/\sqrt[3]4.
\tag{729.21}
$$

The completed values at the relevant torsion points are algebraic
by the same division-polynomial identity. For
$h\in\mathcal H^*$, extend
$\sigma_h(\zeta_n)=\zeta_n^h$ to the algebraic closure while fixing
$K$. Its action on $E_0[p]$ is multiplication by some
$c_h\in\mathbf F_{p^2}^\times$, since it commutes with the full
CM action.

Writing the original finite sums as in R722 (722.2), direct
reindexing of the torsion variable gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sigma_h\bigl(S_2(\theta)/C_0\bigr)
 &=\theta(c_h)^{-h}\,S_2(\theta^h)/C_0,\\
\sigma_h\bigl(S_3(\theta^{-1})/C_0\bigr)
 &=\theta(c_h)^h\,S_3(\theta^{-h})/C_0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{729.22}
$$

The rationality in (729.21) incorporates the change of the tame
torsion marking and of $C_0$. Thus no disjointness of the torsion
field and the cyclotomic field is assumed. The scalar factors
in (729.22) are nonzero.

The two exact original period identities are

$$
L(\xi_{5,h},3)=\frac{\Omega\,2!\mathcal A^2}{36p}S_2(\theta^h),
\qquad
L(\xi_{7,h},4)=\frac{\Omega\,3!\mathcal A^3}{36p}S_3(\theta^{-h}).
\tag{729.23}
$$

The positive limiting primitive averages give at least one
nonzero value in each family for all sufficiently large $p$.
Equations (729.22)–(729.23) then imply that the corresponding
finite sum is nonzero for every $h\in\mathcal H^*$, in each
family separately. In particular both are nonzero for the same
specified original $h=1$.

Finally the full original toric norm is

$$
M_2^2=-\frac{8\zeta^2N_\theta}{(p+1)^2}C_0^2\mathcal P_p,
\qquad
\mathcal P_p=\frac{S_2(\theta)S_3(\theta^{-1})}{C_0^2},
\qquad N_\theta\equiv-1\pmod p.
\tag{729.24}
$$

The outside scalar is nonzero, so (729.4) follows.
This algebraic transfer uses no direct Galois action on a
transcendental $L$ value and needs no weight-two rank assertion.

When combined with R726's weight-two nonvanishing, the same
original $A$ and $\beta$ satisfy the R616 hypothesis for all
sufficiently large $p$. The original coarse expansion has
linear term $M_2s/16$, multiplied in the actual quotient by
the fixed nonzero KS scalar. Thus its Lie-vector first order
is one. R682 then gives common order $m=1$ for all normalized
$t_i^2-t_j^2$. This does not prove that an exterior height
$E_{ij}$ has exact order two; only its known divisibility by
$s^2$ follows automatically.

## 7. A global norm gives an explicit valuation bound

The following bound holds at any prime in (729.1) where
$M_2\ne0$, independently of the asymptotic proof of that fact.
R717 gives

$$
\mathcal P_p\in F,\qquad
D_p\mathcal P_p\in\mathcal O_F,\qquad
D_p=2^{37}3^{26}p^5.
\tag{729.25}
$$

All its complex conjugates are the original products in (729.23),
with

$$
\sigma_h(\mathcal P_p)
 =\frac{\alpha^2p^2}{144\Omega^2\mathcal A^5}
       L(\xi_{5,h},3)L(\xi_{7,h},4),
\quad \alpha=\sqrt[3]4>0,\quad
\mathcal A=\frac{\sqrt3\Omega^2}{2\pi}.
\tag{729.26}
$$

There are at most $d(m)\le2\sqrt m$ ideals of norm $m$.
Using monotonicity of $K_r$ in (729.7) and
$\int_0^\infty K_r(u)\,du=r+1$ gives

$$
|L(\xi_{2r+1,h},r+1)|
 \le4\sum_{m\ge1}K_r(cm/p)
 \le\frac{4(r+1)p}{c}.
$$

Since $c>3/5$,

$$
|L(\xi_{5,h},3)|<20p,\qquad
|L(\xi_{7,h},4)|<27p.
\tag{729.27}
$$

These inequalities hold for every primitive embedding, not just
the one where nonvanishing was first found.

The original real period satisfies

$$
\Omega=\int_1^\infty\frac{dx}{\sqrt{x^3-1}}
 >\int_1^2\frac{dx}{\sqrt{7(x-1)}}=\frac2{\sqrt7},
\qquad \mathcal A>\frac1{14},\qquad \alpha^2<3.
$$

Here $2\pi<8$ and $\sqrt3>1$ suffice for the area bound.
Consequently

$$
\frac{\alpha^2}{144\Omega^2\mathcal A^5}
 <\frac{14^5}{16}<2^{16}.
$$

Combining this with (729.27) yields

$$
|\sigma_h(\mathcal P_p)|<2^{26}p^4,\qquad
|\sigma_h(D_p\mathcal P_p)|<2^{115}p^9.
\tag{729.28}
$$

The second inequality uses $3^{26}<2^{52}$.
Since $D_p\mathcal P_p$ is a nonzero algebraic integer,

$$
0<\left|N_{F/\mathbf Q}(D_p\mathcal P_p)\right|
 <(2^{115}p^9)^g.
\tag{729.29}
$$

The prime $p$ is unramified and splits completely in $F$, because
$p\equiv-1\pmod n$. Every local valuation of
$D_p\mathcal P_p$ above $p$ is nonnegative. The selected valuation
is $5+2h_p$ by (729.24), whose outside factors are $p$-units.
Thus

$$
5+2h_p
 \le v_p\left(N_{F/\mathbf Q}(D_p\mathcal P_p)\right)
 <g\left(9+\frac{115\log2}{\log p}\right).
$$

This proves (729.5). R710/R711 give
$pM_2/(6b_*)\in\mathbf Z_p$, with $v_p(b_*)=0$, and the rational
coefficient line; hence $h_p\in\mathbf Z$ and $h_p\ge-1$.
For $p>2^5$, the right side of (729.5) is less than $32g$,
so integrality gives $h_p\le16g-3$.
For $p\ge2^{115}$ it is at most $10g$, with the strict inequality
retained from (729.29), giving (729.6).

This argument gives an upper bound for the actual full coefficient.
It is not a bound for a residue truncation or a consequence of
the nonvanishing of a complex midpoint.

## 8. A complete Abel isometry without a Cp-unit assumption

Retain R710/R711's original normalization

$$
V=z_{\rm f}^3/p^3,\qquad
p^{-2}\mathcal I(z_{\rm f})/b_*
 =\widetilde P(V)=\sum_{j\ge1}a_jV^j
 \in\mathbf Z_p\langle V\rangle,\qquad
a_1=\frac{pM_2}{6b_*},
\tag{729.30}
$$
$$
V=\Psi(s/p^3),\qquad
\Psi(v)=\frac38v+\sum_{j\ge2}\psi_jv^j,\qquad
\psi_j\in p\mathbf Z_p,\quad
\Psi\in\mathbf Z_p\langle v\rangle.
\tag{729.31}
$$

These are full infinite-series statements. Assume only
$M_2\ne0$ and write $h=h_p\ge-1$.
For $v_p(V)=t>h+1$, the linear term has valuation $h+1+t$,
whereas every term of degree $j\ge2$ has valuation at least
$jt>h+1+t$. Hence the linear term strictly dominates the
whole convergent tail. The complete change (729.31) preserves
$t$ and has relative nonlinear error of valuation at least
$1+t$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{
v_p(\mathcal I(s))=h+v_p(s),\qquad
v_p\left(\frac{\mathcal I(s)}{M_2s/16}-1\right)
 \ge v_p(s)-h-4>0
\quad\text{if }v_p(s)>h+4.
}
\tag{729.32}
$$

A closed-disc form, with an exact restricted inverse, is

$$
\boxed{
\frac{\mathcal I(p^{h+5}u)}{p^{2h+5}b_*}
 =\frac{p^{-h}M_2}{16b_*}u+pH_h(u),\qquad
H_h\in u^2\mathbf Z_p\langle u\rangle.
}
\tag{729.33}
$$

To check all higher terms, set $e=h+2\ge1$ and
$R_e(u)=\Psi(p^eu)/p^e$.
In (729.33), the $j=1$ term is
$(a_1/p^{h+1})R_e$.
For every $j\ge2$, its extra $p$ power is

$$
ej-2h-3=(j-2)(h+2)+1\ge1.
$$

The nonlinear coefficients of $R_e$ are also divisible by $p$.
Thus (729.33) follows in the complete Tate algebra. Its linear
coefficient is a unit. The contraction proof in R721 §5 supplies
an integral restricted inverse and gives

$$
v_p(\mathcal I(s_1)-\mathcal I(s_2))
 =h+v_p(s_1-s_2)
\qquad(s_1,s_2\in p^{h+5}\mathcal O)
\tag{729.34}
$$

over every complete extension. The actual quotient component
$c_\sigma\mathcal I$ adds $v_p(c_\sigma)$ to this valuation;
the original KS constant has not been set to one or called a unit.

For all sufficiently large primes, (729.4) and (729.6) hold
simultaneously. The fixed-for-that-prime disc

$$
\boxed{v_p(a-1)\ge5g+2}
\tag{729.35}
$$

is contained in the disc of (729.33), so it has only the central
scalar Abel zero and satisfies the corresponding scaled isometry.
No assumption on $C_p$ is used. When R721's stronger unit
hypotheses are available, its much shallower depth-four conclusion
remains preferable.

## 9. Scope and continued height work

The original characteristic-zero Abel order is now one for every
sufficiently large allowed prime. The corresponding high-weight
primitive means, original finite CM sums, and selected valuation
bound are all uniform without a restriction on $\omega(p+1)$.
There is no new numerical run in this proof.

The bound $h_p\le5g-3$ is much too large to place every original
candidate with $v_p(a-1)\ge4$ inside (729.35). It supplies no
all-prime unit result and no control on the actual canonical
height coefficients. In particular the original
$\Lambda_h$, $\ell_h$, second-kind periods, and their finite
common-zero problem remain present.

R726 discharges the original weight-two rank/logarithm premise
in the same sufficiently large range. R724's exact height
evaluation and R725's explicit CM elliptic model therefore have
unconditional original rank and first-order Abel inputs there.
The actual $q/\beta$ coordinates, height jets, and common-root
certificate still have to be constructed and proved. No
Serre-uniformity completion is asserted.
