## 1. Fixed actual normalization and result

Let \(p\ge41\) be prime with \(p\equiv5\pmod6\), and fix
\[
E_0:y^2=x^3-1,\qquad \omega=dx/(2y),\qquad D=d/dz.
\]
Set \(q=p^2\), \(e=q-1\), \(N=p(p-1)\), and \(\varepsilon=(3/p)\). Choose the embedding of \(\alpha^3=4\) for which \(\alpha\bmod p\) is the unique element of \(\mathbf F_p\) with cube four. This condition is essential: another cube-root embedding changes an intermediate coefficient by a third root of unity.

The actual c0 finite tame kernel from R586 is
\[
\begin{aligned}
C_0&=72\sqrt3/\alpha,\qquad H_0=C_0R_0,\\
R_0&=\frac{x(7x^6+4x^3+16)}{x^9-228x^6+48x^3-64}\\
&=u^4A(w),\qquad
A(w)=\frac{7+4w+16w^2}{1-228w+48w^2-64w^3},
\quad u=x^{-1/2},\ w=u^6.
\end{aligned}
\tag{589.1}
\]
This is not R595's c1 function \(R_-\), which starts in degree two. The tame character \(\chi_0\) is the specified one fixed by the actual S6 marking in R586; its precise normalization is retained by
\[
H_0(P)=\sum_Q\chi_0(Q)^{-1}E_1^*(P+Q)=C_0R_0(P).
\tag{589.2}
\]
No different character is substituted on the basis of a similar Fourier expansion.

Put
\[
B=(-1)^{(p-1)/2-(p-2)/3}
\binom{(p-1)/2}{(p-2)/3}\in\mathbf F_p^\times,
\qquad
r'_p=[z^{(p-1)^2}]R_0\pmod p.
\tag{589.3}
\]
Then the actual finite sums and the R588-normalized actual CM value satisfy
\[
\boxed{S_0S_1\equiv-1296\varepsilon B r'_p\pmod p,}
\tag{589.4}
\]
\[
\boxed{M_0^2\equiv-72\varepsilon B r'_p\pmod p.}
\tag{589.5}
\]
In particular \(v_p(M_0)\ge0\), and \(M_0\) is a unit if and only if \(r'_p\ne0\). If \(r'_p=0\), the safe implication is \(v_p(M_0)\ge\tfrac12\). We do not assume that \(M_0\) has already been placed in an unramified coefficient field, so we do not silently strengthen this to valuation at least one.

The theorem is a coefficient criterion. It does not prove \(r'_p\ne0\) for every prime. The later exact example \(p=269\equiv8\pmod9\) gives zero; it does not by itself refute a separate assertion restricted to R317's remaining classes \(2,5\pmod9\).

## 2. The full reduced CM multiplication law

Let \(R=W(\mathbf F_{p^2})\), enlarging the unramified coefficient field when necessary. The formal group of the good CM model carries the action of \(\mathcal O_{K_p}\), hence of its Teichmüller subgroup \(\mu_e\). Starting from an integral parameter with linear term one, average
\[
Y=\frac1e\sum_{\xi\in\mu_e}\xi^{-1}[\xi](u).
\tag{589.6}
\]
The average is integral because \(e\) is a unit; it still has linear term one and satisfies \([\xi]^*Y=\xi Y\). It is defined over the unramified CM base, not merely over a punctured generic disc.

On the reduction of \(E_0\), the \(p\)-power Frobenius \(\pi\) has trace zero. One may see the point count directly because \(x\mapsto x^3\) permutes \(\mathbf F_p\), so
\(\sum_x(\frac{x^3-1}{p})=0\) and \(\#E_0(\mathbf F_p)=p+1\). Thus \(\pi^2=[-p]\). The \(p^2\)-power Frobenius acts on the reduced parameter \(Y\) as \(Y\mapsto Y^q\), since its coefficients lie in \(\mathbf F_{p^2}\). Also \([-1]^*Y=-Y\). Therefore
\[
\boxed{[p](Y)\equiv-Y^q\pmod p}
\tag{589.7}
\]
as a complete formal series, not only in its first nonzero term.

Equivariance gives
\[
[p](Y)=Y\left(p+a_1Y^e+a_2Y^{2e}+\cdots\right),
\quad a_1\equiv-1\pmod p,\quad a_j\equiv0\pmod p\ (j\ge2).
\tag{589.8}
\]
For a nonzero \(p\)-torsion point \(P\), write \(\varpi=Y(P)\). The Newton polygon and (589.8) give
\[
v_p(\varpi)=1/e,\qquad
U=\varpi^e/p\in\mathcal O^\times,\qquad U\equiv1\pmod p.
\tag{589.9}
\]
Every nonzero torsion coordinate is \([a]\varpi\), with \([a]\in\mu_e\). These are the actual CM labels used in the finite sums. The congruence for \(U\) is modulo the ideal generated by \(p\), not merely modulo the maximal ideal.

The invariant differential is integral and has
\[
\omega=(1+O(Y^e))\,dY.
\tag{589.10}
\]
Its formal logarithm agrees with \(Y\) through degree less than \(q\). Those finite coefficients are integral: Cartier annihilates \(\omega\), so every coefficient at an exponent one less than a multiple of \(p\) is divisible by \(p\); below \(q\), this cancels the only possible power of \(p\) in integration. The later argument never asserts that the entire logarithm is integral.

## 3. Identifying the actual completed zeta value

Use the odd formal primitive
\[
E(Y)=Y^{-1}+\sum_{\substack{k\ge1\\ k\equiv5\ (6)}}e_kY^k,
\qquad DE=-x.
\tag{589.11}
\]
The support condition follows from the CM \(\mu_6\) action. Integration of the integral Laurent differential \(-x\omega\) gives
\[
v_p(e_k)\ge-v_p(k).
\tag{589.12}
\]
This estimate also proves convergence at a nonzero \(p\)-torsion point: \(k/e-v_p(k)\) tends to infinity.

The division-polynomial identity, in the same normalization, is
\[
D\log\psi_p=pE([p]Y)-p^2E(Y).
\tag{589.13}
\]
It follows from the usual sigma division identity, or by comparing derivatives and poles; the additive constant is zero by oddness. At a simple zero \(P\) of \(\psi_p\), use a local flat parameter \(h\) centered at \(P\). Then
\[
D\log\psi_p=h^{-1}
+\frac{D^2\psi_p(P)}{2D\psi_p(P)}+O(h).
\]
Because \([p](P+h)=[p](h)\), the first term on the right of (589.13) has principal term \(h^{-1}\) and no constant. Hence
\[
\boxed{
E(P)=-\frac{D^2\psi_p(P)}{2p^2D\psi_p(P)}.
}
\tag{589.14}
\]
This is the algebraic completed-zeta value used in the original finite Kronecker calculation. It is not an arbitrary formal antiderivative whose additive constant could change the answer.

## 4. Cartier controls the entire relevant truncation

The exact Cartier formula on the fixed good model is
\[
\mathcal C(x\omega)=B^{1/p}\omega.
\tag{589.15}
\]
Indeed
\(x\omega=x(x^3-1)^{(p-1)/2}dx/(2y^p)\);
the only possible contributing coefficient is that of \(x^{p-1}\), which is the unit (589.3). All factorial arguments in the binomial coefficient are less than \(p\).

Write \(x\omega=\sum a_jY^j\,dY\). Cartier and (589.10) imply that, for \(0<r<e\),
\[
a_{pr-1}\equiv0\pmod p\quad(r\ne1),\qquad
a_{p-1}\equiv B\pmod p.
\tag{589.16}
\]
Now an allowed primitive exponent \(k\) is \(5\pmod6\). If \(p^2\mid k\), the first such positive exponent is \(5q\). Thus below \(5q\) no coefficient in (589.11) can lose two powers of \(p\). When \(p\mid k<5q\), write \(k=pr\). Then \(r<5p<e\) for \(p\ge41\), so (589.16) applies. Consequently
\[
\boxed{pE\equiv-BY^p\pmod p}
\tag{589.17}
\]
through every positive exponent less than \(5q\). The polar term \(pY^{-1}\) is zero modulo \(p\). This is the exact range used below; no larger range is asserted.

## 5. The nonzero low coefficient in S0

The specified c0 tame identity from R586 includes
\[
\sum_Q\chi_0(Q)e_Q=\frac{24\sqrt3}{\alpha^2},
\qquad e_Q=E_1^*(Q).
\tag{589.18}
\]
It is a finite algebraic identity on the eighteen six-torsion points, established there in the same normalization as (589.2). For example their x-coordinates are the roots of \(\Delta=x^9-228x^6+48x^3-64\), and their completed values are
\(e_Q=y_Qx_Q^2(-19x_Q^6+4328x_Q^3-16)/5184\);
the identity can be checked by the corresponding nine-root rational trace. The direction \(\chi_0\), rather than \(\chi_0^{-1}\), in (589.18) matters.

R574 gives \(D^{p-2}x(Q)=e_Q^p\). Applying \(D^{p-1}\) to (589.2) at \(O\), and dividing by \((p-1)!\equiv-1\), gives
\[
[z^{p-1}]H_0
=\sum_Q\chi_0(Q)^{-1}e_Q^p
=\left(\sum_Q\chi_0(Q)e_Q\right)^p\pmod p.
\]
The last equality uses \(\chi_0^p=\chi_0^{-1}\). Since
\(\alpha^p=\alpha\) and \((\sqrt3)^p=\varepsilon\sqrt3\),
\[
\boxed{[z^{p-1}]R_0=\frac{\varepsilon}{3\alpha}\pmod p.}
\tag{589.19}
\]
This is a unit. The change between \(Y\) and the flat parameter does not affect degrees less than \(q\).

Fix \(\theta=\theta_{-1}\). In the R586 convention
\[
S_0=-\sum_a\theta(a)H_0(P_a).
\]
Exact character orthogonality selects exponents \(p-1+je\). The coefficients of \(H_0\) in integral \(Y\) are integral. Equations (589.9) and (589.19) therefore give
\[
S_0=-eC_0\frac{\varepsilon}{3\alpha}\varpi^{p-1}
+\text{an error of valuation at least }1+\frac1{p+1},
\tag{589.20}
\]
and in particular \(v_p(S_0)=1/(p+1)\).

## 6. The full type-three tail and the high coefficient

The actual first mixed kernel has the exact form
\[
H_1=F+E\,DH_0,\qquad F\in\mathcal O[[Y]],
\qquad DH_0\in Y^3\mathcal O[[Y]].
\tag{589.21}
\]
This follows from the full finite tame kernel and \(R_0\in Y^4\mathcal O[[Y]]\); it does not replace the actual kernel by an unrelated derivative.

Define \(S_1=-\sum_a\theta(a)^{-1}H_1(P_a)\). Orthogonality selects
\(n_j=N+je\), \(j\ge0\), with \(N=p(p-1)\). Put \(b_n=[Y^n]H_1\).
Using (589.17),
\[
p\,b_N\equiv-B[Y^{N-p}]DH_0
=-BC_0r'_p\pmod p,
\tag{589.22}
\]
because \(N-p+1=(p-1)^2\equiv1\pmod p\). This index is less than \(q\), so the flat coefficient in (589.3) is the same coefficient in \(Y\).

For \(j=1,\ldots,4\), a contributing primitive exponent is at most
\[
n_j-3\le N+4e-3=5q-p-7<5q.
\]
It therefore loses at most one power of \(p\). The coefficient \(b_{n_j}\) has valuation at least \(-1\), and its extra factor \(\varpi^{je}\) gives at least one more \(p\) than the first block.

For \(j\ge5\), (589.12) gives
\[
v_p(b_{n_j})\ge-\lfloor\log_p(n_j-3)\rfloor
\ge-2-\lfloor\log_p(j+1)\rfloor.
\]
Relative to the lower bound \(-1\) for the first coefficient, its contribution gains at least
\[
j-1-\lfloor\log_p(j+1)\rfloor\ge1.
\tag{589.23}
\]
This inequality is immediate for \(5\le j<p-1\), and remains strict after each multiplication of the index range by \(p\). It controls the entire infinite tail, not just the first few terms.

We conclude
\[
S_1=\frac{eBC_0}{p}r'_p\varpi^N
+\text{an error of valuation at least }1-\frac1{p+1},
\tag{589.24}
\]
with \(v_p(S_1)\ge-1/(p+1)\). This remains a lower-bound statement when \(r'_p=0\).

Multiplying (589.20) and (589.24), and using \(N+p-1=e\), \(\varpi^e/p\equiv1\), \(e^2\equiv1\), and
\[
\frac{C_0^2}{3\alpha}=\frac{15552}{3\alpha^3}=1296,
\]
proves (589.4), with every omitted product term of valuation at least one.

## 7. From the specified finite product to the actual CM value

The actual R588 toric norm, finite projection normalization, and anti-linear identification are
\[
M_0\overline{M_0}
=\frac{N_\theta}{18(p+1)^2}S_0S_1,\qquad
N_\theta\equiv-1\pmod p,\qquad
\overline{M_0}=-M_0.
\tag{589.25}
\]
These statements use the genuine R575 family and c0 marking, not the opposite cubic twist's central L-value. The finite projection normalization is the already established one; no new unidentified Manin scalar is introduced here. Equation (589.25) implies
\[
M_0^2=-\frac{N_\theta}{18(p+1)^2}S_0S_1.
\]
Together with (589.4), this is exactly (589.5). The valuation conclusions follow from the square, without assuming an unramified field for \(M_0\).

## 8. Exact finite coefficient extraction and historical data

The high coefficient has a direct finite prescription. Let
\[
n=(p-1)^2=4+6M,\qquad
Z(w)=\sum_{k\ge0}\frac{\binom{2k}{k}}{4^k(6k+1)}w^k.
\]
All \(Z\)-coefficients up to \(M<(q-1)/6\) are integral. Lagrange inversion and Frobenius give
\[
r'_p=\frac1n[w^M](4A+6wA')\,Z(w)^{-1}Z(w^p)^2\pmod p.
\tag{589.26}
\]
This can be evaluated by direct BigInt central binomial coefficients, exact cancellation of denominator powers of \(p\), one quotient equation \(QZ=4A+6wA'\), and the final sparse \(Z(w^p)^2\) convolution. It needs no new wild torsion orbit.
