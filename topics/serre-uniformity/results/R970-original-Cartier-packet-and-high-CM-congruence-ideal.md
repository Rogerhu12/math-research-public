# R970 — The original Cartier packets and the high CM congruence ideals

Date: 2026-11-20. Authoring agent: literature_frontier. Independently audited by root and handoff_audit.

**Status: proved criterion for both original rows.** The complete original CM packet is Cartier nilpotent. Its original supersingular pairing is nonzero exactly when the full classical congruence ideal of the specified high-weight level-nine CM form is a unit, equivalently when its precisely Manin-period-normalized adjoint value is a unit. This does not prove that these ideals are units for all original primes. No numerical mathematics is executed here.

The new assertion is a unit criterion for the **varying high-weight** CM form in each of the two original rows. It is not an equality of the trace with an adjoint special value, and does not import the fixed weight-four Scholl unit of R961.

## 1. Objects and theorem

Assume the original conditions
\[
p\ge41,\quad p\equiv2,5\pmod9,\qquad
(r,w)=
\begin{cases}
(2,p-1),&p\equiv11\pmod{12},\\
(8,p-7),&p\equiv5\pmod{12}.
\end{cases}
\tag{970.1}
\]
Thus \(w=p+1-r\), \(w\equiv4\pmod6\), and the unchanged original label is \(h=(p+1+r)/2\). No half-period shift is made.
Let
\[
f=F_w=f_{\Xi_w}\in S_w(\Gamma_0(9)),\qquad
\Xi_w((a))=a^{w-1}\epsilon_3(a)
\tag{970.2}
\]
be the original primitive CM newform of R913. It has trivial nebentypus, rational integral coefficients, and first coefficient one. Its original marked vector is exactly
\[
F=V_w^\dagger(Q)=f(Q)-2^w\zeta^2 f(Q^4),
\tag{970.3}
\]
with the original phase, determinant return, and full norm extension of R913/R932. Write \(M_{\rm CM}\) for R955's complete original raw prime-to-\(6p\) generalized CM packet, and \(N\) for its Cartier-nilpotent part. R955 already proves that \(N\) is one odd chain of length \(L\) and that the original complete trace is nonzero if and only if \(L=1\).

Let \(\mathcal O\) initially be an unramified coefficient ring containing the original sixth roots of unity, and let \(\mathbb T_9\) be the classical weight-\(w\) Hecke algebra on \(S_w(\Gamma_0(9),\mathcal O)\), generated by the normalized operators away from nine. Localize at the residual system of \(f\). Its rational characteristic-zero factor is selected by \(\lambda_f:\mathbb T_9\to\mathcal O\). Define its congruence ideal by
\[
\eta_f=\lambda_f(\operatorname{Ann}_{\mathbb T_9}\ker\lambda_f).
\tag{970.4}
\]
Equivalently, if \(e_f\) is the characteristic-zero idempotent of this factor, \(\eta_f\) is its denominator ideal. It is the **full classical Hecke congruence ideal**, with the integral Manin-period convention of Tilouine--Urban §3. No ordinary-at-\(p\) projector is used, and no arbitrarily normalized CM period is substituted for these periods.

The conclusions are
\[
\boxed{M_{\rm CM}=N,}
\tag{970.5}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{\quad
\text{original }S\ne0
\iff L=1
\iff \eta_f=\mathcal O
\iff
\frac{L(\operatorname{Ad}f,1)}{\pi^{w+1}\Omega_f^+\Omega_f^-}
\text{ is a }p\text{-adic unit}.
\quad}
\tag{970.6}
\]
The adjoint Euler factors and periods in (970.6) are precisely those in the cited theorem. In particular its left side does not imply that a bare Eisenstein value or an arbitrarily chosen CM-period-normalized value is a unit.

The proof below establishes all intermediate implications. The all-prime unit assertion itself remains unproved.

## 2. A Fourier-preserving integral realization at level 36

Use the already fixed \(\tau'\) and \(Q=e^{2\pi i\tau'/6}\) of R913/R932. Set \(\tau'=6\tau\). If
\[
\gamma'=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma(6),
\]
then conjugation by \(D_6=\operatorname{diag}(6,1)\) gives
\[
D_6^{-1}\gamma'D_6
=\begin{pmatrix}a&b/6\\6c&d\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{970.7}
\]
Its image is exactly
\[
\{\gamma\in\Gamma_0(36):a,d\equiv1\pmod6\}.
\tag{970.8}
\]
Every element of \(\Gamma_0(36)\) has \(a,d\equiv1\pmod6\) or \(a,d\equiv-1\pmod6\). Since the weight \(w\) is even, adjoining \(-I\) does not change modular forms. Thus
\[
H(\tau')\longmapsto H(6\tau)
\tag{970.9}
\]
identifies the full-six pairing component with the classical space \(S_w(\Gamma_0(36))\). Its Fourier variable is exactly \(q=e^{2\pi i\tau}=Q\). Equation (970.9), rather than a determinant-normalized slash operator, is the convention used here: it introduces no factor \(6^{w/2}\) in the Fourier coefficients.

This identification is integral at every prime \(p>3\). One way to see this is to use the degree-six isogeny represented by \(D_6\); its effect on a Hodge frame is a unit away from six, and its full level structures and cusp transports are tame. Equivalently, apply the Fourier expansion principle at all cusps: the conjugating matrices and the corresponding Hodge-frame factors have denominators supported at two and three. Hence (970.9) carries the full cusp lattice to the full cusp lattice over \(\mathcal O\), not merely to a finite-index sublattice of unspecified \(p\)-index.

Both spaces also have integral base change. On the full-six genus-one curve of R918, the cusp-form line has degree
\[
\deg(\omega^w(-\mathrm{cusps}))=6w-12>0.
\tag{970.10}
\]
Its \(H^1\) vanishes on both fibers. Thus its sections form a free lattice and reduce onto the full residual cusp space. The same statement follows at level 36 from (970.9).

The other Weil-pairing component is retained, with its genuine determinant transport. The finite tame group is
\[
G_6=\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbf F_2)\times
\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbf F_3),\qquad
H_6=\{(\vartheta(g),g):g\in\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbf F_3)\}.
\tag{970.11}
\]
Its order and the order of every projector used here are units at \(p\). On the full component union, the invariant projector is the actual average
\[
e_{H_6}=\frac1{|H_6|}\sum_{h\in H_6}h.
\tag{970.12}
\]
On a selected pairing component one may instead average the stabilizer; the determinant transport reconstructs the same full invariants. We use the full projector when commuting it with prime-to-six adelic Hecke correspondences. In particular a one-component matrix is not silently substituted for the action of an inert prime.

## 3. The precise p operator and nilpotence of the complete CM packet

### 3.1 Classical operator in the Q-rational model

In the level-36 realization (970.9), use the classical normalized good-prime operator
\[
(T_pH)(Q)=\sum_{n\ge1}
\bigl(a_{pn}+p^{w-1}a_{n/p}\bigr)Q^n,
\qquad a_{n/p}=0\text{ if }p\nmid n.
\tag{970.13}
\]
The character in this formula is trivial. Reduction gives the coefficient-extraction operator \(U_p^0\), with no scalar:
\[
\overline T_p=U_p^0,\qquad
U_p^0\Bigl(\sum a_nQ^n\Bigr)=\sum a_{pn}Q^n.
\tag{970.14}
\]
This is because \(w-1=p-r\ge1\) in both rows. It is not a convention declaring the original raw inert correspondence to be a one-mark endomorphism.

Let \(\sigma\) act coefficientwise on the reduction of the **Q-rational level-36 lattice**:
\[
\sigma\Bigl(\sum a_nQ^n\Bigr)=\sum a_n^pQ^n.
\tag{970.15}
\]
The coefficients of the rational Hecke matrices lie in \(\mathbf F_p\), so \(\sigma\) commutes with \(\overline T_p\) and with the classical residual Hecke algebra. This is the specified coefficient semilinearity in (970.9); no claim is made that \(\sigma\) alone fixes the original \(\zeta\)-marked graph subspace.

R952(952.2), (952.7), (952.12–13) then give, by equality of the actual original Fourier expansions,
\[
\boxed{\quad
\mathscr U=\sigma^{-1}\overline T_p,\qquad
\mathcal C\Phi_w(H)=\Phi_w(\mathscr U H).
\quad}
\tag{970.16}
\]
Here \(\sigma^{-1}\) is essential: \(s^p=-s\) and \(\zeta^p=\zeta^{-1}\). R952's root construction proves that the composite returns to the same original invariant space and changes the actual deck character. The normalizing \(1/6\) in \(\Phi_w\) is fixed by inverse Frobenius, so no factor six appears in (970.16). This directly uses the original fixed cusp and frame, instead of inferring an operator equality from equal dimensions or from an eigensystem.

Consequently, on the complete rational level-36 realization,
\[
\mathscr U^a=\sigma^{-a}\overline T_p^{\,a}.
\tag{970.17}
\]
Its restriction to the original subspace is valid because (970.16) proves that subspace is preserved by the composite. It does not require either \(U_p^0\) or \(\sigma\) separately to preserve a single marking.

### 3.2 The two distinct returns and the exact full-component operator comparison

The classical prime-to-\(6p\) residual system of (970.2) is fixed by coefficient Frobenius and by the quadratic character \(\chi_{-3}\): its eigenvalues are rational integers, and \(a_\ell(f)=0\) for inert \(\ell\). Thus
\[
\overline\lambda_f^{\,\sigma}=\overline\lambda_f,
\qquad
\overline\lambda_f\otimes\chi_{-3}=\overline\lambda_f.
\tag{970.18}
\]
Let \(\mathcal M\) be the full-six cusp lattice on the union of the two Weil-pairing components. All finite-group actions in this paragraph act on this union. Put
\[
d_3=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)\in\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbf F_3),
\quad \tau=\vartheta(d_3),\quad
d=(1,d_3),\quad h_0=(\tau,d_3)\in H_6,
\quad r_0=h_0d=(\tau,1).
\tag{970.18a}
\]
Here \(\tau\) is a transposition in \(\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbf F_2)\). The elements \(d,h_0,r_0\) are commuting involutions. The first two have determinant minus one and exchange the pairing components; \(r_0\) is the pure mod-two reflection and preserves each component. The element \(d\) is not an element of \(H_6\), and \(r_0\) must not be erased as though it were in that group.

The choice \(h_0\) is permitted in R575(575.14). Any other determinant-minus-one graph return \(h_1\in H_6\) differs from \(h_0\) by the determinant-one graph subgroup
\[
G=H_6\cap
\bigl(\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbf F_2)\times
\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbf F_3)\bigr).
\tag{970.18b}
\]
Consequently these graph returns give the same map on the original quotient. This verifies compatibility with the already fixed return without identifying \(d\) with that return. In particular the inversion of the original mod-three deck generator in R575 is retained.

For \(\ell\nmid6\), set \(\epsilon_\ell=0\) if \(\ell\equiv1\pmod6\), and \(\epsilon_\ell=1\) if \(\ell\equiv-1\pmod6\). Let \(A_\ell\) be the normalized prime-to-six adelic Hecke operator on \(\mathcal M\), before returning its pairing component. It commutes with every element of \(G_6\): its finite adelic double coset is supported at \(\ell\), disjoint from the factors at two and three. Its correspondence sends a pairing index to its \(\ell\)-th power. The comparison with the original Igusa operator below is used only for \(\ell\ne p\). Define
\[
C_\ell=d^{\epsilon_\ell}A_\ell.
\tag{970.18c}
\]
Thus \(C_\ell\) preserves each pairing component. Its normalization and its return can be checked directly, rather than inferred from an eigensystem. On \(\tau'\), the \(D_6\)-conjugates of the classical double-coset matrices are
\[
M_\infty=\begin{pmatrix}\ell&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
M_j=\begin{pmatrix}1&6j\\0&\ell\end{pmatrix}
\quad(0\le j<\ell).
\tag{970.18d}
\]
Modulo six they equal \(d_\ell=\operatorname{diag}(1,\ell)\) up to the central scalar \(\ell I\). Since \(\ell\equiv\pm1\pmod6\) and \(w\) is even, that central discrepancy acts trivially. The tame return is therefore exactly \(d^{\epsilon_\ell}\). With the determinant-normalized weight-\(w\) slash convention, multiplying the sum of these slash operators by \(\ell^{w/2-1}\) gives
\[
\begin{split}
C_\ell H(\tau')
=\ell^{w-1}H(\ell\tau')
 +\frac1\ell\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}
 H\bigl((\tau'+6j)/\ell\bigr),\\
C_\ell H(Q)
=\sum_{n\ge1}\bigl(a_{\ell n}
+\ell^{w-1}a_{n/\ell}\bigr)Q^n.
\end{split}
\tag{970.18e}
\]
This is the classical level-36 Hecke operator after (970.9), including at \(\ell=p\) before reduction. There is no factor six and no hidden determinant scalar. The argument is the general-prime version of R942(942.5–7).

On the **full union** the two idempotents \(e_d^\pm=(1\pm d)/2\) are integral and commute with every \(A_\ell\). Equation (970.18c) gives the operator identities
\[
C_\ell|_{e_d^+\mathcal M}=A_\ell,
\qquad
C_\ell|_{e_d^-\mathcal M}
=\chi_{-3}(\ell)A_\ell.
\tag{970.18f}
\]
Together with (970.18), this proves equality of the complete generalized residual CM blocks for the two Hecke systems on \(\mathcal M\). The assertion includes nonsemisimple residual blocks. For split primes the operators are equal. For inert primes the CM eigenvalue is zero, and replacing an operator by its negative leaves every kernel of every power unchanged. Taking these conditions for all prime-to-\(6p\) operators proves equality of the full primary subspaces. No diagonalizability modulo \(p\) is invoked.

To see the actual original single-component operator, restrict an \(H_6\)-invariant section to the selected component. This identifies \(\mathcal M^{H_6}\) with its \(G\)-invariant lattice there: the value on the other component is reconstructed uniquely by \(h_0\). In these coordinates the natural operator is
\[
S_\ell^{H}=h_0^{\epsilon_\ell}A_\ell
=r_0^{\epsilon_\ell}C_\ell.
\tag{970.18g}
\]
For an inert prime, \(C_\ell\) first carries the graph \(G\) to \(G'=dGd^{-1}\); the retained reflection \(r_0\) carries \(G'\) back to \(G\). This explains why a single classical \(T_5\) in R942 has a conjugate intermediate marking, while its square returns. The projectors \(e_d^\pm\) were used only on the full union, where they are defined; they were not applied to a single \(G\)-invariant space.

Finally the original high-weight norm/Igusa normalization is exactly
\[
T_\ell^{\mathrm{orig}}
=\ell^{h-1}S_\ell^H
=\ell^{h-1}r_0^{\epsilon_\ell}C_\ell,
\tag{970.18h}
\]
in the specified characteristic-\(p\) chart, by R913(913.36–40). The common exotic/Frobenius transport conjugates this diagram, and the full norm extension reconstructs its other Igusa part when present. Its \(\mathbf F_p^\times\) factor is undone when comparing to \(\lambda_f\); neither a different label nor a scalar-character-only extension is inserted. It follows from (970.18f) and the actual integral projector (970.12) that the original complete CM packet embeds, after component restriction, in the classical block \(M_{36,\mathfrak m}\). The latter block is \(\sigma\)-stable by (970.18). Formula (970.16), including its Cartier scalar, still follows independently from R952's actual expansion.

**Full-component comparison.** The required comparison is the pair of equalities (970.18c) and (970.18g–h), not an identification of the distinct returns \(d\) and \(h_0\). The full-component primary comparison follows from (970.18f); the original graph phase is preserved in (970.18g).

### 3.3 Every characteristic-zero lift in this block is nonordinary

The residual representation of (970.2) has at \(p\) two niveau-two inertia characters
\[
\omega_2^{p-r},\qquad \omega_2^{p(p-r)},
\tag{970.19}
\]
interchanged by Frobenius. They are distinct because \(p+1\nmid p-r\): this would require \(p+1\mid r+1\), whereas \(r+1\) is three or nine and \(p\ge41\). Thus its restriction to \(G_{\mathbf Q_p}\) is irreducible, and in particular the global residual representation is absolutely irreducible.

Each characteristic-zero primitive form \(g\) occurring in \(M_{36,\mathfrak m}\) has the same residual representation, by the prime-to-\(6p\) traces and determinants and absolute irreducibility. If \(a_p(g)\) were a unit, the ordinary local Galois theorem would make its local representation reducible and hence its residual local representation reducible. Intersecting the invariant line with a stable lattice gives a saturated rank-one sublattice, so this last implication does not require a choice of a special integral lattice. It contradicts (970.19). Therefore
\[
v_p(a_p(g))>0
\quad\text{for every such }g.
\tag{970.20}
\]

The good-prime \(T_p\) acts by \(a_p(g)\) on every old copy of \(g\). Its characteristic polynomial on the integral finite free block thus reduces to a power of \(X\): after a splitting extension every root has positive valuation. Cayley--Hamilton gives
\[
\overline T_p^{\,a}=0
\quad\text{on }M_{36,\mathfrak m}
\tag{970.21}
\]
for some finite \(a\). By (970.17), the actual Cartier operator is nilpotent on the original entire CM packet. This proves (970.5). This step does not assert that Cartier is zero, or that \(a=1\). It removes the potentially bijective complementary part that R955 deliberately left open.

No level-lowering assertion is needed for this nilpotence step. The local analysis at two and three is used only for the rank comparison below.

## 4. Why no additional level-two branch survives the original CM localization

Every primitive form appearing in \(S_w(\Gamma_0(36))\) has trivial nebentypus and conductor dividing 36.

At two the original residual representation is unramified and has trace zero and nonzero determinant. Thus the ratio of its two Frobenius eigenvalues is \(-1\).

An irreducible admissible representation of \(\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbf Q_2)\) with unramified central character and conductor exponent at most two has the following possibilities relevant here:

- An unramified principal series.
- An unramified twist of Steinberg, of conductor exponent one.
- A depth-zero supercuspidal representation of conductor exponent two.

There is no further principal-series or twisted-Steinberg case in this range: a ramified character of \(\mathbf Q_2^\times\) has conductor exponent at least two; an unramified product of two such characters gives principal-series conductor at least four, and a ramified Steinberg twist also has conductor at least four.

The Steinberg case has residual Frobenius eigenvalue ratio \(2\) or \(2^{-1}\). For it to be congruent to the original unramified trace-zero representation would require \(2=-1\pmod p\), contrary to \(p>3\). This is the explicit necessary level-raising condition here; no sufficiency theorem is invoked.

The conductor-two supercuspidal case is induced from a regular character of the residue field \(\mathbf F_4^\times\) of the unramified quadratic extension. Its inertia has a nontrivial character of order three. Since \(p>3\), this inertia cannot disappear under reduction, whereas the original residual representation at two is unramified. This case is also excluded. Hence every congruent primitive form is unramified at two.

At three, the local character in (970.2) has the nontrivial residue character \(\epsilon_3\) of conductor \((\sqrt{-3})\). The induced two-dimensional inertia characters have order four; equivalently the regular tame character on the unramified quadratic side has exponents two and six on \(\mathbf F_9^\times\). Reduction at \(p>3\) preserves these characters and their conductor exponent two. A characteristic-zero lift with conductor dividing 36 therefore has conductor exponent exactly two at three. We conclude that every congruent primitive form has **primitive level exactly nine**.

This also checks the two omitted good-prime eigenvalues when moving from an away-\(6p\) system to the Hecke algebra of (970.4): at two unramified local compatibility gives \(a_2(g)\equiv0=a_2(f)\), and (970.20) gives \(a_p(g)\equiv0=a_p(f)\). Thus the residual maximal ideal is the one for the complete away-nine algebra in the cited congruence theorem.

## 5. The actual graph-invariant multiplicity and the localized rank

At three the unique central-trivial cuspidal representation of \(\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbf F_3)\) is two dimensional. It is exactly
\[
\mathrm{std}\circ\vartheta,
\qquad \ker\vartheta=Q_8.
\tag{970.22}
\]
One can check uniqueness directly using the regular characters of \(\mathbf F_9^\times\): central triviality allows the single conjugate pair of exponents two and six. The resulting representation is the two-dimensional representation of \(\operatorname{PGL}_2(\mathbf F_3)\simeq S_4\) factoring through \(S_3\). Its cuspidal character has no unipotent invariants, as required. It is also the explicit original tame type already used in R913/R918.

For an unramified representation at two, its \(K(2)\)-invariants are
\[
\operatorname{Ind}_{B(\mathbf F_2)}^{\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbf F_2)}1
=1\oplus\mathrm{std}.
\tag{970.23}
\]
The trivial summand cannot pair with (970.22) under the graph subgroup. The standard summand pairs with it in exactly one invariant direction, because
\[
\dim\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbf F_3)}
(\mathrm{std}\circ\vartheta,\mathrm{std}\circ\vartheta)=1.
\tag{970.24}
\]
Every congruent primitive level-nine automorphic representation consequently contributes exactly one original \(H_6\)-invariant characteristic-zero direction. This is a statement about the actual graph, not about arbitrary vectors with the same Hecke eigenvalues. More explicitly, the \(G\)-invariants in \(\mathrm{std}\otimes(\mathrm{std}\circ\vartheta)\) are the two pairings of opposite \(A_3\) weights. The independent mod-two order-three deck generator acts on these directions by \(\zeta\) and \(\zeta^2\). The original return transports this same pair as in R913. Thus no extra deck-invariant direction is inserted into R955's two-character source.

For \(f\) itself, R913(913.31–33) fixes this direction explicitly as (970.3), including its oldform phase. The characteristic-zero multiplicity calculation does not rechoose that vector or normalize its source scalar.

Here is the full-component multiplicity count. Over a splitting field, index the holomorphic automorphic decomposition by normalized primitive eigenforms with their coefficient embeddings. Each resulting global cuspidal representation \(\pi\) occurs once in the complete full-six space and contributes \(\pi_f^{K(6)}\). For each representation with the local types just proved,
\[
\pi_f^{K(6)}=(1\oplus\mathrm{std})
\otimes(\mathrm{std}\circ\vartheta),
\qquad
\dim\pi_f^{K(6)}=6,
\qquad
\dim(\pi_f^{K(6)})^{H_6}=1.
\tag{970.24a}
\]
The involution \(d\) has two eigenspaces of dimension three on this six-dimensional space; they give the classical Hecke systems of \(\pi\) and \(\pi\otimes\chi_{-3}\), as in (970.18f). These are two component-descent descriptions, not two copies of its \(H_6\)-invariant line.

If \(\pi\) is the original CM representation, then \(\pi\otimes\chi_{-3}=\pi\). It occurs once in the full automorphic decomposition, and contributes one graph-invariant direction. If \(\pi\) is non-CM, then \(\pi\) and \(\pi\otimes\chi_{-3}\) are distinct global representations. Their local types at two and three are unchanged: the character is unramified at two, and it permutes the two regular characters of the depth-zero type at three. Both have primitive level nine; both lie in the same residual block by (970.18). Each contributes its own single graph-invariant direction, so this pair contributes two, exactly as the two distinct classical primitive eigenforms do. If the two forms are coefficient conjugates, those two embeddings are already counted once each; no further factor is inserted.

Restriction from \(\mathcal M^{H_6}\) to the selected component is injective and surjective onto the \(G\)-invariants, with inverse given by the \(h_0\) reconstruction above. Therefore a second factor of two cannot arise from the two Weil-pairing components. This explicitly handles both the self-twist and the non-CM paired cases.

To pass from characteristic-zero dimensions to the complete residual generalized packet, use the integral projector rather than only the eigenspace calculation. The full tame cusp lattice has base change by (970.10). The local Hecke block is an integral direct summand over the complete henselian coefficient ring, and the tame projector (970.12) commutes with the full-component Hecke action. Taking it is exact, since its denominator is a unit. Therefore its reduction has dimension equal to the rank of that projected integral block. The preceding multiplicity-one calculation gives the identity
\[
\boxed{\quad
\dim_k M_{\rm CM}
=\operatorname{rank}_{\mathcal O}
S_w(\Gamma_0(9),\mathcal O)_{\mathfrak m_f}.
\quad}
\tag{970.25}
\]
A splitting extension may be used to count individual eigenforms and their embeddings, but does not alter the rank identity. In particular no assertion that a chosen eigenline is saturated is substituted for the full free-lattice and unit-projector argument.

**Multiplicity conclusion.** The full-graph multiplicities in (970.24a), the self-twist and non-CM cases just distinguished, and the integral projector prove (970.25). A global representation is counted once, and component restriction is an isomorphism on the full invariants. The two geometric pairing components are not counted independently.

## 6. Congruence ideal and the precise adjoint unit criterion

Because \(f\) is a rational normalized primitive eigenform, its generic factor has dimension one. In the complete local Hecke algebra, the idempotent \(e_f\) is integral if and only if \(\eta_f\) is a unit. If it is integral, locality forces \(e_f=1\), so the local algebra and the localized cusp module have rank one. Conversely a rank-one localized cusp module has only the scalar Hecke algebra \(\mathcal O\), so its congruence ideal is a unit. Thus
\[
\eta_f=\mathcal O
\iff\operatorname{rank}_{\mathcal O}
S_w(\Gamma_0(9),\mathcal O)_{\mathfrak m_f}=1.
\tag{970.26}
\]

Combining (970.5), (970.25), (970.26), and the already proved R955 chain theorem gives the first three conditions of (970.6). A nonunit congruence ideal implies the existence of a different characteristic-zero eigenform in this same local block; otherwise the finite flat, generically semisimple local algebra would have rank one. Conversely any such eigenform makes the block rank greater than one.

Any different form in this block is non-CM. The only possible imaginary quadratic CM field at primitive level nine is \(\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-3})\). Its class number is one, and the conductor \((\sqrt{-3})\) and odd infinity type \(w-1\) uniquely fix the finite residue character \(\epsilon_3\); conjugating the infinity type gives the same theta series. Hence the original form (970.2) is the unique CM primitive form at this weight, level, and nebentypus.

For the final equivalence in (970.6), apply Tilouine--Urban, Theorem 3.1(b), at \(N=9\), \(k=w=p+1-r\). Its numerical assumptions hold in both rows: \(p\nmid6N\varphi(N)\) and \(p>w\). Its (Irr) means absolute irreducibility over \(\mathbf Q\), which follows from (970.19). With their specified integral Manin periods and adjoint Euler factors, the theorem gives
\[
\eta_f\sim_p
\frac{L(\operatorname{Ad}f,1)}{\pi^{w+1}\Omega_f^+\Omega_f^-}.
\tag{970.27}
\]
No ordinary hypothesis at \(p\) is used in this congruence-ideal theorem. The ordinary local theorem was used earlier solely by contradiction to exclude unit \(a_p(g)\).

## 7. Scope and the remaining all-prime input

The new substantive conclusion is that a longer original Cartier chain is equivalent to an actual non-CM congruence to the original high form at primitive level nine. In particular the nilpotent part is the whole CM packet, rather than only one possible Fitting summand of it.

This does not show that \(\eta_{F_{p-1}}\) or \(\eta_{F_{p-7}}\) is a unit for every corresponding original prime. Both the infinity type and the integral periods vary with \(p\). The fixed weight-four CM adjoint value, the fixed low Scholl divided-Frobenius unit of R961, and an unrestricted globally unobstructed deformation ring do not identify these varying ideals.

For \(r=2\) only, R965's already calibrated identity \(\kappa=-g_pS^p/432\), with \(g_p\) a unit, adds the equivalent condition \(\kappa\ne0\) to (970.6). No such formal-height coefficient or scalar identity is asserted here for the \(r=8\) row.
