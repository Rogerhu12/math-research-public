# R916 — The original finite-flat quotient gives the exact good-model Hodge content

Date: 2026-11-16. Status: **proved for the original quotient and finite-flat models of R908–R911**. The mixed-characteristic cotangent map of the actual quotient is injective before reduction to the residue field. This turns the former upper-half content bound into an equality and makes the corresponding weight-$k_+$ Igusa layer in R913 nonzero. It does not remove the original Tate index or calculate a first normal coefficient at $c_1$.

## 1. Statement with the original scale

Keep the original objects and notation
$$
p\ge41,\quad p\equiv2,5\pmod9,\quad
n=p+1,\quad e=p^2-1,\quad \varpi^e=p,
\quad
\mathcal H=\{1\le h\le p:h\equiv1\pmod6,\ (h,n)=1\}.
\tag{916.1}
$$
The good Raynaud abelian part of the original $J_{X_p}$ is $B/R$. The original primitive target gives complete height-two BT groups $\mathscr A_h$ and, as in R908,
$$
e_hq_B=[p^{a_h}]\circ f_h,\qquad a_h\ge0.
\tag{916.2}
$$
The actual integer $a_h$ is preserved.

Let $\omega_h$ be the original unit positive-CM generator of $\omega_{\mathscr A_h}$, and let $I_h$ be the ideal generated by the coordinates of $f_h^*\omega_h$ in the free $R$-module $\omega_B$. The ideal is independent of the choice of an $R$-basis. R911 fixes the Raynaud defect
$$
r_h=
\begin{cases}
0,&h<n/2,\\
2h-n,&h>n/2.
\end{cases}
\tag{916.3}
$$
The equality case is excluded by primitivity. We prove
$$
\boxed{I_h=\varpi^{p r_h}R.}
\tag{916.4}
$$
In particular, for the same original map and same target generator,
$$
\operatorname{content}_R\bigl((e_hq_B)^*\omega_h\bigr)
=\varpi^{e a_h+p r_h}R.
\tag{916.5}
$$
No normalization of $q$ by a power of $p$ is being made.

## 2. The actual kernel closure and its abelian quotient

Let $H_h$ be exactly the finite-flat closed subgroup of $B[p]$ constructed in R908: it is the schematic closure of the generic kernel of $f_h[p]$. Its quotient is the original finite-flat model
$$
\pi_h:B[p]\twoheadrightarrow
\mathscr D_h=B[p]/H_h,
\qquad
f_h[p]=\phi_h\circ\pi_h,
\tag{916.6}
$$
with $\phi_h:\mathscr D_h\to\mathscr A_h[p]$ generically an isomorphism.

The restriction $f_h|_{H_h}$ is zero as a morphism over $R$. It is zero on the generic fiber by construction, and the source $H_h$ is finite flat over the DVR, hence schematically has dense generic fiber. Equality of the two maps can therefore be checked on that generic fiber. This uses the existing kernel closure, not a new saturated kernel.

Form the fppf quotient
$$
C_h=B/H_h,\qquad \pi:B\longrightarrow C_h.
\tag{916.7}
$$
It is an abelian scheme, and $\pi$ is an isogeny. Here is a direct justification over the present, arbitrarily ramified DVR. The subgroup is finite flat and acts freely by translations. The quotient exists as a scheme by the dimension-one case of Moonen, *Quotients by group schemes*, Theorem 4.39. Proposition 4.31 makes $\pi$ a finite-flat $H_h$ torsor. The quotient commutes with base change by 4.30. Each geometric fiber is the quotient of an abelian variety by its finite subgroup scheme, hence is an abelian variety by 4.40. Properness and flatness descend along $\pi$; a flat morphism of finite presentation with smooth geometric fibers is smooth. Thus $C_h$ is a proper smooth group scheme with geometrically connected fibers. This proves the asserted abelian-scheme property without a small-ramification hypothesis.

Since $H_h\subset B[p]$, multiplication by $p$ factors uniquely:
$$
B\xrightarrow{\pi}C_h\xrightarrow{\psi}B,
\qquad
\psi\pi=[p]_B.
\tag{916.8}
$$
It also satisfies $\pi\psi=[p]_{C_h}$, as can be checked after the fppf cover $\pi$. The morphism $\psi$ is an isogeny. Its kernel is not merely generically isomorphic to the original model; fppf exactness identifies it over $R$:
$$
\boxed{\ker\psi=B[p]/H_h=\mathscr D_h.}
\tag{916.9}
$$
Indeed a section of $\ker\psi$ lifts fppf-locally to a section $b$ of $B$; its lift satisfies $[p]b=0$, and two lifts differ by $H_h$. Conversely the image under $\pi$ of every $p$-torsion section lies in $\ker\psi$. This proves equality as fppf sheaves, hence as the represented finite-flat group schemes.

Moreover $\ker\psi\subset C_h[p]$, by $\pi\psi=[p]$. Thus the whole diagram uses actual finite-flat subgroup schemes of abelian schemes. No quotient of $B$ by an infinite generic kernel and no assertion of Hodge saturation for $q_B$ has entered.

## 3. Factoring the same BT map

The isogeny $\pi$ gives an exact sequence of fppf $p$-divisible sheaves
$$
0\longrightarrow H_h\longrightarrow B[p^\infty]
\xrightarrow{\pi}C_h[p^\infty]\longrightarrow0.
\tag{916.10}
$$
For surjectivity, a $p^m$-torsion section of $C_h$ lifts locally to $b\in B$ with $p^mb\in H_h$; since $pH_h=0$, one has $p^{m+1}b=0$.

The original complete-BT morphism $f_h$ kills $H_h$. Consequently it factors uniquely as
$$
f_h=\widetilde f_h\circ\pi,\qquad
\widetilde f_h:C_h[p^\infty]\longrightarrow\mathscr A_h.
\tag{916.11}
$$
One can use the quotient of fppf sheaves in (916.10), and restrict to finite torsion levels to see that this is a morphism of BT groups. It is not necessary to algebraize $\widetilde f_h$ as a map of abelian schemes to a newly chosen elliptic factor.

On the subgroup (916.9), its restriction is exactly $\phi_h$:
$$
\widetilde f_h|_{\mathscr D_h}=\phi_h.
\tag{916.12}
$$
Both sides become $f_h[p]$ after the faithfully flat map $B[p]\to\mathscr D_h$ in (916.6), so the equality holds over $R$.

Write
$$
w_h=\widetilde f_h^*\omega_h\in\omega_{C_h}.
\tag{916.13}
$$
The invariant cotangent modules of the complete BT groups of the good abelian schemes identify with those of the abelian schemes. Thus
$$
f_h^*\omega_h=\pi^*w_h.
\tag{916.14}
$$
Every map in this construction inherits the original descent, deck action, and prime-to-$6p$ Hecke relations from (916.6). The source, positive CM order, $c_0$ rigidification, and $c_1$ centering have not changed.

## 4. The integral cotangent injection

For the isogeny $\psi:C_h\to B$ with kernel $\mathscr D_h$, the cotangent sequence at the identity gives
$$
\omega_B\xrightarrow{\psi^*}\omega_{C_h}
\longrightarrow\omega_{\mathscr D_h}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{916.15}
$$
The first map is injective: the two modules are free over $R$, and on the characteristic-zero generic fiber an isogeny is étale, so the induced cotangent map is an isomorphism. The same argument makes
$\pi^*:\omega_{C_h}\to\omega_B$ injective. Equation (916.8) gives
$$
\pi^*\psi^*=p\,\operatorname{id}_{\omega_B}.
\tag{916.16}
$$
It follows that $\pi^*$ induces
$$
j_h:\omega_{\mathscr D_h}
=\omega_{C_h}/\psi^*\omega_B
\longrightarrow\omega_B/p\omega_B.
\tag{916.17}
$$
This is the cotangent pullback of the actual quotient $\pi_h$ in (916.6). To check that assertion, use the square obtained by restricting $\pi:B\to C_h$ to
$B[p]\to\mathscr D_h$. Its cotangent square is exactly (916.17), and the standard kernel sequence for $[p]_B$ identifies
$\omega_{B[p]}=\omega_B/p\omega_B$.

The map (916.17) is injective **as a homomorphism of $R$-modules**. If $\pi^*w=pv$ for $w\in\omega_{C_h}$ and $v\in\omega_B$, then (916.16) implies
$$
\pi^*(w-\psi^*v)=0.
$$
Injectivity of $\pi^*$ gives $w=\psi^*v$, which is precisely the zero class in the source of (916.17). Thus
$$
\boxed{\omega_{\mathscr D_h}\hookrightarrow\omega_B/p\omega_B.}
\tag{916.18}
$$

This proof does not tensor an injection with the residue field. Such a tensor operation need not preserve it. For example the integral injection
$$
R/(\varpi^{e-c})\longrightarrow R/(\varpi^e),
\qquad x\longmapsto\varpi^c x,\qquad 0<c<e,
\tag{916.19}
$$
induces the zero cotangent map after tensoring with $k$. The same phenomenon occurs in the upper-half case below. Thus (916.18) does not contradict the characteristic-$p$ Frobenius example that rules out a naive special-fiber cotangent injection.

## 5. The original positive coordinate has an exact annihilator

Use exactly the ordered Raynaud coordinates of R908:
$$
\begin{array}{ll}
\mathscr A_h[p]:&
x_0^p=A_0x_1,\quad x_1^p=A_1x_0,\\
\mathscr D_h:&
y_0^p=D_0y_1,\quad y_1^p=D_1y_0,\\
\phi_h^*x_i=c_i y_i.&
\end{array}
\tag{916.20}
$$
They satisfy
$$
v_\varpi(A_0)=0,\quad v_\varpi(A_1)=e,\quad
v_\varpi(c_0)=0,\quad v_\varpi(c_1)=r_h,
$$
$$
v_\varpi(D_0)=r_h,\qquad
v_\varpi(D_1)=e-pr_h.
\tag{916.21}
$$
The units in these equations remain the original units; none is set equal to one.

Taking cotangents at the augmentation in (916.20) gives the direct sum
$$
\omega_{\mathscr D_h}
=R/(D_1)\,dy_0\ \oplus\ R/(D_0)\,dy_1.
\tag{916.22}
$$
In particular
$$
\operatorname{Ann}_R(dy_0)=(D_1)
=(\varpi^{e-pr_h}).
\tag{916.23}
$$
This is an exact annihilator, not just a relation that kills $dy_0$.

On the target, $A_0$ is a unit. Therefore
$$
\omega_{\mathscr A_h[p]}=R/(p)\,dx_0,
\tag{916.24}
$$
with the original positive CM ordering. The reduction of the unit generator $\omega_h$ is a unit multiple of $dx_0$. Pulling back by the actual $\phi_h$ gives a unit multiple of $c_0dy_0$, hence a unit multiple of $dy_0$. There is no additional power of $\varpi$, since $v_\varpi(c_0)=0$.

By (916.12–14), the class of $w_h$ in the quotient (916.15) is this same unit multiple of $dy_0$. Applying the actual injection (916.18) proves
$$
\operatorname{Ann}_R
\left(f_h^*\omega_h\bmod p\omega_B\right)
=(\varpi^{e-pr_h}).
\tag{916.25}
$$
This step uses the original coordinate map in (916.20) and the actual BT factorization; it is not inferred merely from the generic isomorphism type of $\mathscr D_h$.

## 6. Exact content and the nonzero Igusa layer

If $M$ is a free $R$-module and $v\in M$ has nonzero class modulo $p=\varpi^e$, write
$v=\varpi^c v_0$, with $v_0$ primitive and $0\le c<e$. Then
$$
\operatorname{Ann}_R(v\bmod pM)
=(\varpi^{e-c}).
\tag{916.26}
$$
Indeed at least one coordinate of $v_0$ is a unit; it proves both containments in (916.26).

The module in (916.23) is nonzero because $e-pr_h>0$ for every branch in (916.3). Therefore the class in (916.25) is nonzero. Comparing (916.25) and (916.26) gives
$$
c=pr_h.
\tag{916.27}
$$
This proves (916.4), and multiplying by the unchanged $p^{a_h}$ proves (916.5).

For every upper-half label, the actual integral differential
$$
L_h=\varpi^{-p(2h-n)}f_h^*\omega_h
\tag{916.28}
$$
is consequently primitive. Thus the conditional object of R913(913.20–24) now has a nonzero reduction:
$$
\boxed{
0\ne\overline L_h\in
(\omega_{B_{\mathrm I}})_
{\operatorname N^{\,1-h},\,\delta=\zeta}.}
\tag{916.29}
$$
R913 identifies its full marked Igusa weight as
$k_+=2h-p+1$ and its raw Hecke eigenvalues, after the complete correspondence correction, as those of the same-label companion $F_{k_+}$. The other weight
$k_-=2p+2-2h$ still belongs to the Cartier-cokernel functional of R913; no identification of the two objects is made.

## 7. Boundaries of the conclusion

The new equality concerns the full invariant differential module of the good Raynaud abelian part. It does not show that a specified componentwise coefficient is a unit, or that a chosen normal jet at the contracted original component is the first nonzero coefficient.

The original factor $p^{a_h}$ is still present in (916.5). The proof did not prove $a_h=0$, did not replace the original quotient by an optimal quotient, and did not identify the full Hodge image of $q$ with a saturated lattice.

The nonzero vector (916.29) lies in the actual marked Hecke space described in R913. Equality with a normalized named companion, with a specified scalar, requires the corresponding source comparison. Even such an equality must still be transported through the original bad model to the $c_1$ normal parameter. The coefficient of the original $u_{GF}$ in R897/R898, and the primitive same-$h$ expression
$P_{h-1}(1/s)f_i g_j$, have not been replaced by this global good-model result.
