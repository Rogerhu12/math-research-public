# R953 — The original high-weight boundary complex and its regular projection

Date: 2026-11-20. Session: serre-public-astra-20261120-de0a3e3b / literature_frontier. **Status: proved identities and an explicit complex; the low-to-high nonvanishing comparison remains open.** This result continues R944, R947 and R950. It uses R946's Hasse evaluation sequence as an existing input, not as a new result.

The new construction keeps the original $G=\mathcal T^{r-1}F$ and identifies the high-weight inverse-Frobenius class with the regular projection of R947's actual primitive $W$. Its local value is computed, including the original factor six. A modified bundle makes all operations regular on the tame base; its elementary quotient complex also displays the exact extra data that the low-weight principal part does not remove.

## 1. Objects and two different classes

Use the same connected fine full-six base $X$ and cusps $C$ as R944–R947. Let $\Sigma$ be its full reduced Hasse divisor, and put
$$
\mathscr E_r=\operatorname{Sym}^r\mathscr H,\qquad
r\in\{2,8\},\quad k=p+1-r,\quad j=k-2=p-1-r.
\tag{953.1}
$$
The original rows and their prime restrictions remain unchanged. In particular $r<p-1$. Write
$$
F=V_k^\dagger,\qquad V=V_{r+2}^\dagger,\qquad
G=\mathcal T^{r-1}F.
$$
Locally,
$$
A=\mathcal A/\rho^{p-1},\quad
b_P=(\mathcal B/\rho^{p+1})(P),\quad
g_P=(G/\rho^{pr})(P),\quad v_P=(V/\rho^{r+2})(P).
$$
Retain the normalized symmetric-power pairing and the canonical horizontal conjugate frame $\sigma_\rho$ from R944:
$$
\langle\rho,\sigma_\rho\rangle=A,\quad
\sigma_\rho(P)=-b_P\rho(P),\quad
\nabla\sigma_\rho=0.
\tag{953.2}
$$

R947 supplies the global rational section
$$
W=6Z+6^{r+1}r!\frac{\widetilde g\,\sigma_\rho^r}{A^p},
\qquad \nabla W=\Omega_V,
\quad
\Omega_V=(V/\rho^{r+2})\rho^rK_\rho.
\tag{953.3}
$$
Its complete polar part at each $P$ is
$$
L_P\frac{\sigma_\rho^r}{A^p},
\qquad L_P=-\frac{v_Pb_P^{p-r-2}}{r+1}.
\tag{953.4}
$$
The inverse $p$th powers of (953.4) define the **low-weight** class
$z_V\in H^1(X,\omega^{-r})$. R950 proves its specified pairing with $V$ is nonzero.

By contrast, the class used in the original R932 functional will be denoted
$$
v_{\mathrm{high}}\in H^1(X,\omega^{-j}).
\tag{953.5}
$$
It will be obtained from the regular projection of the same $W$, not from (953.4) itself.

## 2. A regular horizontal map on the whole tame base

The conjugate line is the actual line subbundle
$$
\mathscr L_r=\mathsf f_r(F_X^*\omega^{-r})
\subset\mathscr E_r.
$$
Define
$$
\boxed{\quad
\pi_r(s)=\langle s,\sigma_\rho^r\rangle_r\,\rho^{pr}
:\mathscr E_r\longrightarrow F_X^*\omega^r.
\quad}
\tag{953.6}
$$
Here $F_X^*\omega^r$ is identified with $\omega^{pr}$ equipped with its canonical Cartier connection. Its local frame $\rho^{pr}$ is horizontal.

The map is independent of $\rho$: under $\rho'=z\rho$ the two factors in (953.6) change by $z^{-pr}$ and $z^{pr}$. It is regular everywhere because $\sigma_\rho$ is a regular nowhere-zero local frame of the conjugate line, including at supersingular points. It is horizontal because the symmetric pairing is horizontal and $\nabla\sigma_\rho=0$.

It is a surjection of vector bundles. Locally extend $\sigma_\rho$ to a basis and take its normalized symmetric-power dual monomial; the coefficient $r!$ is a unit since $r<p$. Also
$$
\pi_r(\mathscr L_r)=0,
\tag{953.7}
$$
because the alternating pairing of $\sigma_\rho$ with itself is zero. Thus the source and the kernel are fixed rank, but (953.6) is not an idempotent endomorphism of $\mathscr E_r$.

On the ordinary Igusa cover with $\mathfrak a=a\rho$, the scalar map of R947 is exactly
$$
P_r(s)=\frac{\pi_r(s)}{\mathfrak a^{pr}}
=a^{-pr}\langle s,\sigma_\rho^r\rangle_r.
\tag{953.8}
$$
The regular map (953.6) and the singular trivialization $\mathfrak a^{-pr}$ are now distinguished.

Only the $j=0$ term of R944's full expression for $Z$ pairs nontrivially with $\sigma_\rho^r$. Its factor $A^{-r}$ cancels $\langle\rho^r,\sigma_\rho^r\rangle=A^r$. Hence
$$
\boxed{\quad \pi_r(Z)=G,\qquad \pi_r(W)=6G.\quad}
\tag{953.9}
$$
These are global equalities of sections of $\omega^{pr}$, with the second equality interpreted first rationally. The right side is regular, so the projection of $W$ is regular despite its poles.

## 3. An elementary modification where the primitive is regular

Inside $\mathscr E_r(*\Sigma)$ define
$$
\boxed{\quad
\mathscr E_r^+=\mathscr E_r+\mathscr L_r(p\Sigma).
\quad}
\tag{953.10}
$$
Locally it is generated by $\sigma_\rho^r/A^p$ together with a regular complement of $\mathscr L_r$ in $\mathscr E_r$. It is therefore a locally free bundle of rank $r+1$. The connection extends regularly across $\Sigma$: the added generator is horizontal, since $d(A^{-p})=0$, and replacing $\sigma_\rho^r$ by $A^p(\sigma_\rho^r/A^p)$ in the derivatives of a regular complementary basis creates no pole. The extension still has only the original logarithmic singularities at the cusps.

The full-polar-part assertion (953.4), rather than a leading-order assertion alone, implies
$$
W\in H^0(X,\mathscr E_r^+).
\tag{953.11}
$$
Equation (953.7) extends $\pi_r$ regularly and horizontally to $\mathscr E_r^+$. Its value remains $6G$.

Let $\mathscr D(\mathscr E)$ denote the logarithmic two-term connection complex
$[\mathscr E\to\mathscr E\otimes\Omega_X^1(\log C)]$. There is an exact sequence of complexes
$$
0\longrightarrow\mathscr D(\mathscr E_r)
\longrightarrow\mathscr D(\mathscr E_r^+)
\longrightarrow\mathscr Q_r
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{953.12}
$$
At $P\in\Sigma$, the quotient is the completely explicit finite complex
$$
\bigoplus_{m=1}^p\kappa(P)\,\sigma_\rho^r A^{-m}
\ \xrightarrow{\ d\ }\
\bigoplus_{m=1}^p\kappa(P)\,\sigma_\rho^r A^{-m}dA,
$$
$$
d(\sigma_\rho^rA^{-m})
=-m\,\sigma_\rho^rA^{-m-1}dA\quad(1\le m<p),
\qquad d(\sigma_\rho^rA^{-p})=0.
\tag{953.13}
$$
Consequently
$$
\mathcal H^0(\mathscr Q_r)_P
=\kappa(P)\,\sigma_\rho^r/A^p,\qquad
\mathcal H^1(\mathscr Q_r)_P
=\kappa(P)\,\sigma_\rho^r\,dA/A.
\tag{953.14}
$$
All intermediate coefficients $1,\ldots,p-1$ are units. Thus the local cohomological obstruction has one constant and one residue at each point, with no additional exceptional scalar depending on $r$ or $p$. The **number of points and the bundles are still $p$ dependent**; this calculation does not make the global obstruction a fixed-dimensional space.

The image of $W$ in $\mathcal H^0(\mathscr Q_r)$ is (953.4). The connecting map of (953.12) sends it to $[\Omega_V]$, exactly as in R947. The regular map $\pi_r$ kills the entire quotient $\mathscr Q_r$ and its polar coefficients. The nonzero regular section $\pi_rW=6G$ must therefore be retained as separate boundary data.

## 4. The original high-weight boundary class, including its frames

Use the already proved R946(946.15) evaluation sequence in its sheaf form:
$$
0\longrightarrow\omega^{-j}
\xrightarrow{\ \mathcal A\ }\omega^r
\longrightarrow\omega^r|_\Sigma
\longrightarrow0,\qquad j=p-1-r.
\tag{953.15}
$$
Let $\delta$ be its principal-parts connecting map, with the R932 Čech sign.

The section $G|_\Sigma$ of $\omega^{pr}|_\Sigma$ has a unique $p$th root as a section of $\omega^r|_\Sigma$ over the perfect zero-dimensional scheme $\Sigma$. Put
$$
\boxed{\quad
\gamma=(G|_\Sigma)^{1/p}
=\left((\pi_rW)|_\Sigma/6\right)^{1/p},\qquad
v_{\mathrm{high}}=\delta(\gamma).
\quad}
\tag{953.16}
$$
In the local frame $\rho$, its principal part is
$$
\frac{\gamma_P}A\rho^{-j},
\qquad \gamma_P=g_P^{1/p}.
\tag{953.17}
$$
This frame calculation has no residual scalar ambiguity. Under $\rho'=z\rho$,
$g'_P=z_P^{-pr}g_P$ and $\gamma'_P=z_P^{-r}\gamma_P$. The new expression (953.17) is
$$
\frac{\gamma_P}A(z/z_P)^r\rho^{-j},
$$
whose difference from the old expression is regular. This is also the local description of the intrinsic connecting map (953.15).

There is a useful independence statement. Suppose $W'$ is any other global section of $\mathscr E_r^+$ with $\nabla W'=\Omega_V$. Then $\pi_r(W'-W)$ is a global horizontal section of $(\omega^{pr},\nabla_{\mathrm{can}})$. In a local horizontal frame its scalar has zero derivative, hence is a $p$th power in the local normal ring. The unique roots glue with the transition functions of $\omega^r$. Thus
$$
\pi_r(W'-W)=H^p
\quad\text{for a global }H\in H^0(X,\omega^r).
$$
The roots in (953.16) change by $6^{-1/p}H|_\Sigma=H|_\Sigma/6$, and $\delta$ kills this evaluation of a global section. Therefore $v_{\mathrm{high}}$ is independent of the chosen global primitive in $\mathscr E_r^+$.

More generally, this gives a well-defined additive, $p^{-1}$-semilinear boundary operation on global one-forms $\Omega$ which admit a global primitive in $\mathscr E_r^+$. Its value is obtained by choosing such a primitive $W_\Omega$ and applying
$$
\Omega\longmapsto
\delta\left(\left((\pi_rW_\Omega)|_\Sigma/6\right)^{1/p}\right).
\tag{953.18}
$$
For the original $\Omega_V$, it is exactly (953.16). There is no assertion that every arbitrary low-weight cusp form admits a primitive in this bundle.

## 5. Exact identification with the R932 Igusa class

On the original fine Igusa cover write $a=\mathfrak a/\rho$, so $a^{p-1}=A$. Its local Kummer algebra has deck eigensheaves
$$
\pi_{\mathrm I,*}\mathcal O_{\mathrm I}
=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{p-2}\omega^{-i}.
\tag{953.19}
$$
The local map for the $i$th summand sends a section $f\rho^{-i}$ to $fa^i$. Its compatibility follows from $\rho'=z\rho$ and $a'=z^{-1}a$. The normalized compact cover is exactly this cyclic root cover locally at the reduced Hasse divisor and is étale away from it; hence the displayed eigensheaf description applies to the compact cover.

For $i=j=p-1-r$, (953.17) maps to
$$
\frac{\gamma_P}A a^j=\frac{g_P^{1/p}}{a^r}.
\tag{953.20}
$$
This is R932(932.30), with its original parameter, frame, and named $G$. Thus (953.16) corresponds under the actual deck eigensheaf identification to the **same** fine Igusa cohomology class used in $\mathcal R_V$. No re-identification from an inertia exponent or Hecke eigenvalue is being made.

The fine-base Serre pairing against $H\in S_k$ is
$$
\mathcal B_r(V,H):=\langle v_{\mathrm{high}},H\rangle_X
=\sum_{P\in\Sigma}\frac{g_P^{1/p}H_P}{b_P},
\qquad H_P=(H/\rho^k)(P).
\tag{953.21}
$$
This is $p^{-1}$-semilinear in the primitive source and linear in $H$.

The original scalar differential has the exact normalization
$$
\Phi_k(H)=\frac{H/\rho^k}{6a^j}K_\rho.
$$
Multiplying it by (953.20) gives the pullback of
$\gamma_PH_PK_\rho/(6A)$. Residue under $A=a^{p-1}$ multiplies by $p-1=-1$ in characteristic $p$. Consequently
$$
\boxed{\quad
\mathcal R_V(\Phi_k(H))=-\frac16\,\mathcal B_r(V,H).
\quad}
\tag{953.22}
$$
This is the precise base-to-Igusa adjunction, including the ramification and the original $1/6$.

For $H=F$, put $c_r=-6^{r-1}(r-1)!$. The established original evaluation
$g_P=c_rb_P^{r-1}F_P$ gives
$$
S=\sum_{P\in\Sigma}g_P^{1+1/p}/b_P^r
=c_r\mathcal B_r(V,F),
\tag{953.23}
$$
$$
\mathcal B_r(V,F)^p
=c_r\sum_{P\in\Sigma}F_P^{p+1}/b_P^k.
\tag{953.24}
$$
Equations (953.22–23) recover R932(932.36) with its exact sign and factor. Descending to the original component union retains R932's actual multiplier
$m_{\mathrm I}(c_h^{\mathrm F})^{1/p}/d_1$. In particular $d_1$ is not replaced by an orbit count or by $12$.

## 6. The actual regular remainder carries the high-weight value

At $P$, subtract the full horizontal polar section in (953.4) and write
$$
W_{0,P}=W-L_P\sigma_\rho^r/A^p.
\tag{953.25}
$$
This is regular by R947. It satisfies $\pi_rW_{0,P}=6G$ because the subtracted section is killed by $\pi_r$.

Choose any regular complementary frame $\nu$ with $\langle\rho,\nu\rangle=1$. Let $q_P$ be the coefficient of $\nu^r$ in $W_{0,P}(P)$, equivalently the scalar of its bottom Hodge quotient in the frame $\rho^{-r}$. Since $\sigma_\rho(P)=-b_P\rho(P)$ and $r$ is even, the only nonzero term in the pairing with $\sigma_\rho(P)^r$ is
$$
\langle q_P\nu(P)^r,(-b_P)^r\rho(P)^r\rangle_r=b_P^r q_P.
$$
Together with (953.9), this proves
$$
\boxed{\quad
q_P=\frac{6g_P}{b_P^r}
=-6^r(r-1)!\frac{F_P}{b_P}.
\quad}
\tag{953.26}
$$
There is no binomial coefficient missing: the normalized pairing has
$\langle\nu^r,\rho^r\rangle_r=(-1)^r=1$.

The quotient value in (953.26) is independent of the chosen regular subtraction of the same polar part. Two horizontal subtractions with that polar part differ by a regular horizontal section; at $P$ such a section lies in the conjugate line, which equals the Hodge line, and therefore has zero bottom Hodge quotient. It is also independent of the choice of complementary $\nu$ in the usual quotient-line sense.

Thus the two data extracted from the same global primitive are:

| Data from $W$ | Exact value at $P$ | Resulting class |
|---|---|---|
| Horizontal $p$th-power polar coefficient | $-v_Pb_P^{p-r-2}/(r+1)$ | $z_V\in H^1(\omega^{-r})$ |
| Regular bottom Hodge quotient | $-6^r(r-1)!F_P/b_P$ | via $\pi_r$, $\gamma_P=g_P^{1/p}$ and $\delta$: $v_{\mathrm{high}}\in H^1(\omega^{-j})$ |

The first is controlled by R950's nonzero low diagonal. The second is the precise boundary value entering the original high diagonal. Their shared primitive does not make these two local values equal.

## 7. The local nilpotent operator and the relative-Frobenius injection

This section gives an independent description of the horizontal sheaf; it is not needed for the direct Hecke proof in R950.

On the ordinary Igusa cover use R944's basis in that same original connection and the same original Tate coordinate $Q$:
$$
e=a\rho,\quad f=a^{-p}\sigma_\rho,\quad
\nabla e=f\eta,\quad\nabla f=0,\quad
\eta=a^2K_\rho.
$$
Let $D$ be the rational vector field dual to $\eta$. At the original Tate cusp $\eta=6\,dQ/Q$, so $D=\Theta_Q/6$. This uses the already fixed expansion $\mathfrak a(Q)=1$ and the canonical logarithmic extension; no new formal parameter or gauge transformation is made. In characteristic $p$,
$(\Theta_Q/6)^p=\Theta_Q/6$ on Laurent series. Faithfulness of the cusp expansion of rational functions therefore gives
$$
D^{[p]}=D
\tag{953.27}
$$
as rational derivations, not just as operators on a finite truncation.

On $\operatorname{Sym}^r\mathscr H$, write $N$ for the derivation with $N(e)=f$, $N(f)=0$. Its matrix in the displayed monomial basis is constant, and $N^p=0$ because $r<p$. Hence
$$
\nabla_D=D+N,\qquad
\psi(D):=\nabla_D^p-\nabla_{D^{[p]}}=-N.
\tag{953.28}
$$
If $D_\rho$ is dual to $K_\rho$, then $D_\rho=a^2D$. The $p$-linearity of $p$-curvature and $f=a^{-p}\sigma_\rho$ give on the rank-two bundle
$$
\psi(D_\rho)(v)=-\langle v,\sigma_\rho\rangle\,\sigma_\rho.
\tag{953.29}
$$
Both sides are regular at every supersingular point, so the equality extends there. On the symmetric power, (953.29) acts by the induced derivation. Applying it $r$ times gives
$$
\boxed{\quad
\psi(D_\rho)^r(s)=r!\,
\langle s,\sigma_\rho^r\rangle_r\,\sigma_\rho^r
\quad(r\ \mathrm{even}).
\quad}
\tag{953.30}
$$
The factor is obtained by applying the lowering operator successively to a pure dual monomial: it is $r(r-1)\cdots1$. Thus the local composition “inclusion after projection” is rank one and nilpotent. Its scalar $r!$ is a unit for both original rows. It is not a nonzero idempotent, and its diagonal cannot be assigned a positivity interpretation.

This calculation also determines the horizontal sheaf. A horizontal rational section is killed by $\psi(D)$, hence lies in the line generated by $f^r$, or equivalently by $\sigma_\rho^r$. Writing it $h\sigma_\rho^r$, horizontality says $dh=0$, so $h$ is a $p$th power. Since $\sigma_\rho^r$ generates a regular line subbundle, a regular horizontal section has a regular coefficient; its root is regular in the normal local rings. The roots glue with the transition functions of $\omega^{-r}$.

This reasoning includes the cusps. There the connection is the fixed logarithmic Tate extension from R944, $\sigma_\rho$ is still a nowhere-zero horizontal frame, and $D$ is the logarithmic vector field $\Theta_Q/6$. A section regular in the canonical extension has a regular scalar coefficient. Passing from $dh=0$ on the punctured neighborhood to its unique $p$th root introduces no pole at the cusp. No extra cusp-supported horizontal section exists, since the bundle is torsion free.

To specify the Frobenius twist exactly, let $F_X$ in the next paragraph mean **absolute** Frobenius of $X$. Push forward the connection complex along this map. Its differential is $\mathcal O_X$-linear for the action $a\cdot s=a^ps$. The map
$$
\omega^{-r}\longrightarrow
F_{X,*}\mathscr E_r,\qquad
u\rho^{-r}\longmapsto u^p\sigma_\rho^r
$$
is then $\mathcal O_X$-linear, and the preceding argument proves that its image is exactly the kernel of the pushed-forward differential. Thus
$$
\mathcal H^0\!\left(F_{X,*}\mathscr D(\mathscr E_r)\right)
=\omega^{-r}.
$$
If instead one uses the relative Frobenius $X\to X^{(p)}$, the target sheaf and its cohomology carry the corresponding $X^{(p)}$ coefficient twist. The absolute convention just stated is the one used to translate back to R944's $p$-semilinear map on the original $\kappa$-vector spaces.

For a two-term complex on a curve, the cohomology-sheaf spectral sequence always gives
$$
0\longrightarrow H^1(\mathcal H^0)
\longrightarrow\mathbb H^1
\longrightarrow H^0(\mathcal H^1)
\longrightarrow0:
$$
the only possible next obstruction is $H^2(\mathcal H^0)$, which is zero. The absolute Frobenius is finite and a homeomorphism on the underlying space, so this pushed-forward complex has the same additive hypercohomology, with scalar multiplication by $c\in\kappa$ changed to multiplication by $c^p$. Applying the displayed exact sequence therefore proves that
$$
\mathsf F_r:H^1(X,\omega^{-r})
\longrightarrow H^1_{\mathrm{dR,log}}(X,\mathscr E_r)
\quad\text{is injective.}
\tag{953.31}
$$
This is a proof using the explicitly computed horizontal sheaf. It assumes neither Hodge-to-de Rham degeneration nor injectivity from a bare inclusion of vector bundles. R950's argument avoids this issue altogether by calculating the principal-part Hecke action directly.

## 8. The exact unresolved scalar

The complex (953.12), the regular map (953.6), and the boundary (953.18) make the original high-weight pairing an actual operation:
$$
(\Omega_V,H)\longmapsto
\left\langle
\delta\left(\left((\pi_rW)|_\Sigma/6\right)^{1/p}\right),
H\right\rangle_X.
$$
For the named source and $H=F$, it is (953.21–24), exactly the original complete trace up to the displayed known factors. This addresses the source identification; it does not prove the value is nonzero.

The remaining comparison can now be stated without an unnamed projector:
one must relate the low polar-coefficient pairing
$$
\sum_P v_P^{p+1}/b_P^{r+2}
$$
to the bottom-Hodge regular-remainder pairing
$$
\sum_P F_P^{p+1}/b_P^{p+1-r}.
$$
The fixed local constants in the construction are $6$, $r!$, $r+1$, and $c_r$; all are units in the original range. The unknown is not one of those constants. It is the global effect of solving $\nabla W=\Omega_V$ and taking the regular quotient values (953.26), followed by their specified Serre evaluation. R946's exact Hasse evaluation quotient remains $p$ dependent and has the actual high CM support. R951's symmetric Bol obstruction, whose named $F$ is in its radical, is compatible with this distinction.

No step here identifies either pairing with a same-label normal derivative. In particular the R945 counterexamples remain valid. No mathematical computation was executed locally or in a new finite-prime test for this result.
