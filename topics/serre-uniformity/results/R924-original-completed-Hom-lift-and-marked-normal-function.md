# R924 — Completed original Hom lifts fix the marked first normal function

Date: 2026-11-16. Status: **proved for the original allowed primes and the two labels of R921**. Actual integral homomorphisms approximate the divided primitive projection. Their centered maps extend at the original nodes and converge on the whole formal tube of $D_1$. This removes the holomorphic ambiguity in R921 and determines the coefficient content of the original calibrated component. The original Tate index is retained.

## 1. Statement with the original index

Use all original objects, markings, and conventions of R921:
$$
p\ge41,\quad p\equiv2,5\pmod9,\quad
n=p+1,\quad e=p^2-1,\quad
R=R_0[\varpi],\quad \varpi^e=p.
\tag{924.1}
$$
The actual positive primitive quotient is $q:J\to A$. Its centered curve map is
$$
\gamma(P)=q([P-c_1])
=q([P-c_0])-q([c_1-c_0]).
\tag{924.2}
$$
Thus no new translation or base point is chosen. The index $a_h$ is the same integer as in
$$
e_hq_B=[p^{a_h}]\circ f_h.
\tag{924.3}
$$
Here $e_h$ is the original integral real-CM $p$-adic projector and $B$ is the actual good Raynaud abelian part.

Retain the table
$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c|c|c}
p\bmod12&h&r&k_+&g&M&d\\ \hline
11&(p+3)/2&2&4&(p+1)/12&2g&6\\
5 &(p+9)/2&8&10&(p-5)/12&2g+1&12 .
\end{array}
\tag{924.4}
$$
Write
$$
b_0=k_+(p-1),\qquad
t_h=(p-1)h,\qquad
R_*=\frac{2p-k_+}{d},\qquad
m_h=2g-1.
\tag{924.5}
$$
The same original component, center, and nodes are
$$
D_1:\ y^2=x(x^M+A),\quad A\in k^\times,\quad
\overline{c_1}=(0,0),\qquad
UV=\epsilon\varpi^d,\quad\epsilon\in R^\times.
\tag{924.6}
$$
The symbol $A$ in the equation of the curve is its original nonzero coefficient, not the target abelian variety.

There is an integral formal map $\mathscr F_h$ from the formal neighborhood of $D_1$ into the good one-dimensional formal factor $\widehat{\mathscr A}_h$, centered at $c_1$, such that
$$
[p^{a_h}]\mathscr F_h=e_h\gamma,\qquad
\mathscr F_h^*\omega_h=\Omega_h.
\tag{924.7}
$$
The second differential is exactly R921's divided differential. The construction below is a limit of actual centered homomorphisms, not an assertion that arbitrary BT maps algebraize.

Choose its original integral coordinate $Y_h$ with
$\omega_h=(1+O(Y_h))\,dY_h$ and exact semilinear weight $t_h$. Put
$T_h=Y_h(\mathscr F_h)$. Its first normal order and function are
$$
\boxed{
b_D(T_h)=b_0,\qquad
\overline{\varpi^{-b_0}T_h}\bigm|_{D_1}
=\kappa_hx^{g-1}y,\qquad \kappa_h\in k^\times.
}
\tag{924.8}
$$
Consequently R921's two coefficients satisfy
$$
\boxed{
\frac{\beta_h}{\alpha_h}
=
\begin{cases}
5A/4,&p\equiv11\pmod{12},\\
11A/10,&p\equiv5\pmod{12}.
\end{cases}}
\qquad \beta_h\ne0.
\tag{924.9}
$$
The equation is in $k$ and all denominators are units for $p\ge41$.

The original, unaltered map satisfies
$$
b_D\bigl(Y_h(e_h\gamma)\bigr)=ea_h+b_0,\qquad
\overline{\varpi^{-ea_h-b_0}Y_h(e_h\gamma)}
=\kappa_hx^{g-1}y.
\tag{924.10}
$$
There is also an exact conclusion in the original calibrated parameter
$$
z=\frac{a-1}{\varpi^{3p(p-1)}},\qquad
w=\frac{a-1}{p^3},\qquad z=\varpi^{3(p-1)}w.
\tag{924.11}
$$
For the original function of R859/R861,
$$
G_h(w)=\varpi^{-t_h}Y_h(e_h\gamma(P(z)))
       \bigm|_{z=\varpi^{3(p-1)}w},
\tag{924.12}
$$
one has
$$
\boxed{
p^{-a_h}G_h(w)\in wR_0\langle w\rangle,\quad
\deg\bigl(\overline{p^{-a_h}G_h}\bigr)=m_h,\quad
[w^{m_h}]\overline{p^{-a_h}G_h}\ne0.
}
\tag{924.13}
$$
Thus the ideal generated by every coefficient of this original component is exactly $p^{a_h}R_0$. This does not prove $a_h=0$ or a unit linear coefficient.

## 2. Divisibility of actual Hom maps

The input is integral on the original Jacobian, not only on a good-reduction quotient. R906 proves
$$
q(T_pJ)=q_B(T_pB)\subset T_pA.
\tag{924.14}
$$
R908's definition of the same index therefore gives
$$
e_hq(T_pJ)\subset p^{a_h}T_pA.
\tag{924.15}
$$
R859 proves, for the actual target,
$$
\operatorname{End}_{\mathbf Q}(A)\otimes\mathbf Z_p
=\mathcal O_F\otimes\mathbf Z_p
=\prod_{h'\in\mathcal H}\mathbf Z_p.
\tag{924.16}
$$
In particular $e_h$ lies in this completed integral endomorphism ring.

For each integer $j\ge1$, choose an actual
$$
E_j\in\operatorname{End}_{\mathbf Q}(A),\qquad
E_j\equiv e_h
\pmod{p^{j+a_h}(\operatorname{End}_{\mathbf Q}(A)\otimes\mathbf Z_p)}.
\tag{924.17}
$$
Such choices exist by the definition of completion of this finite free abelian group. Equations (924.15–17) imply
$$
E_jq(T_pJ)\subset p^{a_h}T_pA.
\tag{924.18}
$$
Since $T_pJ/p^{a_h}T_pJ=J[p^{a_h}](\overline{\mathbf Q})$ in characteristic zero, the actual homomorphism $E_jq$ kills the whole subgroup scheme $J[p^{a_h}]$. It factors through the genuine quotient by this subgroup, multiplication by $p^{a_h}$ on $J$. Hence there is an actual homomorphism
$$
\phi_j\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf Q}(J,A),\qquad
[p^{a_h}]\phi_j=E_jq.
\tag{924.19}
$$
No residual integral-Tate theorem is assumed: vanishing on the finite étale subgroup scheme and its actual quotient prove the factorization.

The congruence also gives
$$
E_{j+1}-E_j=p^{j+a_h}D_j,\qquad
D_j\in\operatorname{End}_{\mathbf Q}(A).
\tag{924.20}
$$
For a finite free abelian group $M$,
$M\cap p^r(M\otimes\mathbf Z_p)=p^rM$ inside its completion, as a $\mathbf Z$-basis proves coordinatewise.

Multiplication by a nonzero integer is injective on
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf Q}(J,A)$: a homomorphism with image in a finite group scheme has connected image and is zero in characteristic zero. Thus
$$
\phi_{j+1}-\phi_j=[p^j]D_jq.
\tag{924.21}
$$
The actual maps define a canonical element
$$
\phi_h=\lim_j\phi_j
\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf Q}(J,A)\otimes\mathbf Z_p,\qquad
p^{a_h}\phi_h=e_hq.
\tag{924.22}
$$
This is a completed Hom element, not necessarily one algebraic homomorphism. The limit is independent of the approximants by torsion freeness. The next sections prove where its evaluation is integral.

## 3. Extension at nodes and contraction of the same component

Consider the centered maps
$$
\Phi_j(P)=\phi_j([P-c_1]),\qquad
\Psi_j(P)=D_jq([P-c_1]).
\tag{924.23}
$$
They are actual $\mathbf Q$-morphisms to the fixed target: the original $c_1$ is rational, as stated explicitly in R823(823.2), with its original source in R616. In particular centering introduces no extra ramified field or descent character. They satisfy
$$
\Phi_{j+1}-\Phi_j=[p^j]\Psi_j.
\tag{924.24}
$$
All are exactly zero at $c_1$.

Each generic map extends over a proper regular semistable source model with values in the good abelian scheme $\mathcal A/R$. This requires extension at nodes, beyond the Néron property on the smooth locus. Resolve the rational map on the regular surface by point blowups, using Stacks Project, Lemma 54.4.3, Tag 0C5H. Every exceptional component is a projective line over the residue field. Every map from that line to $\mathcal A_k$ is constant: after centering it factors through the Jacobian of $\mathbf P^1$, which is zero. Intersecting exceptional curves have the same constant image.

The resolved map contracts each connected exceptional fiber. The closure of its graph over the original normal surface is proper and has single-point fibers; it is quasi-finite, hence finite. It is birational, so normality makes the graph projection an isomorphism. This descends the map across the indeterminate points. The same argument descends it when rational chains are contracted to the original nodes (924.6). Uniqueness follows from separatedness and agreement on the dense generic fiber. This proves integral extension on the actual nodal model.

The reduction of every map in (924.23) is zero on $D_1$. This is R376's retained local contraction argument, with the positive CM realization fixed in R575/R859. The order-three inertia element fixes $D_1$ pointwise, while its automorphism $\iota$ on the original target satisfies
$$
(1-\iota)(2+\iota)=[3].
\tag{924.25}
$$
Thus the fixed subgroup is finite étale, since $p\ne3$. Equivariance puts connected $D_1$ into that finite subgroup; its value at $c_1$ is zero. Hence its entire image is zero. The argument applies to $\phi_j$ as to $q$: it uses the fixed target representation and actual descent, not the degree or optimality of the homomorphism.

Every node on the closure of $D_1$ is consequently sent to zero as well. No arbitrary translation on an Igusa component is introduced.

## 4. Integral convergence on the whole formal tube

On a smooth affine open of $D_1$, all good formal-group coordinate functions of (924.23) lie in $(\varpi)$. At a node use the actual complete ring
$$
\mathcal R=R[[U,V]]/(UV-\epsilon\varpi^d),\qquad
I_D=(\varpi,U).
\tag{924.26}
$$
Their coordinate functions lie in $I_D$, because their reductions on $D_1$ are zero.

For a commutative formal group over $R$ and an ideal $I$ containing $\varpi$, multiplication by $p$ has linear term $p\,\mathbf Y$ and other terms of total degree at least two. Hence for $m\ge1$,
$$
[p](I^m)\subset I^{m+1},\qquad
[p^j](I)\subset I^{j+1}.
\tag{924.27}
$$
Indeed $p=\varpi^e\in I^e$, $e\ge2$, and
$\min(m+e,2m)\ge m+1$. This is a containment for the actual formal group, not an additive replacement.

By (924.24), the maps are uniformly $\varpi$-adically Cauchy on the smooth tube. At a node their group differences belong to $I_D^{j+1}$, and therefore so do their ordinary coordinate differences. They converge in (924.26). Explicitly,
$I_D^{j+1}\subset(\varpi,U,V)^{j+1}$ gives a complete-local-ring limit, and every $I_D^m$ is closed there. Where $V$ is invertible, (924.26) identifies $I_D$ with $(\varpi)$; the node limit is thus the same as the tube's $\varpi$-adic limit. The local limits glue.

They preserve the group law, actual descent, and centering. The action of the completed target endomorphism ring on the formal group is continuous, as also constructed in R861. The limit $\mathscr F_h$ satisfies
$$
[p^{a_h}]\mathscr F_h=e_h\gamma.
\tag{924.28}
$$
It lies in the $h$ factor. Indeed (924.22) and torsion freeness of the completed Hom module give $e_h\phi_h=\phi_h$. Evaluation on this tube respects completed endomorphisms: its continuity follows from (924.27), since every map being evaluated reduces to zero on $D_1$. Evaluating that identity gives $e_h\mathscr F_h=\mathscr F_h$. This uses the original completed Hom element.

Taking invariant differentials commutes with these limits. In the integral module of R921 §2 one obtains precisely
$$
\mathscr F_h^*\omega_h
=p^{-a_h}(e_hq)^*\omega_h
=\Omega_h.
\tag{924.29}
$$
It is the same $f_h$ differential on the Raynaud abelian part. Complete BT full faithfulness has not been substituted for the original source argument.

The construction is independent of $E_j$. Two choices define the same completed Hom element, and the difference estimates give the same evaluation. Equation (924.28) retains the original map exactly.

## 5. No earlier invisible $p$th-power function layer

Write $T_h=Y_h(\mathscr F_h)$. It is zero modulo $\varpi$ on $D_1$. Let $c$ be its first nonzero normal order. It is finite because (924.29) is nonzero. Locally
$$
\Omega_h=(1+O(T_h))\,dT_h.
\tag{924.30}
$$
Relative differentiation does not lower $\varpi$ order along the smooth component. R921 gives $b_D(\Omega_h)=b_0$, whence
$$
1\le c\le b_0.
\tag{924.31}
$$
Its first function reduction
$$
t_c=\overline{\varpi^{-c}T_h}\bigm|_{D_1}
\tag{924.32}
$$
is a nonzero rational function, regular away from the nodes and zero at $c_1$. Its pole order at every node is at most
$$
\left\lfloor c/d\right\rfloor.
\tag{924.33}
$$
To prove this, put the integral node function in R921's normal form
$\sum a_iU^i+\sum_{j\ge1}b_jV^j$. Substitute
$U=\epsilon\varpi^d/V$. A negative Laurent term in layer $c$ can only come from
$v_\varpi(a_i)+di=c$, so $i\le c/d$. Distinct negative powers cannot cancel a higher pole into this layer, and the $V$-axis terms are regular. All integral lift terms and the node unit remain present.

If $c<b_0$, (924.29–30) imply
$$
dt_c=0.
\tag{924.34}
$$
Over perfect $k$, the kernel of
$d:k(D_1)\to\Omega^1_{k(D_1)/k}$ is $k(D_1)^p$. Thus $t_c$ is a $p$th power, whose pole orders must all be divisible by $p$. But
$$
\frac{b_0}{d}=
\begin{cases}
2(p-1)/3,&p\equiv11\pmod{12},\\
5(p-1)/6,&p\equiv5\pmod{12},
\end{cases}
\quad <p.
\tag{924.35}
$$
Equations (924.31–33) exclude every such pole. The function is constant on proper connected $D_1$, and its value at $c_1$ makes it zero, a contradiction.

Therefore
$$
c=b_0,\qquad
t_h^{\rm nor}:=\overline{\varpi^{-b_0}T_h},\qquad
dt_h^{\rm nor}=\nu_h.
\tag{924.36}
$$
Its poles still have order below $p$. Differentiation raises a pole of order $s<p$ to one of order $s+1$, since $s$ is a unit. R921's exact node pole $R_*+1$ consequently means that $t_h^{\rm nor}$ has exact node pole order $R_*$, with no larger pole at any node.

This excludes lower layers even when differentiation alone could not detect them. The strict inequality (924.35) and the global centered function are essential.

## 6. Unique actual primitive and the holomorphic coefficient

The first function layer has the same original inertia character as the R921 differential:
$$
q_u^{h-k_+},\qquad q_u=u^{p-1}.
\tag{924.37}
$$
Division by $\varpi^{b_0}$ subtracts $b_0$ from weight $t_h$. Since $h-k_+$ is odd, this is anti-invariant under the hyperelliptic involution $q_u=-1$.

An anti-invariant rational function regular on the affine curve is
$$
P(x)y,\qquad P(x)\in k[x].
\tag{924.38}
$$
A finite pole of its rational coefficient would remain a pole at a branch point: $x-x_0$ has order two there while $y$ has order one. Thus no finite denominator is allowed.

The monomial $x^jy$ has character $q_u^{6j+3}$, giving
$$
j\equiv g-1\pmod M.
\tag{924.39}
$$
For $M=2g$ its unique-infinity pole order is $2j+2g+1$. The bound
$R_*=4g-1$ gives $j\le g-1$. For $M=2g+1$, its pole at either infinity is
$j+g+1$, and $R_*=2g$ gives the same inequality. Hence
$$
t_h^{\rm nor}=\kappa_hx^{g-1}y,\qquad\kappa_h\ne0.
\tag{924.40}
$$
This proves (924.8) at the original center.

Differentiating $y^2=x(x^M+A)$ gives
$$
d(x^{g-1}y)
=x^{g-1}
\left[
\left(g+\frac{M-1}{2}\right)x^M
+\left(g-\frac12\right)A
\right]\frac{dx}{y}.
\tag{924.41}
$$
Comparison with R921 yields
$$
\alpha_h=\kappa_h\left(g+\frac{M-1}{2}\right),\qquad
\beta_h=\kappa_h\left(g-\frac12\right)A.
\tag{924.42}
$$
For $p\equiv11\pmod{12}$ the two factors are $(p-2)/6$ and $(p-5)A/12$, whose ratio reduces to $5A/4$. For $p\equiv5\pmod{12}$ they are $(p-5)/6$ and $(p-11)A/12$, whose ratio reduces to $11A/10$. They are nonzero for $p\ge41$. This proves (924.9), with the original $A$ and without changing any node phase.

Since $x=A^{-1}y^2+\cdots$ at $c_1$,
$$
\operatorname{ord}_{c_1}t_h^{\rm nor}=2g-1=m_h,\qquad
\operatorname{ord}_{c_1}\nu_h=2g-2.
\tag{924.43}
$$
The smaller alternative in R921(921.38–39) is therefore attained by the actual source. The completed original Hom construction and global pole bound provide the extra input that the node differential alone lacked.

## 7. Multiplying back the unchanged original index

Retain $[p^{a_h}]$ in (924.28). For the actual formal group with invariant differential
$H(Y)dY$, $H(0)=1$, write
$$
[p](Y)=pY+\sum_{m\ge2}c_mY^m.
$$
Invariant-differential pullback gives
$$
[p]'(Y)=p\,\frac{H(Y)}{H([p](Y))}\in pR[[Y]].
\tag{924.44}
$$
Thus $c_m\in pR$ if $p\nmid m$, and all other $c_m$ are integral.

For an integral function of first order $b>p+1=e/(p-1)$, the linear term has order $e+b$. Terms with $m\ge2$ and $p\nmid m$ have order at least $e+mb>e+b$; terms with $p\mid m$ have order at least $pb>e+b$. Hence
$$
[p](T)=pT+\text{terms of order strictly greater than }e+b.
\tag{924.45}
$$
Here $b=b_0=k_+(p-1)>p+1$. Induction applies to every further multiplication by $p$, proving (924.10) with the same $\kappa_hx^{g-1}y$. The index has not been removed.

## 8. Original calibration and exact coefficient content

The exact source coordinate (924.11) has
$\tau(z)=\xi^{3(p-1)}z$. Expand
$$
T_h(z)=\sum_{m\ge1}b_mz^m\in zR[[z]].
\tag{924.46}
$$
All coefficients are divisible by $\varpi^{b_0}$; the coefficient at $m=m_h$ has exactly that valuation, by (924.40–43) and the unit change between $z$ and local parameter $y$.

Descent gives $\tau(b_m)=\xi^{t_h-3(p-1)m}b_m$. Thus
$$
\widetilde G_h(w)
:=\varpi^{-t_h}T_h(\varpi^{3(p-1)}w)
=\sum_{m\ge1}b_m\varpi^{3(p-1)m-t_h}w^m
\tag{924.47}
$$
has coefficients in $\operatorname{Frac}(R_0)$. Every nonzero coefficient has $\varpi$ valuation divisible by $e$. Integrality of $b_m$, $m\ge1$, and $0<t_h<e$ make that valuation greater than $-e$, hence nonnegative. It tends to infinity with $m$. Consequently
$$
\widetilde G_h\in wR_0\langle w\rangle.
\tag{924.48}
$$
For both labels,
$$
h=k_++3m_h.
\tag{924.49}
$$
The coefficient of (924.47) at $m_h$ therefore has valuation zero. For $m>m_h$ its valuation is at least
$$
b_0+3(p-1)m-t_h=3(p-1)(m-m_h)>0.
\tag{924.50}
$$
Being a multiple of $e$, that coefficient lies in $pR_0$. Hence
$$
Q_h(w):=\overline{\widetilde G_h(w)}
\in k[w],\qquad Q_h(0)=0,\qquad \deg Q_h=m_h.
\tag{924.51}
$$
Coefficients below degree $m_h$ remain undetermined. Higher normal layers before calibration can contribute to them.

Normalize the actual multiplication series in the same coordinate:
$$
[p]^{\rm cal}(W)=\varpi^{-t_h}[p](\varpi^{t_h}W).
\tag{924.52}
$$
Its coefficients lie in $R_0$ by descent. For $m\ge2$ with $p\nmid m$, (924.44) gives valuation at least $e+(m-1)t_h>e$. For $p\mid m$, the valuation is at least $(p-1)t_h>e$. In either case unramified descent rounds this to at least $2e$. The series is restricted since $t_h>0$. Thus
$$
[p]^{\rm cal}(W)=pW+p^2H(W),\qquad
H\in W^2R_0\langle W\rangle.
\tag{924.53}
$$
Iteration gives
$$
[p^{a_h}]^{\rm cal}(W)\in p^{a_h}R_0\langle W\rangle,\qquad
p^{-a_h}[p^{a_h}]^{\rm cal}(W)\equiv W\pmod p.
\tag{924.54}
$$
Substitute (924.47) in (924.28). Equations (924.51–54) prove exactly
$$
p^{-a_h}G_h\in wR_0\langle w\rangle,\qquad
\overline{p^{-a_h}G_h}=Q_h.
\tag{924.55}
$$
This proves (924.13). The full calibrated coefficient ideal is $p^{a_h}R_0$ for the unmodified original quotient.

## 9. Original-disc zeros and the still specified critical coefficient

The rightmost unit coefficient of $p^{-a_h}G_h$ has index $m_h$. Restricted Weierstrass preparation gives at most $m_h$ zeros, counted with multiplicity, on $v_p(w)\ge0$. The center is one. The source change (924.11) is the original branch from R378, so
$$
\#\{\text{noncentral zeros of this component in }v_p(a-1)\ge3\}
\le m_h-1=
\begin{cases}
(p-11)/6,&p\equiv11\pmod{12},\\
(p-17)/6,&p\equiv5\pmod{12}.
\end{cases}
\tag{924.56}
$$
Over an algebraically closed complete extension of the coefficient field, the open disc's exact number of zeros, counted with multiplicity, is the least unit-coefficient index, as in R857. It inherits the displayed bound, and that least index is not asserted to equal $m_h$. This is not an exact count of rational zeros.

On this calibrated disc the divided good coordinate is
$\varpi^{t_h}\widetilde G_h(w)$, with $v_p(\varpi^{t_h})=h/(p+1)>1/2$. It lies in the injective formal-logarithm domain, as does its image under $[p^{a_h}]$. Thus this is also a zero bound for the centered original Abel logarithm in the component. A positive-rank rational-point candidate has not been assumed to have zero Abel image.

The theorem proves the integral formal source lift, exact first normal function, nonzero $\beta_h$, and equality of calibrated content with the actual Tate index. It does not prove that index zero, or replace a specified low coefficient by the unit at degree $m_h$.

In particular R898's target
$$
\overline{J_{3,2}Z_h(u_{GF})}
=\frac{(6B)^5}{12}
 \left(P_{h-1}(1/s)\,f_i g_{N-1-i}\right)^p,
\qquad p=6N+5,\quad h=6i+7,
\tag{924.57}
$$
still requires its same-label source comparison. The polynomial $Q_h$ has growing degree, and its lower coefficients have not been identified with this expression. R706's first-block exception and R739's reflection counterexample remain valid. The original height comparison is separately unfinished.
