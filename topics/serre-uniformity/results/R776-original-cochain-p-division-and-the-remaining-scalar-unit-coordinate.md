# R776: the original cochain p-division and the remaining scalar unit coordinate

Date: 2026-11-14. **Status: proved.** Assume that the original
ordinary class coefficient $b$ is nonzero. The genuine supported
dual primitive of R755 has the following exact expression in the
specified ordinary scalar-layer coordinate of R773:

$$
\boxed{
\frac{I_\alpha}{\varepsilon}
=32\bigl(1+i-18ib\eta\bigr).
}
\tag{776.1}
$$

Here $\eta$ is precisely the coefficient in
$U_V=(4/C)n+\eta u$, with the original $A_\alpha$, original
unaveraged projection, and original mixed Robert unit.
The formula is obtained by constructing the actual Kummer
division cochain over the original scalar field and comparing
its restriction with the already existing rational supported
primitive. It does not substitute an arbitrary local p-th root.

Write $\eta=\eta_R+i\eta_I$ with $\eta_R,\eta_I\in\mathbf F_{43}$.
Rationality and (776.1) give the additional exact statements

$$
\boxed{
18b\eta_R=1,\qquad
\frac{I_\alpha}{\varepsilon}=32+17b\eta_I.
}
\tag{776.2}
$$

For the original finite-flat target, put
$\eta_{\rm fl}=\eta+8i$. Then

$$
\boxed{
\frac{I_0}{\varepsilon}
=32+17b(\eta_I+8)
=21\sigma_2+2b(J_\ell-18).
}
\tag{776.3}
$$

The remaining $\eta_I$, $\sigma_2$, $J_\ell$, and $b$ have
not been evaluated. In particular the case $b=24$ is not
excluded by this comparison alone.

## 1. The original fields, ordinary classes, and phase map

Keep the original $p=43$, $\mathcal O=\mathbf Z_{43}[i]$,
$k=\mathcal O/p$, $F$, $M$, and

$$
V=M^T=LF_s,\qquad
\Gamma=\langle\gamma\rangle,\qquad
S=\langle s\rangle,\qquad
x=\gamma-1,\quad z=s-1.
$$

In this result $z$ denotes the augmentation operator $s-1$.
The local group element $f^{39}$ will instead be denoted by
$g_s$, to distinguish it from this operator.
The negative-adjoint direction of R755 is denoted by $y_{\rm dir}$:

$$
y_{\rm dir}=\ell_{\rm cyc}-2i\kappa_q
=2\kappa_S-2i\kappa_q.
\tag{776.4}
$$

The symbol $y$ without this subscript continues to denote the
original ordinary Gamma norm unit.

Use additive notation in multiplicative character modules.
R768 proves that, when $b\ne0$, the true restriction of the
supported primitive $E_\alpha$ to $G_V$ is represented by an
ordinary class $e_V\in E_V/pE_V$, and that the original class
$d_q=\kappa(A_\alpha)$ is represented there by $U_V$.
There is an actual full-divisor principalization $Z\in\mathscr K_V$
such that

$$
U=A_\alpha-pZ\in E_V,\qquad [U]=U_V,
\tag{776.5}
$$

where $\mathscr K_V=(V^\times\otimes\mathcal O)_{\chi^-}$.
The full divisor of Z is the one in R768; no q prime has
been removed from the ordinary class group.

For any ordinary representative $e\in E_V$ of $e_V$,

$$
xe\equiv-2iU,\qquad ze\equiv2U\pmod{pE_V}.
\tag{776.6}
$$

R773 gives the complete ordinary lattice, generated by the
actual $c=c_s,u_s,y$, and the full direct q phase map

$$
r_\mu:E_V\longrightarrow k,\qquad
r_\mu(c)=9,\quad r_\mu(u_s)=25,\quad
r_\mu(y)=25+9b.
\tag{776.7}
$$

Every ordinary unit of V has p-primary residue in $\mu_p$,
not merely in $\mu_{p^2}$. Both Gamma and S fix this residue.
Consequently

$$
r_\mu(pE_V)=r_\mu(xE_V)=r_\mu(zE_V)=0.
\tag{776.8}
$$

R773 also proves

$$
r_\mu(U)=r_\mu(e)=0.
\tag{776.9}
$$

These are full phase statements in the original marking.
The first follows from $U_V=(4/C)n+\eta u$, and the second
from the explicit form of all solutions of (776.6).

## 2. The two divisions are actual ordinary units

Equation (776.6) makes the following quotients actual elements
of the ordinary lattice:

$$
\boxed{
F_\gamma=\frac{xe+2iU}{p}\in E_V,\qquad
F_S=\frac{ze-2U}{p}\in E_V.
}
\tag{776.10}
$$

No field-norm replacement is involved in their existence.
The congruences occur in $E_V/pE_V$, and $E_V$ is a
torsion-free $\mathcal O$ lattice.

The two numerators have a useful exact cancellation. Set

$$
D_*=x+iz,\qquad \mathfrak a=(x,z)\subset\mathcal O[\Gamma,S].
$$

Then

$$
F_\gamma+iF_S=\frac{D_*e}{p}.
\tag{776.11}
$$

For any class $\bar h\in E_V/pE_V$ killed by $D_*$, define

$$
\mathcal R(\bar h)
=r_\mu\left(\frac{D_*h}{p}\right).
\tag{776.12}
$$

It is well defined: replacing $h$ by $h+pv$ changes the
quotient by $D_*v\in\mathfrak aE_V$, whose phase is zero
by (776.8). It is k-linear. In particular

$$
\mathcal R(e_V)=r_\mu(F_\gamma)+i\,r_\mu(F_S).
\tag{776.13}
$$

Changing the actual principalization in (776.5) by an ordinary
unit $v$ replaces $U$ by $U-pv$, and changes the two divisions
by $-2iv$ and $2v$, respectively. Their combination in
(776.13) is unchanged. Thus the comparison uses the actual
class of $A_\alpha$, not a choice of its unit correction.

## 3. A global Kummer root cochain with the exact homotopy sign

This section compares (776.10) with the genuine primitive,
rather than identifying a module solution by its dimension.

Over $G_F$, the original representation $W$ is trivial and
$W(1)$ is identified with the original k-valued Tate module.
The field V does not contain $\mu_p$. For example,
$[V:K]=44p^2$, whereas $[K(\mu_p):K]=42$ does not divide
$44p^2$. In particular the p-torsion in $\mathscr K_V$ is
zero and

$$
H^0(G_V,W(1))=0.
\tag{776.14}
$$

Use the Kummer cochain $d_q$ of the actual $A_\alpha$.
Choose global p-th roots, in the algebraic multiplicative
module with coefficients in $\mathcal O$,

$$
p\rho=A_\alpha,\qquad p\beta=e.
$$

These roots implement the Kummer construction; they are not
selected by prescribing a local residue. A different global
root changes only the usual Kummer coboundary.

For $g\in G_F$, let $\widetilde y_g\in\mathcal O$ lift
$y_{\rm dir}(g)\in k$. The conjugation identity for the
restriction of the actual cochain, already proved in R768,
is

$$
[g e-e]=y_{\rm dir}(g)[A_\alpha]
\quad\text{in }\mathscr K_V/p\mathscr K_V.
$$

Thus there is a unique $F_g^A\in\mathscr K_V$ satisfying

$$
pF_g^A=g e-e-\widetilde y_gA_\alpha.
\tag{776.15}
$$

Uniqueness uses the absence of p-torsion just noted.
Define the p-torsion-valued cochain

$$
k_e(g)=g\beta-\beta-\widetilde y_g\rho-F_g^A.
\tag{776.16}
$$

Multiplying the right side by p gives zero, so this is
a $W(1)$-valued cochain under the original Kummer
identification. It is independent of the chosen lifts
$\widetilde y_g$: increasing one lift by $pc$ decreases
$F_g^A$ by $cA_\alpha$, and the two changes in (776.16)
cancel exactly.

Here is its differential, including the carry in those lifts.
Write

$$
\widetilde y_g+\widetilde y_h-\widetilde y_{gh}
=p\,c(g,h).
$$

Since $A_\alpha$ is fixed by $G_F$, (776.15) gives
$\delta F^A(g,h)=-c(g,h)A_\alpha$.
Using $p\rho=A_\alpha$ in (776.16) therefore yields

$$
\delta k_e(g,h)=-y_{\rm dir}(h)d_q(g).
\tag{776.17}
$$

This is the opposite-order product. The required correction
is the diagonal homotopy

$$
H_y(g)=y_{\rm dir}(g)d_q(g).
$$

The cocycle identities give, directly,

$$
\delta H_y(g,h)
=-y_{\rm dir}(g)\,g d_q(h)
 -y_{\rm dir}(h)d_q(g).
$$

Consequently the correct primitive is

$$
\boxed{
E'(g)=k_e(g)-H_y(g),\qquad
\delta E'=y_{\rm dir}\cup d_q.
}
\tag{776.18}
$$

The minus sign in (776.18) is essential.
On $G_V$, the direction vanishes and $F_g^A=0$.
Hence $E'|_{G_V}$ is exactly the Kummer cocycle of e.

Compare $E'$ with the restriction of the actual supported
rational $E_\alpha$. Their difference is a $G_F$ cocycle
whose restriction to $G_V$ has zero class. Such a cocycle
is a coboundary by (776.14). One can see this without
suppressing an inflation term: subtract a coboundary so
that it vanishes on $G_V$; normality then forces each of
its remaining values to be $G_V$ invariant, and (776.14)
forces that value to be zero.

Thus (776.18) computes the q restriction of the actual
rational primitive, up to a coboundary. It does not assert
that every bare solution of (776.6) is a rational supported
primitive. The actual supported primitive was used to choose
$e_V$ at the start. The construction retains its support
and cohomology class; no new arbitrary global class is inserted.

## 4. The original q generators and the quadratic constant

Work at the original quadratic completion
$K_q=\mathbf Q_{q^2}$. Retain the marked tame element
$x_q$ and positive quadratic Frobenius f of R755.
Set $g_s=f^{39}$; its image on V is precisely s, since
the original image of f is $s^{32}$ and
$32\cdot39\equiv1\pmod{43}$.
The marked values are

$$
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 &x_q&g_s\\ \hline
y_{\rm dir}&-2i&2\\
d_q&1&i\\
v=\kappa_S+i\kappa_q&i&1\\
\mathfrak p=-i\bar v^2&i&-i
\end{array}
\tag{776.19}
$$

All entries are in the same original coefficient algebra.
In particular $g_s$ is the actual scalar lift, not an
independently chosen local generator.

Put $\rho_U=\rho-Z$, so $p\rho_U=U$.
By (776.9), the p-primary residues of local roots $\beta$
and $\rho_U$ are in $\mu_p$. Both x_q and $g_s$ fix this
subgroup: the first is inertia, and the second acts through
the original unramified scalar direction, congruent to
$1$ modulo p. Therefore the p-primary phase of
$g\beta-\beta$ is zero for either of these two elements.

Rewriting (776.15) with $A_\alpha=U+pZ$ gives

$$
F_g^A=F_g^U-\widetilde y_gZ,\qquad
pF_g^U=g e-e-\widetilde y_gU.
$$

For the two marked elements, the ordinary units $F_g^U$
are exactly $F_\gamma,F_S$ of (776.10).
If $r_U$ denotes the phase of $\rho_U$, (776.16) gives

$$
k_e(x_q)=2ir_U-r_\mu(F_\gamma),\qquad
k_e(g_s)=-2r_U-r_\mu(F_S).
$$

These equalities take the p-torsion values in (776.16)
through the fixed original residue identification.
The root phase cancels in their specified combination:

$$
k_e(x_q)+i k_e(g_s)
=-r_\mu(F_\gamma)-i r_\mu(F_S).
\tag{776.20}
$$

The diagonal homotopy has the different, nonzero combination

$$
H_y(x_q)+iH_y(g_s)
=(-2i)\cdot1+i(2\cdot i)=-2(1+i).
$$

By (776.18),

$$
E'(x_q)+iE'(g_s)
=-\mathcal R(e_V)+2(1+i).
\tag{776.21}
$$

The original quadratic representative contributes
$\mathfrak p(x_q)+i\mathfrak p(g_s)=1+i$.
After subtracting it, the regularized cocycle therefore has

$$
\mathscr S_\alpha(x_q)+i\mathscr S_\alpha(g_s)
=1+i-\mathcal R(e_V).
\tag{776.22}
$$

Equation (776.22) is unaffected by the rational local gauge.
Indeed that gauge adds $y_{\rm dir}k_q$, whose displayed
combination is $(-2i+2i)k_q=0$, for every $k_q\in k$.
A coboundary over $G_F$ is zero on the quadratic local
group, where the coefficients are trivial.
Hence the comparison in §3 passes to this exact local
quantity, including its rational normalization.

R755's trace pairing gives

$$
\frac{I_\alpha}{\varepsilon}
=i\mathscr S_\alpha(f)+32\mathscr S_\alpha(x_q).
$$

The regularized restriction is an additive cocycle, so
$\mathscr S_\alpha(f)=32\mathscr S_\alpha(g_s)$.
Combining with (776.22) proves

$$
\boxed{
\frac{I_\alpha}{\varepsilon}
=32\bigl(1+i-\mathcal R(e_V)\bigr).
}
\tag{776.23}
$$

Both the factor 32 and the constant $1+i$ have thus been
obtained from the original marked generators and the exact
quadratic and diagonal corrections.

## 5. Exact division phases for n and e_n

We now evaluate (776.12) by the integral presentation of R773.
Use its specified elements

$$
n=x^{40}z^{42}c+i x^{41}z^{41}c-x^{42}z^{40}c,
$$
$$
e_n=-2i x^{39}z^{42}c+2x^{40}z^{41}c
       +2i x^{41}z^{40}c-2x^{42}z^{39}c,
$$
$$
e_u=-8(x^{40}z^{42}c+x^{42}z^{40}c).
\tag{776.24}
$$

The same letters denote these actual integral expressions
when a representative is required, and their reductions
when used in $E_V/pE_V$.

Because $\gamma^p=s^p=1$, the following identities hold
over the integers:

$$
x^{43}=-p\sum_{j=1}^{42}\frac{\binom{43}{j}}p x^j,
\qquad
z^{43}=-p\sum_{j=1}^{42}\frac{\binom{43}{j}}p z^j.
\tag{776.25}
$$

Every coefficient displayed on the right is integral.
In particular $x^{43}/p$ and $z^{43}/p$, as operators,
have positive augmentation degree.

The two derivatives of the specified $e_n$ telescope exactly:

$$
xe_n+2in=-2x^{43}z^{39}c,\qquad
ze_n-2n=-2i x^{39}z^{43}c.
\tag{776.26}
$$

After division by p, both right sides still belong to
$\mathfrak aE_V$, so their full q phases are zero.
Equivalently,

$$
D_*e_n=2x^{39}z^{43}c-2x^{43}z^{39}c,
\qquad \boxed{\mathcal R(e_n)=0.}
\tag{776.27}
$$

Likewise,

$$
D_*n=i x^{40}z^{43}c-x^{43}z^{40}c,
\qquad \boxed{\mathcal R(n)=0.}
\tag{776.28}
$$

The actual unit u is fixed by both groups integrally, so

$$
\boxed{\mathcal R(u)=0.}
\tag{776.29}
$$

These are calculations in the original integral lattice.
They do not follow merely from a mod-p socle shape.

## 6. The remaining division has the exact phase 18ib

It remains to evaluate $\mathcal R(e_u)$. This is where
division by p retains the original ordinary coefficient.

Work temporarily in the quotient $E_V/p\mathfrak aE_V$.
Write congruences in this quotient as $\equiv_*$. By
(776.25),

$$
x^{43}E_V\equiv_* z^{43}E_V\equiv_*0,
\qquad p\mathfrak aE_V\equiv_*0.
$$

Use the exact original identities, with $a=15i/7$,

$$
N_\Gamma c=(1-s^{11})u_s,\qquad
N_Sc=c_q,\qquad
xy=a\gamma^{22}c_q,\qquad
N_\Gamma y=N_Su_s.
\tag{776.30}
$$

In this quotient $N_\Gamma\equiv_*p+x^{42}$ and
$N_S\equiv_*p+z^{42}$ as operators. Put

$$
e_0=x^{42}z^{41}c,\qquad e_1=x^{41}z^{42}c.
$$

Multiplying the first relation in (776.30) by $z^{42}$
gives $x^{42}z^{42}c\equiv_*0$.
Multiplying it instead by $z^{41}$ gives

$$
e_0\equiv_*-11z^{42}u_s,
\tag{776.31}
$$

since $1-s^{11}=-zH_{11}(s)$ and the constant term of
$H_{11}(1+z)$ is 11.
Next apply $x^{41}$ to the third relation in (776.30).
The terms containing p times a positive derivative vanish,
and the top corner just shown to be zero removes the
$22x^{42}$ term. The result is

$$
x^{42}y\equiv_*a e_1.
\tag{776.32}
$$

The last norm relation in (776.30) now gives the precise
lift of the mod-p corner relation:

$$
\boxed{
p(y-u_s)\equiv_*-\frac1{11}e_0-ae_1.
}
\tag{776.33}
$$

This is the ordinary p-multiple which would be lost by
working only in $E_V/pE_V$.

From (776.24),

$$
D_*e_u=-8(e_1+i e_0+i x^{40}z^{43}c+x^{43}z^{40}c)
\equiv_*-8(e_1+i e_0).
$$

Since $a\equiv-4i$ and $1/11\equiv4\pmod{43}$,
the difference between $ai$ and $1/11$ is divisible by p.
Its multiple of $e_0\in\mathfrak aE_V$ is zero in the
current quotient. Therefore (776.33) proves

$$
D_*e_u-\frac8a\,p(y-u_s)\in p\mathfrak aE_V.
$$

The numerator $D_*e_u$ is in $pE_V$ by R773's exact
mod-p derivative equations. Since the ordinary lattice is
p-torsion-free, the last inclusion can be divided by p:

$$
\frac{D_*e_u}{p}
\equiv\frac8a(y-u_s)\pmod{\mathfrak aE_V}.
\tag{776.34}
$$

Apply the actual phase map (776.7). Because
$8/a\equiv2i$ and $r_\mu(y-u_s)=9b$, this yields

$$
\boxed{
\mathcal R(e_u)=2i\cdot9b=18ib.
}
\tag{776.35}
$$

No q-torsion enumeration or new numerical computation is
needed for this value. It follows from the exact ordinary
relations and the previously proved marked generator phases.

## 7. Evaluation on the genuine class and all remaining choices

R773 proves, for the actual restriction $e_V$,

$$
U_V=\frac4C n+\eta u,\qquad C=9ib\ne0,
$$
$$
e_V=\frac4C e_n+\eta e_u+A n+B u
\quad(A,B\in k).
\tag{776.36}
$$

This describes the ordinary solution containing the genuine
restricted primitive; it does not enlarge the rational
supported ambiguity. The linear functional (776.12)
annihilates both of the displayed invariant directions by
(776.28)--(776.29). Thus it is unnecessary to choose A or B,
and (776.27), (776.35) give

$$
\boxed{\mathcal R(e_V)=18ib\eta.}
\tag{776.37}
$$

Combining (776.37) with the genuine cochain comparison
(776.23) proves (776.1).

Here are the choice checks at the exact levels where they
are needed. Changing an ordinary representative e by $pv$
changes the division phase by $r_\mu(D_*v)=0$.
Changing Z changes the two divisions by $-2iv$ and $2v$,
which cancel. Changing a global p-th root changes the
Kummer cochain by a coboundary; the quadratic q restriction
of that coboundary is zero. Changing the local killing
zero-cochain adds $y_{\rm dir}k_q$, whose combination in
(776.22) is zero. Finally any change of the genuine supported
primitive has restriction in the specified space
$\mathbf F_p d_0+\mathbf F_p d_q$, hence in the invariant
span of u and n. Its contribution to (776.12) is zero.

The stronger annihilation of the whole k-span of n and u
is an explicitly proved phase identity. It is not used to
claim that this larger k-span consists of allowed rational
primitive changes.

## 8. Rational descent, the true flat correction, and the known reference

The original trace invariant $I_\alpha/\varepsilon$ belongs
to $\mathbf F_{43}$. Substitute
$\eta=\eta_R+i\eta_I$ into (776.1):

$$
1+i-18ib\eta
=1+18b\eta_I+i(1-18b\eta_R).
$$

The imaginary coefficient must vanish. Because $b\ne0$,
this proves $18b\eta_R=1$. The real coefficient, using
$32\cdot18\equiv17\pmod{43}$, proves (776.2).

The original correction is fixed by R185/R743:

$$
A_{\rm fl}=A_\alpha-\frac{8i}{1848}u,\qquad
d_{\rm fl}=d_q-8d_0.
$$

Because $1848\equiv-1\pmod{43}$, it changes the actual
ordinary coordinate by $\eta\mapsto\eta+8i$, exactly as
in R773. The local class of the target at q remains the
same, so the quadratic constant in (776.23) remains $1+i$.
Consequently

$$
\frac{I_0-I_\alpha}{\varepsilon}
=-32\cdot18ib\cdot8i=7b
\quad\text{in }\mathbf F_{43}.
\tag{776.38}
$$

This is the identical correction of R755, now obtained from
the actual ordinary division cochain.

There is also a separate reference check. For the target u,
the local Kummer class at q is zero. The quadratic and
diagonal constants in §4 then vanish, and the same root
calculation gives

$$
I(u)/\varepsilon=-32\,\mathcal R(e_u)=-32\cdot18ib
=26ib.
$$

The original reference is $d_0=(i/1848)u=-iu$ modulo p.
By linearity its invariant is $26b$, agreeing exactly with
R755's $9-(9+17b)=26b$ and R749's actual scalar reference.
Thus neither the sign of the diagonal homotopy nor the
normalization of the ordinary phase is being calibrated
by a newly selected root or by an unknown scalar.

Finally insert R755's already proved identity

$$
I_\alpha/\varepsilon=21\sigma_2+2bJ_\ell.
$$

The exact remaining exchange can now be written

$$
\boxed{
21\sigma_2+2bJ_\ell=32+17b\eta_I,
}
\tag{776.39}
$$
$$
\boxed{
21\sigma_2+2b(J_\ell-18)=32+17b(\eta_I+8).
}
\tag{776.40}
$$

The original rational cochain, the original ordinary integral
lattice, and the original first-moment normalization appear in
these same equalities.

## 9. Boundary cases and the remaining arithmetic input

If $b=0$, R768 does not assert the same ordinary scalar-layer
descent, and R773's conditional lattice generation is not used.
No division by b or C in this result is asserted in that case.
The original obstruction is already
$\Omega_2=22\varepsilon\ne0$ when $b=0$.

At the remaining comparison value $b=24$, the proved statements
specialize to

$$
\eta_R=22,\qquad
I_\alpha/\varepsilon=32+21\eta_I,\qquad
I_0/\varepsilon=28+21\eta_I,
$$
$$
5(J_\ell-18)=28+21(\eta_I-\sigma_2).
\tag{776.41}
$$

There is no contradiction in these identities alone.
They identify the exact remaining scalar combination, but
do not supply its value. In particular a direct zero phase
of the ordinary restricted unit was not used as a zero
regularized pairing: the retained quadratic constant and
the ordinary p-division in (776.23) are essential.

The next arithmetic input must determine the actual remaining
coordinate of $A_\alpha$ relative to the specified mixed
Robert unit, or compare that coordinate with the actual second
scalar action coefficient. Neither the abstract socle, the
known scalar reference, nor a free local root specifies it.
The full J-to-b conclusion reserved for R723 is not claimed.
