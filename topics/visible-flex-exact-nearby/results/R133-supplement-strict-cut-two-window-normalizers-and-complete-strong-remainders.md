## 6. The complete strong remainder of the inverse

The same ordered operators have the exact identity
$$
 \mathcal N-(1-A)
 =\bigl[A^2-(1+B)^{-1}B\bigr](1+A)^{-1}.
 \tag{TN.17}
$$
Indeed $(1+A)^{-1}=1-A+A^2(1+A)^{-1}$ and
$(1+B)^{-1}-1=-(1+B)^{-1}B$. No commutation of $A$ with $B$ is used.

Let $\gamma_J=\Lambda_J/(4J)$. The primitive gains one angular derivative on the high space with norm at most $\gamma_J$. In $A^2$, its two occurrences are separated by complete multipliers, so the relevant intermediate windows are $-1/3$ and $2/3$. Consequently
$$
 \|A^2\|_{H^{-4/3}\to L^2}
 \le \gamma_J^2\Lambda_J^{-2/3}
         \mathfrak m_{S,2/3}\mathfrak m_{S,-1/3},
$$
$$
 \|B\|_{H^{-4/3}\to L^2}
 \le\gamma_J^2\Lambda_J^{-2/3}\mathfrak m_{2,2/3}.
 \tag{TN.18}
$$
The last factor $\Lambda_J^{-2/3}$ is the high output embedding from $H^{2/3}$ to $L^2$. It is not an extra gain assumed for either multiplier.

Combining (TN.17)--(TN.18) with the inverse on its required side gives
$$
 \boxed{\quad
 \|\mathcal N-(1-A)\|_{H^{-4/3}\to L^2}
 \le
 C_{\mathcal N}:=
 \frac{\gamma_J^2\Lambda_J^{-2/3}}{1-\tau_{1,-4/3}}
 \left\{
   \mathfrak m_{S,2/3}\mathfrak m_{S,-1/3}
   +\frac{\mathfrak m_{2,2/3}}{1-\tau_{2,0}}
 \right\}.
 \quad}\tag{TN.19}
$$
The right inverse is charged in the negative input window; the left inverse multiplying $B$ is charged on the $L^2$ output. This is a complete two-gain calculation followed by the declared embedding.

The forward map also has a complete first order expansion,
$$
 T_1T_2=1+A+(1+A)B,
$$
$$
 \|T_1T_2-(1+A)\|_{H^{-4/3}\to L^2}
 \le(1+\tau_{1,0})\gamma_J^2\Lambda_J^{-2/3}
                                   \mathfrak m_{2,2/3}.
 \tag{TN.20}
$$
Both remainders vanish on the finite input complement, including the means. This fact describes these coordinate maps; it does not remove any finite row of the transformed evolution.
