## 3. Transported finite rows and the old low head

Let $Q_L$ be an actual finite orthogonal Fourier projection on the original input circle, and let
$$
 E_L:\mathbb C^N\longrightarrow\operatorname{ran}Q_L,
 \qquad E_LE_L^*=Q_L
 \tag{BF.4}
$$
be its normalized Fourier coordinate map. Fix the original right factor
$$
 R=T_F^{-1}\mathcal S^{-1},
 \qquad
 \|R:H^{-4/3}\to H^{-4/3}\|
       \le K_R:=K_{F,-4/3}K_{\mathcal S^{-1},-4/3}.
 \tag{BF.5}
$$
This is the exact order in WY.37--38. Any other required sign-diagonal factor remains on its declared side; a low-mode extension of $\Lambda_\sigma^{-1}$ is not silently assigned by its high-mode formula.

If the actual old operator is defined on these finite columns, then
$$
 \boxed{\quad
 F_{\rm low}=A_{\rm old}Q_LR=L_{\rm low}V_{\rm low},
 \qquad L_{\rm low}=A_{\rm old}E_L,\quad
 V_{\rm low}=E_L^*R .
 \quad}
 \tag{BF.6}
$$
Every column $A_{\rm old}e_j$ is a genuine original column, including its root, waist and mean normalization. A theorem only defined on $P_{97}H^{-4/3}$ does not define these columns by itself.

The individual rows and the joint row operator satisfy
$$
 \begin{split}
 \|(V_{\rm low})_j\|_{(H^{-4/3})^*}
       &\le K_R\Lambda_j^{4/3},\\
 \|V_{\rm low}:H^{-4/3}\to\ell^2\|
       &\le K_R\max_{j\in L}\Lambda_j^{4/3}.
 \end{split}
 \tag{BF.7}
$$
To prove the first inequality, the dual row is $R^*e_j\in H^{4/3}$ and the adjoint of the same negative-window bound has norm $K_R$. To prove the second, use the finite diagonal embedding on $Q_L$ before applying BF.5. There is no additional $\sqrt N$ in this joint operator bound.

For the full complex Fourier head $|j|<97$, the maximum in BF.7 is $\Lambda_{96}^{4/3}$ and there are $193$ scalar Fourier slots. If the original realization excludes its auxiliary zero mode, use its actual $192$ nonzero slots and retain the separately defined physical means. This is a choice of actual coordinates, not permission to identify the two types of mean.

If complete column bounds $l_j\ge\|A_{\rm old}e_j\|_{\mathcal X^0}$ are supplied, one permitted whole bound is
$$
 \|F_{\rm low}:\mathcal X^{-4/3}\to\mathcal X^0\|
 \le
 \left(\sum_{j\in L}l_j^2\right)^{1/2}
 K_R\max_{j\in L}\Lambda_j^{4/3}.
 \tag{BF.8}
$$
The sum pays the entire finite column operator. A sharper actual finite matrix norm may replace this whole column bound. No current receipt is asserted here to contain the missing $l_j$.

Equations BF.5--7 first refer to the indicated scalar circle. In BF.8, $E_L$ is embedded into the declared circle component of $\mathcal X$, with the other physical mean coordinates zero, and $R$ means its specified actual extension. For the direct sum $R\oplus1_{\mathbb C^m}$ the complete bound is $\max(K_R,1)$ (equal to $K_R$ when $K_R\ge1$). Use that complete bound in BF.8. An arbitrary coupled matrix extension is not assigned the scalar bound by notation.

More generally, if $F_0=L_0V_0$ and the actual left/right maps $A,B$ are bounded on the relevant output/input windows, then
$$
 AF_0B=(AL_0)(V_0B).
 \tag{BF.9}
$$
Rank is preserved or decreased. Columns remain in the transformed finite span and rows remain in the transformed dual space. They need not have finite Fourier support in the new principal angle. In particular,
$$
 V_{\rm low}P_J=E_L^*T_F^{-1}\mathcal S^{-1}P_J
 \tag{BF.10}
$$
is not set to zero. The old head is not the head of the new principal angle.

The explicit IC ranks have the same factorization. With
$(u\otimes v)f=u\langle vf\rangle$, a row in $H^{4/3}$ and a column in $L^2$ give
$$
 \|u\otimes v:H^{-4/3}\to L^2\|
                  \le\|u\|_2\|v\|_{H^{4/3}}.
 \tag{BF.11}
$$
Use the appropriate Hermitian representative for the dual pairing. This keeps the complete original zero mode and both signs. Finite codomain without the stated dual membership is not a substitute for BF.11.

## 4. An exact split before any inverse test

The following is the required operator identity, on one complete square realization:
$$
 \boxed{\qquad
 \mathbb B=\mathbb T+\mathbb E+LV .
 \qquad}
 \tag{BF.12}
$$
Here:

- $\mathbb T$ is the actual principal factor, with the complete auxiliary scalar mean and identity on any separately declared physical mean block.
- $\mathbb T$ is a bounded isomorphism on both stated spaces, and the same $\mathbb T^{-1}$ is already established on $\mathcal X^0$ and $\mathcal X^{-4/3}$, with bounds $K_0,K_-$.
- $\mathbb E:\mathcal X^{-4/3}\to\mathcal X^0$ has a **complete** bound $C_E$.
- $L:\mathbb C^N\to\mathcal X^0$ and $V:\mathcal X^{-4/3}\to\mathbb C^N$ are genuine finite factors in the exact identity, with all their rows and columns specified.

For a single scalar IC principal factor one may use the MI inverse of $1+M_{c_i}I$. For a declared diagonal collection, use its complete matrix-space bounds. If an infinite Maslov or sign operator is retained in the principal factor instead, that different principal factor and its complete inverse must be identified; BF.12 does not assume it is the MI scalar factor.

The word “complete” for $\mathbb E$ is decisive. The WE paid bound may enter it unchanged, but every additional infinite retained operator assigned to $\mathbb E$ needs a same-object strong bound. An unproved opposite-sign or second-reference block cannot be placed in $LV$. Conversely, a leading operator not of strong order must remain in an appropriately defined principal relation, with its inversion requirements stated separately.

The split assigns each actual operator term exactly once. In the minimal use of this theorem, the entire already paid WE/CG/TF submap stays in $\mathbb E$; only additional genuinely finite retained terms enter $LV$. If one instead moves an already norm-paid finite term out of that submap, its remaining strong bound must be justified from the original operator decomposition. Subtracting a norm upper bound from the paid total is invalid.

WE gives individual wave-row constants in a stated two-sign convention. To use them for a larger Euclidean output, pay the complete square-sum row bound or a sharper proved matrix bound. A common numerical bound for each component is not automatically the same bound for the pair.

## 5. The common high inverse only uses the complete strong part

Set
$$
 A=\mathbb T^{-1}\mathbb E,\qquad U=\mathbb T^{-1}L,\qquad
 P=P_{\{|j|\ge J\}}\oplus0_{\mathbb C^m},\qquad Q=1-P .
 \tag{BF.13}
$$
Thus $Q$ includes every scalar zero mode and every independent physical mean. All finite matching coordinates stay in $Q$.

The full strong composition satisfies
$$
 \|A:\mathcal X^{-4/3}\to\mathcal X^0\|\le K_0C_E .
 \tag{BF.14}
$$
For the high block
$$
 H=1_{\operatorname{ran}P}+PAP,
 \qquad \chi=K_0C_E\Lambda_J^{-4/3},
 \tag{BF.15}
$$
the two bounds are
$$
 \|PAP:\mathcal X^0_P\to\mathcal X^0_P\|\le\chi,
 \qquad
 \|PAP:\mathcal X^{-4/3}_P\to\mathcal X^{-4/3}_P\|\le\chi .
 \tag{BF.16}
$$
The first uses the high-input embedding into $H^{-4/3}$. The second uses the high-output embedding from $L^2$. Both use the same $K_0$ from BF.14; $K_-$ is not incorrectly substituted into this strong chain.

If
$$
                              \chi<1,
 \tag{BF.17}
$$
then the Neumann inverse $H^{-1}$ exists in both windows with
$$
                       \|H^{-1}\|_s\le(1-\chi)^{-1}.
 \tag{BF.18}
$$
The partial sums are the same operators. On their common $L^2$ domain their limits agree in distributions, so these are two bounds for one inverse.

No size condition on $PUVP$ is used. It is retained in the finite auxiliary system below. The criterion also does not use the norm of an unpaid infinite remainder. If $C_E$ is still missing, BF.17 remains a conditional test.

For a logarithmic principal certificate $K_0\le e^{L_0}$ and $C_E>0$, the sufficient test can be stated without evaluating the exponential:
$$
             L_0+\log C_E-\frac43\log\Lambda_J<0 .
 \tag{BF.19}
$$
If $C_E=0$, the strong part vanishes and $H=1$. A formal existence of some finite $J$ does not certify a feasible numerical head, its matrix entries, or its inverse.

## 6. Exact augmented finite equation

Write the original equation as $\mathbb Bx=f$, and set
$$
 r=\mathbb T^{-1}f,\qquad p=Px,\quad q=Qx,\quad z=Vx .
 \tag{BF.20}
$$
Left multiplication by $\mathbb T^{-1}$ gives
$$
                       (1+A)x+Uz=r .
 \tag{BF.21}
$$
The high row is solved exactly by
$$
           p=H^{-1}\big(Pr-PAQq-PUz\big).
 \tag{BF.22}
$$
Substitute this into the low row and into the retained equation $z=V(p+q)$. The resulting finite matrix is
$$
 \boxed{
 \mathfrak S=
 \begin{pmatrix}
  1_Q+QAQ-QAPH^{-1}PAQ&
       QU-QAPH^{-1}PU\\
  -VQ+VPH^{-1}PAQ&
       1_{\mathbb C^N}+VPH^{-1}PU
 \end{pmatrix}.
 }
 \tag{BF.23}
$$
Its complete right-hand side is
$$
 \mathfrak S\binom{q}{z}=
 \binom{Qr-QAPH^{-1}Pr}{VPH^{-1}Pr}.
 \tag{BF.24}
$$
In particular the second right-hand component has the displayed positive sign.

All compositions have their original order. Neither $V$ nor a sign selector is moved through $P$, $H^{-1}$ or a true circle map. The variable $z$ keeps the whole finite map, including its high/high part. The matrix acts on
$\operatorname{ran}Q\oplus\mathbb C^N$; its dimension includes the complete principal head, all auxiliary and physical means, and the actual finite factor coordinates.

The entries in BF.23 are well defined in either window. The $L$ columns, their $U$ images, and the finite Fourier columns lie in $\mathcal X^0$. All rows of $V$ act on $\mathcal X^{-4/3}$. The same $H^{-1}$ is used, so BF.23 is one finite matrix, not two different numerical constructions.

A convenient exact inverse formula follows. Define
$$
 \begin{split}
 \Gamma(q,z)&=q-H^{-1}PAQq-H^{-1}PUz,\\
 \Delta r&=\binom{Qr-QAPH^{-1}Pr}{VPH^{-1}Pr}.
 \end{split}
 \tag{BF.25}
$$
If the actual finite matrix is invertible, then
$$
 \boxed{\quad
 \mathbb B^{-1}
  =\big[H^{-1}P+\Gamma\mathfrak S^{-1}\Delta\big]\mathbb T^{-1}.
 \quad}
 \tag{BF.26}
$$

To check equivalence, first introduce the exact augmented system
$$
 \begin{pmatrix}1+A&U\\-V&1\end{pmatrix}
                 \binom{x}{z}=\binom{r}{0}.
 \tag{BF.27}
$$
Eliminating its last row gives $1+A+UV=\mathbb T^{-1}\mathbb B$. Reordering into $p,q,z$ and eliminating $H$ gives BF.23. Both eliminations are invertible operations. Consequently, under BF.12 and BF.17, $\mathbb B$ is invertible if and only if the actual finite matrix $\mathfrak S$ is nonsingular. Redundant finite factor coordinates do not invalidate this equivalence.

The identity applies simultaneously to the two windows. For quantitative norm bounds, the finite head in BF.23 carries the inherited $\mathcal X^s$ weight and the auxiliary $z$ its stated Euclidean norm. Thus an unweighted Euclidean matrix bound is not silently reused for a differently weighted finite head. With actual bounds for these same finite maps,
$$
 \|\mathbb B^{-1}\|_s
 \le \big[(1-\chi)^{-1}
       +\|\Gamma\|_s\,\|\mathfrak S^{-1}\|_s\,\|\Delta\|_s\big]K_s,
 \qquad s=0,-4/3 .
 \tag{BF.28}
$$
The right-hand preconditioning uses $K_0$ or $K_-$ in its own window. The two independent physical mean equations are part of this finite system, not inferred from the means of $c_i,c_o$.
