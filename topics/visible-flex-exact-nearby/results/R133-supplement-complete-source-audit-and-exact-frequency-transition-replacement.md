## WTS3. Exact change of trial on the complete strict state

Let $U(r)$ be another declared piecewise differentiable trial on the same original input core and in the same full state coordinates. Its two physical rows must both be specified. Write
$$
 F_U=(\partial_r-G_D)U,\qquad j_{U,\ell}=[U]_\ell.
 \tag{WTS.9}
$$
Let $\chi_r(N)$ be an input Fourier diagonal depending only on $r$ and $n=4|j|$. Its values are real and equal on $j$ and $-j$. On the finite core assume piecewise differentiability. Define, with every diagonal on the right,
$$
 B(r)=(U(r)-V(r))\chi_r(N),\qquad
 V_\chi=V+B=V(Id-\chi)+U\chi .
 \tag{WTS.10}
$$
There is no commutation of $\chi$ through a coefficient, $J$, a primitive, $T_J$, $S$, the common-root normalizer or the actual input map. The exact source is
$$
 \boxed{\quad
 F_\chi=F(Id-\chi)+F_U\chi+(U-V)\partial_r\chi .
 \quad}
 \tag{WTS.11}
$$
This follows from the product rule and $G_D(U\chi)=(G_DU)\chi$. The latter is left action on an input operator, not a claim that $G_D$ and a Fourier multiplier commute on the output.

If $\chi$ is continuous at every join, the complete jump is
$$
 \boxed{\quad
 j_{\chi,\ell}=j_\ell(Id-\chi_\ell)+j_{U,\ell}\chi_\ell .
 \quad}
 \tag{WTS.12}
$$
If a discontinuous choice is made, the side convention is mandatory:
$$
 j_{\chi,\ell}
 =j_\ell(Id-\chi_-)+j_{U,\ell}\chi_-
                      +(U-V)_+[\chi]_\ell.
 \tag{WTS.13}
$$
Using $\chi_+$ in the first two terms instead requires $(U-V)_-$ in the last term. That new jump is never free. A smooth transition is the simpler exact construction.

The new cut discrepancy is
$$
 e_{\chi,c}=e_c-B(c).
 \tag{WTS.14}
$$
For the same fixed compressed waist target $V_J(0)$ the corresponding waist term is
$$
 e_{\chi,0}=V_\chi(0)-V_J(0)=e_0+B(0).
 \tag{WTS.15}
$$
Thus, including (WTS.15), the full represented error $Y_A-V_J(0)$ remains exactly unchanged for any such $B$. If one instead compares with the modified waist trial $V_J(0)+B(0)$, its error is exactly $E_*-B(0)$.

The particularly useful choice is $B(0)=0$. It preserves the original waist leading expression and its existing coefficient/rank ledger. It does not set $B(c)$ or the transition source to zero.

Here is the complete cancellation proof. On each piece,
$$
 \partial_r\{U_D(0,r)B(r)\}
      =U_D(0,r)(\partial_r-G_D)B(r).
$$
Consequently
$$
 \begin{aligned}
 \int_0^c U_D(0,r)(\partial_r-G_D)B\,dr
  ={}&U_D(0,c)B(c)-B(0)\\
     &-\sum_\ell U_D(0,r_\ell)[B]_\ell .

$$
The cut change $-U_D(0,c)B(c)$, the source change in (WTS.16), and the jump change $+\sum U_D[B]$ sum to $-B(0)$. Adding (WTS.15) restores the fixed waist target exactly. All signs use the same backward error convention as (WTS.7).

The identity includes the independent complete mean row: it is an identity in $\mathcal Y$, before applying $\mathcal R$. It does not require either trial to be reflected. The original $Q_R$ is applied to the complete output.

## WTS5. The fixed-cut Airy parameter and the genuine order requirement

On the real negative fold,

$$

$$

$$

 <\frac{388}{50000}<1 .
 \tag{WTS.20}
$$
This is an exact qualitative deduction from the displayed old enclosing record. It is not a recomputed Airy value, a new profile bound, or a rejection of FA. FA explicitly used a real remainder theorem valid for every positive argument and did not require $\zeta\ge1$. The observation explains why a far-field expansion can have a large but valid first-omitted coefficient at the lowest original input.

Suppose a proposed frequency cut has $\rho_n$ proportional to $n^{-2/3}$, with a proved real Langer comparison $-T\asymp\rho$ on its domain. Then $\zeta_n$ is of constant order. A fixed-order large-$\zeta$ expansion alone has no frequency-decaying omitted factor there. In particular a bounded value of $1/(n\rho_n^{3/2})$ is not an $n^{-1}$ gain.

The transition derivative has no automatic compensating frequency gain either. For (WTS.17),
$$

 \qquad

on its full transition interval. The width cancels the derivative. The gain in the third term of (WTS.11) must come from the actual complete matching difference $U-V$, or from a proved cancellation in the entire received tuple.

For a more general choice $\rho_n\asymp n^{-\alpha}$, with $0\le\alpha<2/3$, an omitted relative asymptotic term $\zeta^{-m}$ has the formal frequency exponent

This exponent is useful only after the relevant expansion and its full remainder domain have been proved. If the actual reconstructed matching estimate has an additional prefactor $n^\kappa\rho^{-\beta}$, the full conditional exponent is instead

These statements neither differentiate a saved Airy error ball nor treat asymptotic notation as an all-frequency remainder theorem.

## WTS6. Complete strong-gain tests with no assumed output orthogonality

The following criteria apply to the **whole received error**, including (WTS.7) or its exact replacement. They do not infer gain from separate upper records.

Choose a unit $L^2$ basis $\varphi_j$ of the original reflected natural input, supported in the pair of modes $\pm j$, and write
$$
 \Lambda_j=(1+16j^2)^{1/2},\qquad n_j=4j,\qquad j\ge97.
$$
All physical output rows and signs must be included in the column norm. If a complete operator $E$ satisfies
$$
 \sum_{j\ge97}\Lambda_j^{8/3}\|E\varphi_j\|_{L^2}^2<\infty,
 \tag{WTS.24}
$$
then
$$
 \boxed{\quad
 \|E:H^{-4/3}_{\rm nat,97}\to L^2\|
 \le
 \left(\sum_{j\ge97}
       \Lambda_j^{8/3}\|E\varphi_j\|_{L^2}^2\right)^{1/2}.
 \quad}
 \tag{WTS.25}
$$
Indeed $\Lambda_j^{4/3}\varphi_j$ is an orthonormal input basis for the indicated negative norm, so the right side is the Hilbert--Schmidt norm. No output Fourier orthogonality of different rays is assumed.

In particular a uniform complete column estimate
$\|E\varphi_j\|\le Cn_j^{-p}$ is sufficient for this direct test only when
$$
 p>\frac{11}{6},
 \tag{WTS.26}
$$
because the comparison exponent is $8/3-2p<-1$. A complete $n^{-2}$ column estimate is sufficient. A bounded or first-order transition mismatch does not pass this test merely because each finite-frequency problem is meaningful.

A sharper block criterion can be used when a true block operator estimate is available. Let $P_k$ be disjoint original natural input bands
$2^kN_*\le n<2^{k+1}N_*$, with $N_*=388$. Suppose
$$
 \|EP_k:L^2\to L^2\|\le\varepsilon_k .
$$
Then
$$
 \boxed{\quad
 \|E:H^{-4/3}\to L^2\|
 \le
 \left[
   \sum_{k\ge0}
      (1+(2^{k+1}N_*)^2)^{4/3}\varepsilon_k^2
 \right]^{1/2}
 \quad}
 \tag{WTS.27}
$$
whenever this series converges. Apply the triangle inequality to
$\sum EP_kf$, then Cauchy--Schwarz with the orthogonal negative input bands. This needs no output almost-orthogonality.

For example $\varepsilon_k\le C(2^kN_*)^{-p}$ passes this block test for $p>4/3$. The borderline $p=4/3$ does not follow from (WTS.27); an additional full operator theorem would be needed. Neither the block estimate itself nor output almost-orthogonality is supplied by isolated column estimates.

The actual selector belongs before any later input coordinate change:
$$
 (E\chi_r(N))N_i
 \tag{WTS.28}
$$
keeps that order. One cannot replace it by $EN_i\chi_r(N)$ or reinterpret $P_k$ as the transported final Fourier projector. A complete bound on the natural input may subsequently be composed with the admitted $N_i$ in its stated negative window, exactly once.
