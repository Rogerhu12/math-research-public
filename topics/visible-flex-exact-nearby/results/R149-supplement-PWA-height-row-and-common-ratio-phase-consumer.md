## PWA96.3. One shared ratio, two consistent complete row objects

From (PWA96.1--2) compute the whole directed signed interval

$$
\rho=\frac{c_P}{c_H}<0.                              \tag{PWA96.5}
$$

This scalar is common to all96 columns. Save and read it back
before computing any dependent target. Let $r$ be one exactly
recorded dyadic midpoint choice, $R=\sup|\rho|$ and
$\Delta=\sup|\rho-r|$. These are bounds for the same actual ratio;
no column is separately normalized.

The complete reduced phase row has signed enclosures

$$
S_j=P_j-\rho\mathcal H_j,
\qquad s_j=p_j-rh_j.                                  \tag{PWA96.6}
$$

Every $s_j$ is an exact dyadic, since $p_j,r,h_j$ are exact dyadics.
The complete vector of96 such centres is recorded, independently
of how the signed output balls are printed. Its joint radius obeys

$$
\boxed{\quad E_S=E_P+R E_H+\Delta\|h\|_{\ell^2}.\quad}
\tag{PWA96.7}
$$

Indeed

$$
S-s=(P-p)-\rho(\mathcal H-h)-(\rho-r)h.
$$

The triangle inequality proves (PWA96.7) without an independence
assumption. It preserves the shared scalar and uses the whole saved
P96 joint certificate, the whole new saved height radius and the
whole saved ratio. The signed component boxes and the joint ball
are simultaneous enclosures of the same true row. The joint radius
is certified by this identity, not by replacing all entries with
their printed component midpoints or by asserting that the entire
Cartesian product of the component boxes lies in the joint ball.
Serialization of those looser component boxes therefore cannot
silently change the exact centre vector in (PWA96.6).

This is the original height-first row $\ell-\rho\mathcal H$ of
