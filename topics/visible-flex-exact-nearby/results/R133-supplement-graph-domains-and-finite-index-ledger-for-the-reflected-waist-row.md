## WFI3. The graph domain and its complete reference inverse

Define the full nonmean graph domain
$$
 \mathcal D
    =\{x\in X_b^{-1}:\overline Mx\in Y_a^0\},
 \qquad
 \|x\|_{\mathcal D}
    =\left(\|x\|_{H^{-1}}^2+
                    \|\overline Mx\|_2^2\right)^{1/2}.
 \tag{WFI.13}
$$
The distributional equality in this definition is unambiguous because $\overline M:X_b^{-1}\to Y_a^{-1}$ is bounded.

The graph is closed. Indeed, if $x_\ell\to x$ in $H^{-1}$ and $\overline Mx_\ell\to y$ in $L^2$, then the second convergence also holds in $H^{-1}$. Continuity of $\overline M$ on $H^{-1}$ gives $\overline Mx=y$. Consequently $\mathcal D$ is a Hilbert space with its graph norm.

The same set and an equivalent graph norm result from using $M_\nu$ in place of $\overline M$. This follows from the finite bound (WFI.12):
$$
 \mathcal D
    =\{x\in X_b^{-1}:M_\nu x\in Y_a^0\}.
 \tag{WFI.14}
$$
Thus the low reference does not enlarge the actual model's graph domain, even if an untested low multiplier vanishes.

For $y\in Y_a^0$, split its ordinary low and high parts and set
$$
 \overline G y
    =G_{\nu,97}P_{97}y
        +L_{\rm low}^{-1}Q_{97}y .
 \tag{WFI.15}
$$
The high part lies in $H^{-1}$ by (WFI.9); the low part is smooth. Equation (WFI.11) gives $\overline M\overline Gy=y$, so $\overline Gy\in\mathcal D$. Conversely every Fourier reflection line of $\overline M$ has a nonzero multiplier, by (WFI.9) on the high part and by (WFI.10) on the low part. Hence
$$
 \boxed{\quad
 \overline M:\mathcal D\longrightarrow Y_a^0
       \text{ is a bounded isomorphism, with inverse }\overline G .
 \quad}
 \tag{WFI.16}
$$
For example its $L^2\to H^{-1}$ bound can be kept symbolically as
$\max\{1,2K_{\rm CY}\}$; its graph bound follows by adding the exact output norm. No numerical product is evaluated.

The particular regularity fact needed for the finite complement is
$$
 X_b^0\subset\mathcal D
       \quad\hbox{continuously}.
 \tag{WFI.17}
$$
This is proved by the bounded map $\overline M:X_b^0\to Y_a^0$ from (WFI.11):
$\|x\|_{\mathcal D}\le(1+\|\overline M\|_{L^2\to L^2})\|x\|_2$ is one safe bound. It is not inferred from the inclusion $L^2\subset H^{-1}$ alone. The forward model is still not claimed bounded $H^{-1}\to L^2$ on its whole ambient space.

Ordinary frequency truncations are bounded on $\mathcal D$ and converge there. They commute with $\overline M$ and converge both in $H^{-1}$ and on the $L^2$ output. In particular finite real reflection sums are a dense graph core for (WFI.16).

## WFI4. The actual finite complement is bounded on this graph

All coordinate maps in (WFI.3), their inverses, and the fixed unitary $D_+$ are compatible at $s=0,-4/3$ as the same distributional maps. Interpolation therefore makes them bounded at $s=-1$. This uses no new evaluated interpolation constant or parameter-continuity assertion.

$$
 \mathcal U=\mathcal A^{-1}\mathcal C D_+
   :\operatorname{Fix}(R_*)\cap H^s_0
           \longrightarrow X_b^s
 \quad(s=0,-1,-4/3)
 \tag{WFI.18}
$$
is a real bounded isomorphism with its original ordered inverse. This is an intertwining identity. It does not say that $\mathcal C$ commutes with $R_b$, or that any of these maps preserves individual frequencies.

Since $D_+$ commutes with $Q_{97}$, the actual complement from (WFI.4) can be written, on the reflected carrier, as
$$
 \widetilde Q_{97}=Id-\widetilde P_{97}
    =\mathcal U Q_{97}\mathcal U^{-1}.
 \tag{WFI.19}
$$
Choose an orthonormal real basis $e_1,\ldots,e_{96}$ of the original root-even modes $1\le|j|\le96$. Define
$$
 c_n=\mathcal Ue_n,\qquad
 \ell_n(x)=\langle e_n,\mathcal U^{-1}x\rangle,\qquad
 \widetilde Q_{97}x=\sum_{n=1}^{96}c_n\ell_n(x).
 \tag{WFI.20}
$$
For a distributional input the displayed row is the dual pairing with a smooth finite Fourier function after the bounded $H^{-1}$ map $\mathcal U^{-1}$. It is not an unqualified $L^2$ pairing on two rough functions.

Each $c_n$ belongs to $X_b^0$ by the proved $L^2$ bounds, and hence belongs to $\mathcal D$ by (WFI.17). Each $\ell_n$ is bounded on $X_b^{-1}$ because $\mathcal U^{-1}$ is bounded there and evaluation of a fixed finite Fourier coefficient is continuous in $H^{-1}$. Thus
$$
 \|\widetilde Q_{97}x\|_{\mathcal D}
     \le\sum_{n=1}^{96}
              \|c_n\|_{\mathcal D}\|\ell_n\|_{(H^{-1})^*}
                                      \|x\|_{H^{-1}},
 \qquad x\in\mathcal D .
 \tag{WFI.21}
$$
This proves that $\widetilde Q_{97}$ is a bounded finite-rank projection on $\mathcal D$. Its rank is 96 there: its full range is spanned by the 96 independent columns in (WFI.20), all of which have just been shown to lie in $\mathcal D$, and $\ell_n(c_m)=\delta_{nm}$.

Consequently the exact high graph domain is
$$
 \mathcal D_{\rm true}
    =\mathcal D\cap\widetilde X_{-1}
    =\widetilde P_{97}\mathcal D,
$$
$$
 \mathcal D
    =\mathcal D_{\rm true}
           \mathbin{\oplus}\operatorname{ran}(\widetilde Q_{97}),
 \qquad
 \operatorname{codim}_{\mathcal D}\mathcal D_{\rm true}=96 .
 \tag{WFI.22}
$$
Both projections in this graph decomposition are bounded. No commutation of $\overline M$ with $\widetilde P_{97}$ has been used. Such a commutation is generally unavailable.

The rank 96 is the real reflected rank, and also the complex dimension of its complexification. The full nonmean complex carrier has rank 192; that is a different ambient count, not a second factor in (WFI.22). The columns in (WFI.20) are actual coordinate columns and need not have finite Fourier support.

 \tag{WFI.23}

$$

 \tag{WFI.25}

Indeed bounded graph sets are bounded in $H^{-1}$, and on the compact circle

          \quad\hbox{is a compact embedding}.

The limit of any convergent subsequence remains in $\widetilde X_{-4/3}$, since its defining reflection and projection are bounded at that index. Moreover $(M_\nu-\overline M)|_{\mathcal D_{\rm true}}$ is finite rank by (WFI.12). Therefore

\quad\hbox{is a compact perturbation on }\mathcal D_{\rm true}.

Every original mean rank contained in the complete error remains in the bracket. Compactness does not authorize deleting it from an actual equation or a later finite matrix.

## WFI6. The index and the distinct high/low ledgers

The full reference (WFI.16) is an isomorphism. Its restriction to the closed codimension-96 subspace in (WFI.22) is injective, has closed range of codimension 96, and has index $-96$. Fredholm stability under compact perturbations in (WFI.26) proves
$$
 \boxed{\quad
 \mathcal L:\mathcal D_{\rm true}\longrightarrow Y_a^0
     \text{ is Fredholm},\qquad
 \operatorname{ind}\mathcal L=-96 .
 \quad}
 \tag{WFI.27}
$$
Equivalently, the cokernel dimension is the kernel dimension plus 96. This is a count for the specified homogeneous high-input primitive row with its whole target. It is not an injectivity or surjectivity result.

 \tag{WFI.28}

These are 96 actual low output equations. They are not the artificial equations of $L_{\rm low}$ and are not identically zero because the original input cutoff was high. They include the actual cross maps of the complete row.

For the fifth row, if genuine physical columns $\ell^{\rm phys}_1,\ldots,\ell^{\rm phys}_{96}\in Y_a^0$ are supplied, write

It does not follow that those columns span the actual cokernel or that the augmented kernel is zero. The required actual pairings and finite determinant remain separate. ## WFI7. Coordinate invariance, complete physical rows and the remaining system

The source change $\mathcal W^{-1}\mathcal A$ is an isomorphism on $H^{-1}$ by the same two-window interpolation used above. The target map $\mathcal B^{-1}$ is an $L^2$ isomorphism on $Y_a^0$. Transport the actual graph domain by these exact changes rather than presuming that an arbitrary source map preserves $\mathcal D$. Equation (WFI.5) then gives a graph-norm isomorphism between the two physical-row realizations. This is the usual invariance of the Fredholm index under bounded isomorphisms of the declared domain and target.

Likewise the nonzero original factor $2\kappa$ and the already qualified true target coordinate can be restored with their complete inverse maps. They do not change the index of this same homogeneous primitive trace row. They do change its numerical norms and the definitions of every coupled row and column, so those factors cannot be dropped in a quantitative or bordered problem.

The index zero in a high-target comparison is not the index of the entire boundary problem. The latter still requires its actual finite rows and independent columns, with their boundedness on the chosen graph spaces. A bounded addition of $p$ finite input coordinates and $q$ finite output equations changes this row's index by $p-q$; it does not by itself prove compatibility or invertibility.

In particular, adjoining one genuine metric-parameter column and one genuinely independent scalar equation has net index change zero only after both are realized as bounded maps on these spaces. It cannot by itself cancel the 96-dimensional ledger in (WFI.27). The two stored physical mean slots, their reflected rank-one relation where applicable, the even-mean equation, waist amplitude, exterior cap constraints and independent source ports retain their original roles. A radial mean function or an independent radial head space is not a single finite coordinate unless a separate exact elimination has proved that fact.

No radially varying integral of finite-rank maps is reclassified here as one finite-rank operator. The full such contributions already in the bounded strong error are used in (WFI.25) as complete compact trace contributions, with their equations and actual values unchanged.

The conclusion is at the fixed selected parameter with the same full WPL/WBP trace qualification. It assigns no operator-norm continuity to the true Riccati map, no uniform selected chart to all real $\delta$, no all-$s$ error bounds, no background tame estimate and no nonlinear inverse. For a different Sobolev target or another parameter realization, its corresponding complete strong trace bound and compatible graph maps must first be supplied.
