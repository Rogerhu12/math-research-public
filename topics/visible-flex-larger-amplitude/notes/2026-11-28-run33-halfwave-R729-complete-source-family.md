## 3. Why the same kernel applies to this physical family

The kernel operates on the actual complete common-slack linearized problem after the original QPv3 attempt. It does not operate on a seed formula, a normal chart, a sampled mesh subset, an approximation to a derivative, or a hard-coded physical model.

The class retains every original selected physical row, its original gradient-coordinate norm, positive row-normalization scale and physical g. It supplies every original physical column and the complete slack column through full-row contiguous column slices. Its augmented constraints are

$$
u_i z-\beta_i t\le b_i,\qquad \beta_i>0,
$$

including the original nonnegative-total-slack row. It neither deletes dependent constraints nor substitutes a chosen independent face.

The restoration objective remains

$$
\frac12(\tau_0+t)^2+\frac12\mu^2(\|z\|^2+t^2),
\qquad H=\operatorname{diag}(\mu^2I,1+\mu^2).
$$

Here z is the complete original coordinate-space vector. The physical norm/preconditioner and original coordinate lift are not replaced by an unweighted norm in another space.

For the actual full C, full bounds and positive damping, R729 uses

$$
G=CH^{-1}C^{\mathsf T},\quad
r=b+CH^{-1}q,\quad q=\tau_0 e_{\rm slack},
$$

and nonnegative dual multipliers. Its original-rows, positive-slack and finite-source conditions are read from the actual problem object; no assumption about the run28 or R728 physical profile is needed to transfer the kernel's algebra. The complete proof explicitly permits singular G and dependent rows.
