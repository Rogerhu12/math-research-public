# R745. Equal polar meridians and a complete transverse Jacobi return on a thick bent-disk sphere

This is a finite closed \(C^2\) family for \(0\le t\le\pi\) with all-phase reach at least \(0.108\), \(D<3.45\), a single pointwise fixed upper grip of diameter at least \(0.75\), \(\delta_{\rm hold}\ge0.717\), and \(\delta_{\rm E}>0\). The two reflected meridians from the upper polar center to the equator have equal arclength. Their actual intrinsic curvatures give the same transverse Jacobi field, with the correct value and derivative through both poles and around the entire closed symmetry geodesic. These conditions do not solve the full two-dimensional fixed-metric equations. The three fixed cubic marks have height \(0.002\), which also gives the upper bound \(\delta_{\rm E}\le0.002\).

[The physical generator](../scripts/resume_20261126/r745_equal_meridian_jacobi_two_jets.py) solves the actual smoothed length equation and provides all six jets on the cylinder, both heads and both flat caps, on the material disks, and for free additional physical cylinder corrections. The bounds below apply to the exact integral definitions; quadrature and root finding in the generator approximate them.

## 1. A finite equal-length tilt

Use the arclength-parametrized planar strong disk \(C(x)=(u(x),H+v(x))\), \(-1\le x\le1\), of R740, with \(H=.15,\ h=.30,\ b=.26\) and its original curvature ramps. Its tangent is \((\cos\beta,\sin\beta)\), \(0\le\beta\le\pi/2\). It is horizontal until \(x=h\) and vertical past \(x=h+b\). Its endpoints are \(B=(-1,H)\), \(P=(.30+q,H+.44+q)\), with \(.13\le q<.184\) by R740 or R744 §1. Work in physical \(xz\)-coordinates and set
\[
 c=(.595,0),\quad e=(\cos\gamma,\sin\gamma),\quad n_0=(-\sin\gamma,\cos\gamma),\quad \Pi=c+\mathbb R e .
\]
Extend \(\Pi\) parallel to the physical \(y\)-axis in space. At \(B\) continue outward with tangent \((-\cos\tau,-\sin\tau)\) through \(a_L=\pi/2+\gamma\); at \(P\) use \((\sin\tau,\cos\tau)\) through \(a_R=\pi-\gamma\). On each turn use radius \(r=.15\), ramp length \(d=.001\) and the \(S_5\) curvature rule of R744: rise from zero to \(1/r\) in length \(d\), stay constant in length \(r a-d\), decrease symmetrically in length \(d\). Total length is \(r a+d\), total turn \(a\). Both terminal tangents equal \(-n_0\). From each turn end follow this direction to \(\Pi\). Reflect the whole upper curve in \(\Pi\) and traverse the reflection in reverse.

Write \(V(p)=(p-c)\cdot n_0\). At a given angle the actual turn position is within \(d\) of the corresponding circle: the measure \(ds-r\,d\tau\) is nonnegative with total mass \(d\). The comparison left and right wall heights are respectively \(V(B)-r\cos\gamma\) and \(V(P)-r\sin\gamma\), obtained by integrating the two tangents. For \(.47\le\gamma\le.50\), the disk has \(V>.18\): on the curved/tongue part use \(.15\cos\gamma+.111\sin\gamma\), and on the flat part the bound is larger. The left turn starts at \(V(B)>.84\) and has length \(<.312\); the right starts at \(V(P)>.68\) and has length \(<.403\). Both walls are positive and the nonwall upper pieces stay above \(\Pi\).

Subtract the arclength from the upper disk center to the left equator from that to the right equator. The unit disk radii cancel and the result is
\[
 \Delta=.15(\pi/2-2\gamma)+(.59+q)\cos\gamma-(1.45+q)\sin\gamma+\zeta,\qquad |\zeta|\le.002 .
 \tag{1}
\]
At \(.47\) it is positive even with \(q=.13,\zeta=-.002\); at \(.50\) it is negative even with \(q=.184,\zeta=.002\). Here \(.8915<\cos(.47)<.8916,\ .4528<\sin(.47)<.4530,\ .8775<\cos(.50)<.8777,\ .4794<\sin(.50)<.4795\) follow from the Taylor alternating bounds. All integrals depend continuously on \(\gamma\). The root in this interval is unique: each turn remainder splits over its two ramps, of excess mass \(d/2\) each. Their shape relative to each endpoint tangent is independent of total angle. Only the final tangent rotates with \(\gamma\), at angular speed one in magnitude. Differentiating a turn remainder has norm at most \(d/2\), and differentiating the normal in its projection contributes at most \(d\). Hence \(|\zeta'|\le3d\), while the derivative of the main expression is \(-.30-(.59+q)\sin\gamma-(1.45+q)\cos\gamma<-1.6\). Use this exact root and denote the common length by \(\ell\).

## 2. Prescribing an entire physical Jacobi return

Orient the closed section \(\Gamma(s)\) by increasing arclength up the left wall and through the upper disk in increasing \(x\), as in R744. Write \(T=\Gamma'\), \(n=(-T_z,T_x)\), \(T'=-kn,\ n'=kT\). On the strong disk \(k=-\beta'\), so \(|k|<6.91\). On the added turns \(0\le k\le1/.15\). Use \(y\in[-1,1]\) on its extrusion. Let \(a\) be outward arclength into either added turn measured from its disk endpoint. Define \(w\ge0\) on \([.02,.28]\) by increasing from zero to \(25/6\) with \(S_7(z)=35z^4-84z^5+70z^6-20z^7\) on \([.02,.04]\), holding constant through \(.26\), and decreasing with the reflected ramp through \(.28\). Extend by zero. It is \(C^3\) and \(\int w=(25/6)(.24)=1\). Both turns have \(k=1/.15\) on its entire support.

Let \(\rho\) be arclength along either outward symmetry ray from the upper pole. Set \(j(\rho)=\rho\) up to radius one, and prescribe
\[
 j(1+a)=1+a-\int_0^a(a-v)w(v)\,dv,\qquad A(a)=-.15\,w(a)/j(1+a).
 \tag{2}
\]
Beyond the disk \(j\ge1,\ 0\le j'\le1,\ j''=-w\); \(j\) is constant from \(a=.28\) onwards. Set \(A=0\) off those turning subarcs and extend by reflection to the lower half. With \(d_y=1/1024\), replace the physical extrusion by
\[
 F(s,y)=(\Gamma(s)+f(s,y)n(s),\,y),\quad
 f=A(a)b(y),\qquad b(y)=\frac{y^2(1-y^2)_+^4}{2(1+y^2/d_y^2)^2}.
 \tag{3}
\]
The displayed components are planar-plus-transverse. Equations (2)–(3) leave the material disks intact and meet the original ends through three jets at \(|y|=1\). The symmetry geodesic \(y=0\) retains its actual positions and arclength. Here \(b=b'=0,\ b''=1\); the induced metric in the two coordinate directions is the identity and the scalar second form is \(\operatorname{diag}(-k,A)\). The actual intrinsic curvature on the geodesic is
\[
 K=-kA=w/j . \tag{4}
\]
Elsewhere on the geodesic outside these corrections the extrusion is intrinsically flat. The spatial vector \(e_y\) is a parallel transverse tangent along it. Thus \(j''+Kj=0\) with the polar rotation initial data \(j(0)=0,\ j'(0)=1\). Both directions give the same function through the common equator at \(\ell\). Reflect it evenly about \(\ell\), where it is constant on an open interval. Then it stays positive up to the lower pole at \(2\ell\) and there has data \((0,-1)\). Extend with opposite sign along the other meridian of length \(2\ell\) and identical curvature profile. The full signed \(4\ell\)-periodic Jacobi solution returns to \((0,1)\). This is a meridional restriction of the desired Killing field.

In the central extrusion no open flat subset contains an entire \(s\)-winding curve. Fix one \(a\) strictly inside the plateau of \(w\). At this meridian value, \(K\) is real analytic in \(y\in(-1,1)\), from (3) and the immersion bounds below, and is nonzero at \(y=0\). No transverse open interval can therefore belong to a flat open subset. Every winding curve meets this transverse line. This statement concerns the central extrusion; it does not prove Killing continuation across either head.

## 3. Bounds for the new full-width correction

The derivatives \(S_7'=140z^3(1-z)^3,\ S_7''=420z^2(1-z)^2(1-2z)\) give \(w\le25/6,\ |w'|<457,\ |w''|<274000\). With (2) this gives \(|A|\le.625,\ |A'|<70,\ |A''|<41300\) in either arclength orientation. For \(h=y^2/[2(1+u)^2]\), \(u=y^2/d_y^2\), we have \(0\le h\le d_y^2/8,\ |h'|\le d_y/2\), \(h''=(1-8u+3u^2)/(1+u)^4\), whence \(|h''|\le4\). The cutoff multiplying \(h\) has first and second derivatives bounded by \(8,56\). Hence \(|b'|<.5011d_y,\ |b''|<4.009\). In particular
\[
 |f|<8\,10^{-8},\quad |f_s|<.000009,\quad |f_y|<.00031,\quad
 |f_{ss}|<.005,\quad |f_{sy}|<.035,\quad |f_{yy}|<2.51 .
 \tag{5}
\]
On the support \(k=1/.15\) is constant. The full tangents and second derivatives there are
\[
 F_s=(1+kf)T+f_sn,\quad F_y=e_y+f_yn,\quad F_{ss}=2kf_sT+[f_{ss}-k(1+kf)]n,\quad F_{sy}=kf_yT+f_{sy}n,\quad F_{yy}=f_{yy}n .
\]
The metric has least eigenvalue at least \((1-|kf|)^2>.999998\). The normal before normalization is \(n-f_sT/(1+kf)-f_ye_y\), giving a unit-normal change \(<.00032\). By (5) the two absolute row sums of the scalar second form are \(<6.72,2.55\), respectively. Including the inverse metric gives absolute principal curvatures \(<6.73\).

## 4. All closed heads, actual diameter and reach

The whole uncorrected section has support \(\sigma=(\Gamma-c)\cdot n\ge.110\). On the bent disk the support is \((.595-u)\sin\beta+(H+v)\cos\beta\), at least \(.111\), and on its flat part it is \(H=.15\). On the right turn the ideal center is \(O_R=(.45+q,H+.44+q)\), and \(n=(-\cos\tau,\sin\tau)\). Thus support is at least \(r-|q-.145|-d\ge.110\). On the left \(O_L=(-1,H-r)\) and \(n=(-\sin\tau,\cos\tau)\) give \(r+1.595\sin\tau-d\ge.149\). On each wall the segment is tangent to \(n_0\), hence its section support equals that of the turn endpoint. Reflection preserves support. The section tangent and normal turn by a total \(2\pi\) in magnitude: on the upper arc the clockwise turns \(a_L+a_R\) are offset by the disk turn \(\pi/2\); the reflected reverse adds the same again. Its position angle about \(c\) differs continuously from that of the normal by less than \(\pi/2\), because \(\sigma>0\). Thus it also winds once. Its polar angle derivative has fixed nonzero sign by \(\sigma>0\), proving the curve is simple.

Use the complete R744 head construction with this new section. The transverse turn has \(R=.108,\ \eta=.0005,\ L_y=R\pi/2+\eta<.171\), angle \(\alpha\) from zero to \(\pi/2\), curvature at most \(1/R\), and \(p'=\cos\alpha,\ d_0'=\sin\alpha\), both integrals starting at zero. The two end annuli have planar section \(\Gamma-d_0n\) and \(y=\pm(1+p)\). Here \(p,d_0\le.1085,\ d_0\le L_y\sin\alpha,\ d_0\le R(1-\cos\alpha)+\eta\). The first and third inequalities follow from the excess-measure comparison, and the second from monotonicity. All offsets have positive speed \(1-d_0k\), support \(\sigma-d_0>.001\) and the same unit winding. Their polar angle about \(c\) is a bijection. At fixed polar angle the radius decreases with increasing offset, since their outward normal has positive radial component and displacement along the normal per unit offset is \(-1\). Consequently all lie inside the filled original section. Fill each terminal ring by its planar star disk about \(c\); near the seam use a short further-inward normal collar, as in R744 §2. The transverse jets there satisfy \(d_0'=1,\ p'=d_0''=p''=0\); curvature ramps give matching third derivatives. At the central cylinder the initial tangent of the head is the straight transverse tangent with zero acceleration. The extra normal graph (3) vanishes through three jets at \(|y|=1\). These give a closed unmarked \(C^3\) sphere, with the regular planar chart used at each cap center.

On the head annuli the outward support is
\[
 (\sigma-d_0)\cos\alpha+(1+p)\sin\alpha\ \ge\ .110\cos\alpha+(1-L_y)\sin\alpha\ \ge\ .110 .
\]
Their absolute principal curvatures are \(|k\cos\alpha/(1-d_0k)|\) and \(|\alpha'|\). For \(k\le0\) the first is at most \(6.91\); for \(0<k\le1/r\) it is at most \(1/(r-\eta)\) by the third bound for \(d_0\) and \(r>R+\eta\). The second is at most \(1/R\). The flat caps have support \(1+p(L_y)\) and curvature zero.

For the diameter, choose \(\bar c=c-.55 e\) on the mirror plane. In the root interval, \(.104<\bar c_x<.114,\ 0<H-\bar c_z<.4141\). On the original flat part the distance to \(\bar c\) is at most \(\sqrt{1.114^2+.4141^2}\), and on the disk bend/tongue it is at most \(\sqrt{.380^2+1.039^2}\). On the left turn use \(|O_L-\bar c|+r+d\le\sqrt{1.114^2+.265^2}+.151<1.297\). On the right use \(\sqrt{.530^2+1.039^2}+.151<1.319\); the actual bound from these numbers is below \(1.318\). On either wall the planar distance to \(\bar c\) decreases on the way to the mirror. The lower reflection preserves that distance. The entire filled Jordan section is in the same planar ball by convexity of that ball. All head annuli and planar end disks project inside it. The correction (3) affects only the cylinder and changes positions by \(<8\,10^{-8}\). Consequently the entire corrected projection is still in the ball of radius \(1.319\). With \(|y|\le1.1085\) the full spatial diameter is at most \(2\sqrt{1.319^2+1.1085^2}<3.45\). This also gives \(|F-c|\le\sqrt{(1.319+.55)^2+1.1085^2}<2.3\) for the old surface. The normal and position changes in (5) therefore lower the former support \(.110\) by less than \(.00074\). Together with §3, all corrected pieces have support \(>.109\) and principal curvatures at most \(1/R\).

## 5. The half-turn family, one fixed grip and quotient separation

On each upper/lower complete material disk and its extension to radius \(1.01\), use polar coordinates of the material \((x,y_m)\) as in R744 §5. Continue \(x\) by signed profile arclength and \(y_m\) by signed head arclength past the two rectangle edges. These charts have the prescribed seam jets. The two radius-\(1.01\) neighborhoods are disjoint: the pole separation along either profile arc is \(2\ell>2.02\), and their \(y_m\) extensions exist because \(L_y>.01\). For \(0\le t\le\pi\) change the material angle by \(-t\chi(\rho)\), where \(\chi=1\) on the unit disk, zero outside \(1.01\), and \(1-S_7((\rho-1)/.01)\) between. Its inverse uses the opposite sign, because the radius is preserved. Follow this diffeomorphism by the actual embedding and ambient rotation \(R_t\) about the vertical axis. The unmarked image is exactly \(R_t\) of the closed reference. The complete disks retain their Euclidean intrinsic metric. The two phase-transition annuli still carry a nonzero unverified whole-metric cost. The \(2\pi\)-periodic circle action constructed conditionally in R741 is distinct from the present physical phase interval.

On the upper chart take the open rectangle
\[
 |x|<.12,\qquad -.88<y_m<-.13 .
\]
Its radius is \(<.89\), and the pre-rotation bend coordinate is \(x\cos t+y_m\sin t\le|x|<.12\) for every \(t\in[0,\pi]\). Thus the material point remains on the horizontal flat part before ambient rotation, and the combined map fixes it pointwise. This proves a single upper grip of diameter at least \(.75>.2D\); no lower fixed grip is used. Take its union with the overlapping upper disk of radius \(.25\) if a single connected grip also containing the marks is desired. All of that disk is likewise fixed.

On the upper grip add the three disjoint stationary cubic graphs \(m(1-|p-p_i|^2/r_i^2)_+^3\) at \((p_i,r_i)=((-.10,.12),.077),((.10,.12),.086),((0,-.13),.093)\), with common height \(m=.002\). Their supports lie in radius \(.243\), disjoint from the open strip \(|y_m|<.034\). Hence at the reference phase the marked surface is identical to the unmarked one on a neighborhood of the full geodesic in §2 (the marks occur only on the upper disk). The polar Jacobi statement holds for this marked reference. At intermediate phases the time-dependent transition annuli still require the two-dimensional metric solve. The maximum slope is \(96m/(25\sqrt5\,r_i)<.045\); this follows by maximizing \(6t(1-t^2)^2\) on \([0,1]\). The Hessian eigenvalues in absolute value are at most \(6m/r_i^2<2.03\), from the tangential factor \((1-t^2)^2\) and radial factor \((1-t^2)(1-5t^2)\), both at most one in magnitude. The graph curvature is bounded by the same number. At phase \(t\) use \(c_t=R_tc\) as the common center. On the marks the support is at least
\[
 \frac{.15-.045(.595+.243)}{\sqrt{1+.045^2}}>.112.
\]
For the remaining surface transfer the actual unmarked support and curvature bounds by the ambient rotation. The marks stay inside the same rotated diameter bounding cylinder: their horizontal distance from its axis and their vertical height are bounded using \(|p|\le.243,\ |\bar c_x|<.114,\ H-\bar c_z<.4141,\ m=.002\), and their transverse magnitude is at most \(.243\). Hence \(D<3.45\) still holds for all phases. Apply R723-A to the complete connected sphere at each \(t\), with identity support matrix and that phase's single center. It gives embedding and reach at least \(.108>.03D\), including all mixed point pairs. The chart maps and marks are jointly continuous in \(t\) in \(C^2\).

Take the point on the upper right turn at angle \(\tau=\pi/2,\ y=0\). Its abscissa is at least \(.30+q+.15\ge.58\), and its height is at least \(H+.44+q+.15\ge H+.72\), since each coordinate of the excess-measure displacement over that first quarter is nonnegative. The correction (3) vanishes there. On the original upper central cylinder, every point came either from \(x_{\rm phys}\ge.30\) or from \(z\le H\). This uses the monotone bend, the right turn's nonnegative horizontal velocity and the right wall direction \(-n_0\); on the left pieces the height never exceeds \(H\). Reflection to the lower section adds \(2V\sin\gamma\) to horizontal position and subtracts \(2V\cos\gamma\) from height, with \(V\ge0\). Thus this same alternative holds on the entire uncorrected lower cylinder. Correction (3) relaxes each bound by at most \(8\,10^{-8}\). After the half-turn, a high cylinder point therefore lies at abscissa at most \(-.30+8\,10^{-8}\), and any remaining cylinder point has height at most \(H+8\,10^{-8}+.002\), marks included. All head and cap points have \(|y|\ge1\) under that half-turn. The distance from the chosen witness to the full half-turned marked image is at least \(.717>.2D\).

The unmarked image is \(C^3\). Its only added non-\(C^3\) points are exactly the three full marker circles, from the nonzero one-sided third derivative transverse to each. Every Euclidean congruence between the endpoints would permute these circles; the three radii are distinct, so it would fix the three noncollinear centers. Such an isometry is the identity or reflection in \(z=H\). Each marker circle bounds a small graph disk containing none of the other marked circles, whereas the complementary component contains both; hence any such congruence must carry that small marked disk to its counterpart. Reflection is excluded because its interior has positive height above \(H\) in both endpoint surfaces. Identity is excluded by the witness. Since a sequence of Euclidean motions aligning two compact nonempty sets to Hausdorff error tending to zero has bounded translations and a convergent subsequence of orthogonal parts, \(\delta_{\rm E}>0\). Aligning the unmarked images by a rotation shows \(\delta_{\rm E}\le.002\): the two sets of marker graphs after that alignment differ only by vertical displacements in \([0,.002]\) over the common central plane. With \(D\ge2\), this gives \(\delta_{\rm E}/D\le.001\); it gives no macroscopic lower bound for the quotient displacement.

The complete meridional return (4), fixed upper grip and global tube are properties of this one new physical field. They do not certify a Killing field on the full closed surface, a single common metric for the two transition annuli, or convergence of a fixed-target refinement.
