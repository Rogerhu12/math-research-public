# R772. A coherent family of complete developed caps, inward moving heads and thick closed translation tubes

This is a new fixed material sphere and initial metric. It has a closed $C^2$ embedded field with a fixed open grip of diameter $>.68$, initial diameter $D<1.55$, a half-phase endpoint Hausdorff distance $>.315$, and reach at least $1/21$ at every phase. Its two enclosed volumes differ by more than $.11$ and its endpoint Euclidean quotient distance is at least $.019$. On both complete developing cap disks the physical field is exactly isometric to the initial one. The entire remaining tube has exactly unit length in each of its coordinate directions, including every closed transverse ring. The sole error on that tube is an explicitly identified mixed coefficient. No statement here certifies the full fixed metric.

## Cross-section and axial material

In the world $(X,Z)$ plane let $C(e)$ be a clockwise arclength-parametrized rounded square centered at zero. Start at $(-b,H)$ with tangent $(1,0)$, take the straight side of length $2b=.14$, then round clockwise by $\pi/2$ with radius parameter $r=.27$. The arc turning speed ramps from zero to $1/r$ over $d=.001$ with $S_5(z)=10z^3-15z^4+6z^5$, stays constant and then ramps symmetrically down over $d$. Its length is $l=r\pi/2+d$. Let $d_c$ be its displacement in the original tangent direction, equal by symmetry to its inward displacement normal to that tangent. Set $H=b+d_c$. Repeat the segment and turn four times by quarter rotations. This closes a convex $C^2$ curve of perimeter $P=4(.14+l)$. Its outward unit normal is $n$ and $n'=k C'$ with $0\le k\le1/.27$. Symmetry gives $d_c\ge r$: changing to tangent angle on the increasing arc costs $de/d\alpha\ge r$. Also $d_c\le r+.001$, since the surplus arclength relative to $r\,d\alpha$ is exactly $.001$ and $|\cos\alpha|\le1$. Thus $.34\le H\le.341$. Every section support $C\cdot n\ge H$: on each corner its initial point has $C\cdot n=b\sin\alpha+H\cos\alpha$ plus the integral of $\sin(\alpha-\alpha(u))$ along that corner. On $[0,\pi/4]$ this is at least $b\sin\alpha+H\cos\alpha+r(1-\cos\alpha)\ge H$, since $b\sin\alpha\ge (H-r)(1-\cos\alpha)$ there. Reflect for the other half. The enclosed area $B$ is at least $\pi H^2$.

Use axial material $x\in[0,1]$, written $v=x-.5$ in the source. For $t\in[0,\pi/2]$ put $a=.01+1.66\sin t$, let $P_c$ be R767's normalized $.06$ clock, and define
$$
 h=\sin(2\pi P_c(x)),\quad w=h(1.5-.5h^2),\quad q_t=\arctan(a w),\qquad
 A_t(x)=.5-\int_x^1\cos q_t(u)du,\quad Z_t(x)=\int_0^x\sin q_t(u)du .
$$
Then $A_t'>0$, $Z_t\ge0$, $Z_t(0)=Z_t(1)=0$. The sign of $q_t$ reverses about $1/2$, and $q_t=q_t'=0$ at both axial ends. The entire coherent tube is
$$
 F_t(x,e)=(C_X(e),\ A_t(x),\ C_Z(e)+Z_t(x)).
$$
Its two circular-in-the-parameter openings at $x=0,1$ are the actual unchanged rounded-square curves up to translation. At both ends append a full outward axial quarter turn of length $L=.09\pi/2+.001$, by the same smooth clock with radius $.09$. With $\alpha$ its positive turning angle, write $p'=\cos\alpha,d'=\sin\alpha$, $p(0)=d(0)=0$. The two world shells are
$$
 (C_X-d n_X,\ A_t(x_*)+\sigma p,\ C_Z-d n_Z),\qquad (x_*,\sigma)=(0,-1),(1,1).
$$
At their rims $p_b=d_b\in[.09,.091]$. Fill each by the complete planar disk bounded by $C-d_b n$ at its actual constant axial height. Inward offset is simple, regular and convex: its tangent speed is $1-d_b k>0$, it retains the nondecreasing turn and total turn $2\pi$, and the explicit four straight edges and four shorter symmetrically monotone corners close without overlap. The cap polar chart $r(C(e)-d_b n(e))$, $0\le r\le1$, covers it. The Cartesian disk chart uses the actual world $(X,Z)$ coordinates. The normal chart uses $C-(d_b+u)n$; its true polar material labels follow by angular inversion on the convex star rim. The head-shell tangent initially is $\sigma e_Y$, with zero second axial and mixed derivatives, matching the tube after reversal at the left. At the disk it is $-n$ with zero normal acceleration. The tangent-normal derivatives of the offset chart are precisely those of the planar normal collar. This proves all four physical two-jet joins. These surfaces embeddedly bound the solid formed by the translated planar convex cross-section on each axial slice, rounded caps and terminal disks. Strict axial monotonicity and separate terminal planes prove embeddedness, including at the two regular disk centers.

## Entire developed disks and the remaining physical shear

On the whole moving tube one has exactly
$$
 g_{xx}=g_{ee}=1,\qquad g_{xe}=\sin q_t(x)\, C_Z'(e).
$$
Its area density is at least $\cos q_t\ge 1/\sqrt{1+1.67^2}>.51$. In particular every transverse closed material ring has its exact pointwise speed and its original complete circumference; the motion of that whole ring is one rigid translation. This applies equally to the two sides of any short corner.

The two nonrigid cap disks above and below the axis can be specified as material sets without extending a local development across another chart by assumption. On each straight transverse section $Z=\pm H$, $|X|\le b$, take its whole axial sweep $0\le x\le1$. In the two end rolls take the sweep of the same full material straight segment up to each terminal planar disk. In each planar disk extend by the rectangle $|X|\le b$, $.06\le\pm Z\le H-d_b$. This rectangle is inside the actual disk by convexity and its two opposite parallel straight edges. The union for each sign is one material disk (a rectangle capped by two rectangular strips); the two signs are disjoint. On its center the field is exactly $(X,A_t(x),\pm H+Z_t(x))$, a plane development along unit-speed curves with fixed transverse directions. Its edge strip through the actual whole end roll and the planar extension each undergo just its own head's rigid translation. Their common physical two-jets have just been proved. Hence all three entries on each entire identified disk, through both ends, are exactly those of its initial map. In addition both whole terminal planar disks and the two full axial quarter-turn shells retain all their initial metric. The two remaining connected bands cover the two intervening side arcs and have mixed error exactly $(\sin q_t-\sin q_0)C'_Z$. This formula includes the rounded portions and the true transverse extrema.

## Whole-image held, grip and Euclidean quotient

The cap source at $x=1$ is stationary. A closed portion including its full near-rim at $v=0$ has an open material interior and contains two points at distance $2H\ge.68$ (one can also include a compact right-head collar and its seam). All initial points fit in a box of dimensions at most $1.182,.682,.687$: the axial cap overhang is $\le .091$ and $Z_0\le.005$ since $|\sin q_0|<.01$ and the first half generates the maximum. Therefore $D<1.55$.

Let $D_A=\int_0^1(\cos q_0-\cos q_{\pi/2})dx$. The whole moving body's minimum axial coordinate is $A_t(0)-p_b$, so the original leftmost point is at distance at least $D_A$ from the entire terminal image. Here $D_A>.315$. One direct bound uses $dx\ge.94\,dP_c$ and symmetry on the four quarter cycles. On $0\le z\le\pi/2$ the function
$$
 d(z)=1-[1+1.67^2\sin^2 z\,(1.5-.5\sin^2 z)^2]^{-1/2}
$$
is increasing. For $j=1,\ldots,15$ rigorous lower bounds for $d(j\pi/32)$ in order are
$$
 (.027,.098,.182,.260,.324,.374,.411,.438,.456,.470,.477,.481,.484,.485,.485).
$$
They follow by using $z_j=3.14159j/32$, $h_j=z_j-z_j^3/6+z_j^5/120-z_j^7/5040\le\sin(j\pi/32)$, followed by the rational check $(1-d_j)^2[1+1.67^2 h_j^2(1.5-.5h_j^2)^2]>1$ for the tabulated decimals $d_j$. The left rectangular sum over 16 intervals is thus at least the sum of the displayed bounds divided by 16. Multiplying by $.94$ and subtracting $.01^2/2$ for the actual initially preloaded track gives $>.315$. This is a material grip exceeding $.2D$ together with a whole-image held distance exceeding $.2D$.

The cross-sectional area stays exactly $B$ on every axial slice of the coherent field. The rounded terminal volumes are rigid. Thus the enclosed volume decreases by precisely $B D_A\ge\pi(.34)^2(.315)>.11$. A compact embedded boundary determines its bounded component; all Euclidean isometries preserve its volume. If the infimum of the Hausdorff distances over Euclidean isometries were zero, the translations of an approximating sequence would be bounded and the orthogonal parts compact, giving an exact endpoint congruence. This proves strictly positive rigid quotient for this particular field.

## Principal curvatures on all phases

Write $c=\cos q$, $s=\sin q$, $\nu=n_Z$, $W=(1+\tan^2q\,\nu^2)^{1/2}$. The outward unit normal in world coordinates is $(n_X,-\tan q\,\nu,n_Z)/W$. The first form in $(x,e)$ is the matrix with diagonal ones and off diagonal $s C'_Z$, with determinant $c^2W^2$. The second form has zero mixed coefficient and diagonal absolute values $|q'|\,|\nu|/(cW)$ and $k/W$. Since the squared dual norms of $dx$ and $de$ are both $1/(c^2W^2)$, the operator norm of the shape map is at most
$$
 k/c^2+|q'|\max_{0\le z\le1} z/[c^3(1+\tan^2q\,z^2)^{3/2}].
$$
One has $|q'|\le16.75 c^2(1-h^2)^{3/2}$, directly by differentiating $a(1.5h-.5h^3)$ and $P_c'\le1/.94$. For $|\tan q|\le1/\sqrt2$ the maximum in this formula is exactly 1 at $z=1$. The resulting bound $16.75c^2+1/(.27c^2)$ is below 21 on $2/3\le c^2\le1$ by convexity and the endpoint values. Otherwise the maximum is $2/(3\sqrt3\,c^2|\sin q|)$. If $|q|\le.85$ use $|\sin q|\ge1/\sqrt3$, $\cos^2(.85)>.43$, giving $<20$. If $|q|>.85$, then $|h|\ge\tan(.85)/(1.5\cdot1.67)>.45$, $|\sin q|>.75$, and $1/c^2\le1+1.67^2$. The bound is then less than $1.67^2/.27+1/.27+16.75(2/(3\sqrt3))(.798)^{3/2}/.75<21$. On the static heads the two principal curvature magnitudes are $\alpha'\le1/.09$ and $k\cos\alpha/(1-dk)\le1/(.27-.091)<6$. Both terminal planes have zero curvature.

## Injectivity of the whole signed normal tube

Take the axial center $m=(A_t(0)+A_t(1))/2$. Set $I(x)=Z_t(x)-(A_t(x)-m)\tan q_t(x)$. It is nonnegative since $Z_t\ge0$ and the other term has the same sign on both halves. Its maximum is at the first maximum of $q$ or its reflection, by $I'=-(A_t-m)(\tan q_t)'$. At $P_c(x_*)=1/4$ the maximum is
$$
 I_* = \int_0^{x_*}\sin q_t\,dx+a\int_{x_*}^{1/2}\cos q_t\,dx<.472 .
$$
Indeed the two symmetric clock quarter ranges have the same distribution of $h$. Their common uniform weight $.94\,dP_c$ contributes at most $(.94/4)\sqrt{1+1.67^2}$ by $\sin q+a\cos q\le\sqrt{1+a^2}$. Only the first quarter has excess clock mass $.03$, all at $P_c\le(.06-.03)/.94$, where $\tan q\le a(1.5)\sin(2\pi(.03/.94))<.50$, and hence $\sin q<.448$. The two contributions total less than $.472$. Choose world center $(0,m,I_*/2)$. The coherent tube support is at least $(H-.236)/\sqrt{1+1.67^2}>.053$. On either end roll the outward normal has axial component $\sigma\sin\alpha$ and transverse component $\cos\alpha\,n$. The transverse support is at least $H-d_b-.236>.012$. The axial contribution is at least $(.5/\sqrt{1+1.67^2})\sin\alpha$. If $\sin\alpha\ge.2$ this is $>.051$; if $\sin\alpha<.2$ then $d\le L(.2)<.029$, so the transverse term alone exceeds $.073$. The flat terminal disks have axial support $>.25$. Thus the full surface has strict outward support $>1/21$ about this phase's center.

For each fixed signed offset $|z|<1/21$, the parallel surface $F_z=F+zN$ is regular since every principal curvature has magnitude $<21$. Its oriented normal remains $N$, and $(F_z-c_*)\cdot N>0$. Radial projection of its parametrizing sphere about $c_*$ is consequently a local diffeomorphism. Compactness makes it a cover of the sphere, and the connected domain and simply connected target make it one-to-one. For a fixed spatial radial direction $u$ let $R_z u+c_*$ be the unique projected point. Differentiation in $z$ and taking its scalar product with $N$ gives $R'_z=1/(u\cdot N)>0$. This strictly separates every two different signed offsets along every ray. The full normal map on $|z|<1/21$ is therefore injective; closest-point displacements to a compact smooth embedded surface are normal, proving the reach bound. In particular $1/21>.03D$.

The same normal tube gives a quantitative quotient bound. The area of the final surface is $<3.85$. In fact the core area density is at most one, hence its area is at most $P$; the two rolls have combined area at most $2PL$ and the two terminal disks combined area at most $2(2H)^2$. Suppose a Euclidean transform placed the initial boundary within Hausdorff distance $d<.019$ of the final one. Its whole bounded solid lies inside the solid bounded by the final outward $d$-offset: the transformed boundary is in that solid, and the connected outside of the offset sphere reaches infinity without touching the transformed boundary. The outer volume gain is at most
$$
 3.85\int_0^d (1+21z)^2dz=3.85d[1+21d+(21d)^2/3]<.107
$$
for $d<.019$. This contradicts the $>.11$ volume difference. Thus the endpoint Euclidean quotient distance is at least $.019$, exceeding $.012D$ for this specified coherent field.

## Distinguishing the normal-offset comparator and the shear payment

The generator also provides $F^n=(C_X+Z_t n_X,A_t,C_Z+Z_t n_Z)$. This independent initial metric has the orthogonal tube form $dx^2+(1+kZ_t)^2de^2$. Despite its two exact tube components, prescribing all four flat band positions from this comparator cannot be rescued by changing only its four curved corner interiors. On one smoothed quarter the chord between the two flat endpoints is $\sqrt2(d_c+Z_t)$, whereas the initial material length over that corner is $l+\pi Z_0/2$. At the mid-axial station the terminal $Z_t$ exceeds $.16$: on $.15\le x\le.35$ one has $h>.70$ and $\sin q_t>.82$. Hence the terminal chord exceeds $.60$ while the initial material curve has length $<.434$. This argument does not apply to the coherent field, whose entire cross-sectional point differences at each $x$ are unchanged.

In fact no relabeling of individual transverse rings can repair the coherent product field. If $e\mapsto E(x,e)$ is an orientation-preserving circle reparametrization at each fixed $x$, preserving the fixed $g_{ee}=1$ forces $E=e+\eta(x)$. The mixed row then becomes $\eta'(x)+\sin q_t C'_Z(e+\eta)$. Integrating over a full ring against $de$ gives $P\eta'=0$ since both its old and requested $C'_Z$ have zero mean. A shift also preserves $\max|C'_Z|=1$, so equality at every $e$ would force $|\sin q_t(x)|=|\sin q_0(x)|$, false on a full open axial interval. The actual mixed residual thus requires further physical degrees of freedom or a map also moving the axial label. This limitation does not exclude general closed fields on this frozen new metric.
