## 4. A finite euclidean-quotient witness

The held witness $>.739$ in R759 uses a final point fixed by the true material description of the translating left head, and the initial R757 image. It applies to these whole closed embeddings. The grip has its original diameter $\ge .72$. To prove a rigid-quotient lower bound, let $A_\theta$ be the area within the moving section. The ideal comparison strip has vertical height $.34$ over the horizontal projection of the centerline, whose length is $\int_0^U\cos\psi\,dx$. The left comparison head is translated to its corresponding ideal track endpoint and the right head is unchanged. Their contributions to the planar area are constant. Between actual and ideal profiles pointwise distance is $<e_1$ (at zero $<.00058$) and the linear homotopy has arclength $<8$ at each step by convexity of the speed; the ideal tracks have speed 1 in the old coordinate and the real ones have speed $\sqrt{1+f'^2}$. The integral area derivative is bounded by displacement times arclength. Hence for $\theta=.78,\ j=2$,
$
 A_0-A_\theta\ \ge\ .34(7U/11)(1-\cos(.78))-8(e_1+.00058)>.0639 .
$
No estimate of the two ramps is needed for this lower bound. For every $0\le d\le d_b$ the inner parallel encloses area $A_\theta-Pd+\pi d^2$, since its boundary arclength is $P-2\pi d$; normal coordinates are injective and total signed turning is $2\pi$. Consequently the volume between the two planar cap planes, bounded by the center and the two actual rolls, changes by exactly $2(Y+p_b)(A_0-A_\theta)$. The additional two cap graph volumes have total difference of magnitude less than .008, using height $<.000351$ and the common rectangle area $<5.6$ for each planar disk. Also $p_b\ge .108+.81\cos(1/1024)>.9179$. Therefore the complete enclosed volume drops by more than .156.

The initial surface area is $<34$. The central-plus-roll contribution is at most $2(Y+L)P$; the two graph disks contribute at most $2(5.6)\sqrt{1+.071^2}$. Suppose a rigidly moved final surface had Hausdorff distance $\varepsilon\le .004$ from the initial boundary $\Sigma$. The eroded initial interior at distance $>\varepsilon$ from $\Sigma$ is connected; one can retract the interior onto a slightly smaller closed core using its inward normal collar of radius .106. The unbounded exterior beyond the corresponding offset is connected by the analogous collar. The moved final bounded domain must contain that interior core. Otherwise it would lie entirely in the closed $\varepsilon$-tube of $\Sigma$, since its boundary is in the tube and the unbounded exterior contains no part of its interior. Such containment is impossible: the final domain contains an open interior ball of radius .05 by its proved tube, whereas at every point of the $\varepsilon$-tube one can exit it by following the unique signed normal for distance at most $2\varepsilon+.001<.05$. The missing initial volume can thus be at most the inner collar volume $34\varepsilon(1+\varepsilon/.106)^2<.147$, a contradiction. Continuity of the rigid-motion minimization on bounded translations and the compact orthogonal group gives $\delta_E>.004$. This bound and $\delta_{\rm hold}>.739$ pertain to the same named physical motion and every intermediate phase has the stated reach. Its unresolved metric components still prevent classification as a solution of the topic.
