The phase interval is $0\le\phi\le\pi/2$. Every refinement uses the one exact metric $g=F_0^*e$ defined below on a material sphere. The three source files are [the section](../scripts/resume_20261126/r757_curved_profile_two_jets.py), [the initial closed surface](../scripts/resume_20261126/r757_delayed_metric.py) and [the moving two-jet field](../scripts/resume_20261126/r757_curved_rectifying_closed_two_jets.py). Integrals and inverse arclengths in the mathematical definitions are exact. Finite public computations use quadrature and numerical inversion.

There is no intrinsically flat open patch on either open roll or either cap of this initial metric. This includes every open collar immediately inside each cap rim. Our named entire phase family consists of embedded $C^2$ closed spheres, has reach at least $.107$ throughout, initial diameter $D<3.50$, a pointwise fixed grip of diameter at least $.72$, held displacement $>1.519$ and positive Euclidean-quotient displacement. Both independent *rigid* cap motions and the entire prescribed central motion realize their respective exact initial tensors. A global isometric motion on this new metric remains open.

## 1. The new plane curve

$
Keep the original curve elsewhere. The first four derivatives of $f$ vanish at each endpoint. The curve has no new corner at the equator. ## 4. The positive geometry family and the remaining tensor

On each roll put $w_7=S_7(v/L)$, choose any of the three R753 material transports $S(s,v,\phi)$, and retain the actual displacement
$
 (q(S)-dn(S),y)+(1-w_7)\Delta_C+w_7\Delta_{\rm fan,pose}
                        +V_\pm(s,v,\phi).
$
Here $y=\pm(Y+p(v))$, $\Delta_C=C(S,y)-(q(S),y)$. The second displacement uses the R753 fan and independent rigid pose, applied to the *planar extension* of the actual initial cap up to the rim. The two $V_\pm$ are independent arbitrary three-vector corrections with transverse zero two-jets at both rims, vanishing at phase zero. On the actual cap apply its fan displacement to $P$ first, retain the signed cap height $\pm f_c(P)$ in the transverse component, then apply its own full rigid pose about $(.595,\pm(Y+p_b),0)$. Since $f_c$ vanishes through three derivatives on the rim, these are exactly the same physical boundary two-jets. The fixed grip is untouched. The rigid pose and outer axial rotation are genuine Euclidean transformations; they preserve the whole cap's actual metric when the cap's fan amplitudes are zero.

More precisely, let $W(P)$ be the transverse displacement component of the R753 isometric development of the plane. Before the rigid pose the actual moving cap tensor equals
$
 g_{\rm cap} \;+\; \pm(df_c\otimes dW+dW\otimes df_c).
$
This follows directly by adding the transverse signed height to the three-vector of the planar development. Thus the six optional fan directions have a true price.
