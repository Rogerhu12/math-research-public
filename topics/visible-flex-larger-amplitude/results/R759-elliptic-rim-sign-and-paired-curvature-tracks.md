# R759. The elliptic boundary sign and a paired-track central motion on the R757 metric

All signs below concern the fixed material normal orientation. Let a $C^2$ immersion of a rolled annulus with the R757 initial metric extend $C^2$ to its prescribed center. On an open interval $I$ with initial $k_0(s)>0$, $K(s,v)=d''(v)k_0(s)/(1-d(v)k_0(s))>0$ for all $s\in I,\ 0<v<L$. In a continuous unit normal the second fundamental form is definite throughout this connected open strip: the determinant is positive, and neither eigenvalue can pass through zero. The scalar $II(\partial_s,\partial_s)$ therefore has one strict sign on the open strip. Its continuous boundary values on the whole arc $I\times\{0\}$ have one weak sign. The Gaussian identity used here holds also for a $C^2$ isometry: on a regular interior chart the weak Gauss identity (obtained by smoothing the immersion in $C^2$) equates its continuous extrinsic curvature density to the intrinsic curvature density. In more detail, metrics of the smooth approximants converge in $C^1$, Christoffel symbols converge uniformly, their derivatives converge in distributions, and the remaining quadratic terms converge uniformly. The limiting metric on the open roll is the given smooth-enough $g$, so the identity holds pointwise by continuity. This proof uses neither a presumed analytic motion nor an extension across a characteristic parabolic edge.

On the original seven R757 shift-center designs, at the upper material track the central image, up to a common rigid motion, is $(q(s+h),y)$ with $h=.15\phi$. This station is away from the R753 spatial bending window, so every allowed original hard parameter gives the same boundary normal curvature there. The initial bowed cell has $f=.04b^2[4t(1-t)]^5$ and $k_0=-f''/(1+f'^2)^{3/2}$. In particular $k_0>0$ on $1/3<t<2/3$, and $k_0<0$ directly to the right of $2/3$. Let $s_r$ denote that simple zero in the true new arclength. For all sufficiently small $h>0$, both $s_r-3h/2,\ s_r-h/2$ belong to the same initial positive cell. Their shifted stations $s_r-h/2,\ s_r+h/2$ have opposite signed curvature. On the central seam the second form on $\partial_s$ evaluates, up to a single overall normal sign, to $k_0(s+h)$. This contradicts the preceding boundary condition. No choice of roll or cap field can repair these seven prescribed center traces, even with arbitrarily many smooth physical modes. The conclusion only concerns their exact limit. In particular it does not prohibit an independently varying center on the same initial metric, or establish a positive quantitative lower bound for a particular finite Gram norm.

## A finite control preserving all elliptic rim signs

Let $U=1.30$ and $b=U/11$. The upper and lower originally horizontal tracks have identical bowed cells in their respective increasing material directions: on the upper track progress $\xi\in[0,U]$ runs from left to right, on the lower it runs from right to left. Their unmodified $xz$-derivatives in this *old* label are exactly opposites $(1,f_\xi)$, $(-1,-f_\xi)$, because the two old tracks run in opposite directions, the lower outward normal is reversed, and the cell template is symmetric. Their common true speed is $e=(1+f_\xi^2)^{1/2}$. Write $R_\psi$ for planar positive rotation. Multiply both of those derivative vectors by the **same** rotation $R_{\psi(\xi)}$ at matching progress, keeping every other tangent on the profile unchanged. Thus the two contributions to the closure integral cancel for every $\psi$. Hold the right complete connecting head fixed and translate the entire left connecting head by the one constant
$
             -D(U),\qquad D(x)=\int_0^x(R_{\psi(\xi)}-I)(1,f_\xi)\,d\xi .
$
The upper track displacement is $D(\xi)-D(U)$, the lower is $-D(\xi)$. Taking $\psi=0$ at the track ends with sufficient zero jets gives the original smooth joins. The new closed curve $q_\phi$ is exactly unit speed in the *same* R757 $s$. Its true curvatures are $k_\phi=k_0-\psi_\xi/e$ and $k_{\phi,s}=k_{0,s}-\psi_{\xi\xi}/e^2+\psi_\xi f_\xi f_{\xi\xi}/e^4$ along both tracks.

For explicit finite controls use $j=2$ or $3$ full equal cells to raise $\psi$ from zero to $\theta=\theta_{\max}\sin\phi$, stay constant through the middle $11-2j$ cells, and reverse the rise on the final $j$ cells. On each rising cell, the derivative of $\psi$ in original label $\xi$ is $\theta B(t)/(jb m)$, where $t$ is its unit cell coordinate and $m=2(1/3-.025)$. On each of $[a,b']=[0,1/3],[2/3,1]$ take $S_5((t-a)/.025)-S_5((t-b'+.025)/.025)$, extended with the constant tails of the quintic smootherstep, and sum the two. This gives $0\le B\le1,\ \int_0^1 B=m$. The density and its first two derivatives vanish at the joins. Its support with positive density lies strictly where $k_0<0$. On every positive-curvature material cell the moving **entire** central second form thus retains its boundary sign and magnitude. The central tensor on the entire transverse extrusion is exactly $ds^2+dy^2$. This family fixes the real circular grip pointwise in the world frame at every phase.

For $\theta_{\max}\le .81,\ j\ge2$, $|\psi_\xi|<5.57$. On its support $k_0<0,\ |k_0|\le1.6$, and elsewhere $k_\phi=k_0$, so throughout $k_\phi\le1/.15$, $k_\phi>-7.18$. The two new physical parallel rolls
$
        F_\pm(s,v)=(q_\phi(s)-d(v)n_\phi(s),\quad \pm(Y+p(v)))
$
are regular as parametrized surfaces. On every one of their material meridians the two boundary points are connected by this actual unit-speed path of the prescribed length $L$. Everywhere $g_{vv}=1,\ g_{sv}=0,\ g_{ss}=(1-dk_\phi)^2$. Thus all three roll rows have the original exact values except the single angular row on the altered subcells; the center-to-rim length and the elliptic strip do not have to be inferred from a sampled set of chords. Any extra moving cap pose coupled onto a roll adds its own measured fee.

The left head undergoes a finite displacement already before any free roll correction. For $\theta=.78,\ j=2$ its downward translation has magnitude $D_z(U)=\int [\sin\psi+(\cos\psi-1)f_\xi]d\xi$. On each ramp cell $\psi$ has its mean at the midpoint of its two endpoint values by the symmetric density. Concavity of sine gives a lower bound for that integral by the trapezoidal mean of those two endpoint sine values times the cell length. Therefore
$
 D_z(U)\ge b\{7\sin(.78)+2[\sin(.39)+\tfrac12\sin(.78)]\}
                   -.0201 U(1-\cos(.78))>.74 .
$
For this inequality it suffices to use $\sin(.78)>.703,\ \sin(.39)>.380,\ \cos(.78)>.710$. Also $|D_x(U)|\le U[1-\cos(.78)+.0201\sin(.78)]<.397$. A final lower left-track endpoint originally $(-1,-.17)$ on the center $y=0$ consequently has $x<-.603,\ z<-.91$. On the complete *initial* R757 body, every point at abscissa $x<.18$ has $z\ge-.171$: every planar projection lies in the initial bounded section, whose lower boundary there consists only of the left turns, left wall and the bowed horizontal track, with outward displacement $<.00058$. The whole initial section to the right of that cutoff has planar horizontal distance $>.783$ from this particular final point. Thus its distance to the complete initial image exceeds $.739$, a genuine directed whole-image Hausdorff witness $>.2D$ since $D<3.50$. Changing the roll or cap away from this central point does not affect this direction of the comparison. This estimate gives a finite held witness for the new zero-pose geometry if the whole moving field is embedded. It is a point-set distance estimate on its own; it does not prove whole-body reach.
