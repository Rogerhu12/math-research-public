## 1. Straight transverse lines for a C² isometry of a flat material strip

Let an open material rectangle have its Euclidean metric in coordinates $(s,v)$. On a neighborhood along its lower edge prescribe
$$
 F(s,0)=\gamma(s),\qquad F_v(s,0)=e,\qquad
 |\gamma'|=1,\quad \gamma\perp e\ \text{up to a constant},\quad |e|=1 .
$$
Suppose this is the trace of the actual adjacent generalized cylinder $F(s,v)=\gamma(s)+ve$ for negative $v$, and $\gamma''(s_*)\ne0$. Any $C^2$ isometric continuation across the strip has
$$
 F(s_*,v)=\gamma(s_*)+ve                                      \tag{1}
$$
until the initially straight material segment first leaves the flat open region. All its cylindrical two-jets continue along the line. The statement holds when that open region is assembled from several charts, provided its Euclidean development extends the same straight strip.

Here are the regularity and continuation details. The map normal $n$ is $C^1$. The Gauss identity gives $\det II=0$ on a flat isometric chart. At a nonzero value of $II$ the Gauss map therefore has constant rank one on a neighborhood. By the constant-rank theorem we can write it as $n=N(t)$ for a regular transverse $C^1$ coordinate $t$, with $N'(t)\ne0$. On a level curve $t=\mathrm{constant}$, $F\cdot N=h(t)$ is constant, since its derivative along that curve is tangent dotted with normal. Differentiation with respect to $t$ gives $F\cdot N'=h'$. Thus each level has image in the straight line of intersection of the two planes with normals $N,N'$. The map is regular there, so each level is an open straight segment. Its material curve is straight as well: a unit-speed straight curve on the image is a geodesic, and the material chart is Euclidean. Equivalently the $C^2$ isometry keeps local lengths and the short segment is minimizing. This reasoning requires neither third derivatives nor analyticity.

In the cylinder the Gauss map varies with $s$ and its levels at $s_*$ are the $v$-lines, since $\gamma''\ne0$. On a small open strip around this line the transverse rulings starting from the cylinder must therefore be $v$-parallel unit-speed lines, giving the whole product germ $F(s,v)=\gamma(s)+ve$. To verify that the germ cannot terminate at an interior flat point, take the supremum of its continuation lengths on the middle line, allowing the neighboring $s$-intervals to narrow. At a putative first endpoint the first and second jets on the middle line tend to the cylindrical jets by continuity. In particular $F_{ss}=\gamma''(s_*)\ne0,\ F_{sv}=F_{vv}=0$. There is another constant-rank neighborhood there and the unique ruling of that point is still the $v$-line. Take a slightly earlier $v_1$ in the same neighborhood where the old cylindrical germ is known on an open interval of $s$. The ruling directions are continuous in this neighborhood. All rulings through a sufficiently small neighborhood of the endpoint intersect the preceding interval at $v_1$, remaining within that constant-rank chart between the two levels. On this interval those rulings have the exactly prescribed direction and spatial tangent. Hence they remain the product there and past the proposed endpoint. This proves continuation through each interior point. Position extends continuously to the final rim. Isolated tangencies of a ray with a remote rim do not matter: stop at its *first* contact.

Interior compact pieces admit neighborhoods of straight lines in the same planar development. If at another phase the full prescribed cylinder is $\Gamma_\theta(s)\times[-Y,Y]$ with $\Gamma_\theta''(s_*)\ne0$, apply (1) in the two strips with directions $+e_y,-e_y$. ## 5. What happens if one tries to keep both tilted collars throughout the flat caps

The geometry beyond the paid right collar is essential. Take an initial right-wall label just before the end of its final return ramp. Its curvature is nonzero, while its normal is arbitrarily close to horizontal and its height close to the small final vertical dwell. That dwell is less than .002, and $|z|<.004$ after the terminal offset for labels sufficiently close to this end. In the initial cap the inward normal ray from the terminal point runs a distance $w>.5$ before reaching another rim. For instance at the limiting vertical track its terminal abscissa is at least $x_R-d_b>.59$, the terminal strip at small positive height contains the horizontal segment from $x=0$ to the right rim, and the same remains true for every sufficiently close interior label. The horizontal segment lies inside by the two vertical sides and the upper/lower main sheets above/below the strip. Near the right side the offset wall stays a vertical graph, and at such small height no other positive-height piece intervenes.

If both exact end rolls and their collars (3)–(4) are retained, the new transverse direction into either cap has planar component $-n_\theta/a$ and $y$-component $\pm\sqrt{1-a^{-2}}$. Just inside the cap (4) has straight generators. Its other principal direction has nonzero normal curvature for $a>1$ at the chosen label: its normal projection onto the section normal has magnitude $\sqrt{1-a^{-2}}$, and $1-(d_b+w)\kappa_0$ is positive there. Section 1's local rank-one ruling argument therefore continues *the straight line* through the original flat cap until its first exit. For this nonparallel family of initial rays more care with nonzero curvature is needed. In initial cap Fermi coordinates its angular unit tangent is $T_\theta$, independent of $w$; the principal curvature magnitude is
$$
 \frac{|\kappa_\theta|\sqrt{1-a^{-2}}}{|1-(d_b+w)\kappa_0|}.
$$
Choose the stated label on the last ramp, where $\kappa_0<0$ (upper orientation $k=-\lambda T_*'$). Its inward initial normal rays diverge, so this denominator never vanishes. At any interior ray point the nearby initial normal coordinates are valid and the rank-one proof applies with these straight rays. A compact subsegment can be continued by its successive normal-coordinate germs even if more distant lines meet elsewhere. The positive nonzero numerator and finite denominator at any interior trial endpoint give the same open-and-closed argument as in §1. At the exit point, both caps have reached the same other *initial* rim label, so the initial distance ceiling is again $2+2L$. The forced spatial separation is
$$
 2\{1+P(L)+w\sqrt{1-a^{-2}}\}.
$$
At the final phase the loss ratio is .165/.03. Since $(1-\cos z)/z^2$ decreases in the present range, $a^2\ge5.5$, so $\sqrt{1-a^{-2}}>.90$. With $w>.5,\ P(L)\ge p_b\ge.132$ the ceiling fails strictly. This last argument does not assert uniqueness for all possible Cauchy problems in the initially curved rolls. It shows that the particular exact roll and collar branches (3)–(4) cannot be extended through both entire original flat caps on this fixed metric. The simultaneous full closed two-jet fields are honest geometric inputs for a further solver that can vary at least one of these end data. Allowing initially nonflat cap curvature along the forced rays is another distinct fixed-target construction. Neither change can be replaced by relabeling the given flat cap.
