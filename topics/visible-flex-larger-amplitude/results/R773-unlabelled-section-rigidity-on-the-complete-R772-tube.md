# R773. Unlabelled congruent sections force rigidity on the complete R772 material tube

Let $C(e)=(C_X(e),0,C_Z(e))$, $e\in\mathbb R/P\mathbb Z$, be the actual centered clockwise unit-speed rounded square of R772. It has exactly four maximal straight intervals, each of length $.14$ and with starts spaced by $P/4$. Throughout each intervening open corner the tangent turns strictly clockwise. Write $x\in[0,1]$ for the axial label, $H(x)=\sin q_0(x)$, $K(x)=\cos q_0(x)>0$, and $c_0'(x)=(0,K,H)$. The initial tube is $C(e)+c_0(x)$. Its prescribed metric has unit diagonal and mixed coefficient $H(x)C'_Z(e)$.

**Theorem.** Assume a $C^2$ map $F$ of the whole cylinder has precisely this metric. Suppose for every $x$ its complete transverse material circle maps onto a space curve Euclidean-congruent as a set to $C$. Then $F$ on this cylinder is one ambient Euclidean isometry of the original field. The congruence and the parametrization may initially be chosen separately for every section; no labeling, matching point, preferred frame or common plane for those congruences is prescribed.

For fixed $x$ the unit-speed circle has length $P$, and its image is the regular simple curve of length $P$. Thus it traverses this image exactly once. Arclength on a closed curve is unique up to shift and reversal. Any fixed reversal can be absorbed in the known planar reflection symmetry of $C$ (for instance reflection across $X=0$ sends $e$ to $.14-e$). A rigid map on a two-plane can be extended to a proper three-dimensional rotation by the corresponding choice of the normal sign. Consequently locally in $x$ one has
$$
 F(x,e)=b(x)+R(x)C(e+\eta(x)),\qquad R\in SO(3).
$$
Here differentiable choices really are available for the stipulated regularity. To see this, fix a representation at $x_*$ and unwrap $\eta$ there. Modulo the four quarter rotations the shift is unique for a parametrization with this orientation: its true maximal straight intervals identify their starts up to quarter turns. Local continuity of that class follows also by compactness. Any sequence $x_j\to x_*$ has bounded translations (evaluate at one point), a convergent subsequence of rotations and shifts, and the limit must be a representation of the same curve and map at $x_*$. Adjust by the finite quarter-turn symmetry to use the nearby lift of the shift. Pick two fixed material labels $e_0,e_1$ such that $e_0+\eta_*$ is interior to a straight segment and $e_1+\eta_*$ lies strictly inside an adjacent corner with nonzero curvature and tangent neither parallel nor antiparallel to the first. Then
$$
 F_e(x,e_0)\cdot F_e(x,e_1)=C'(e_0+\eta)\cdot C'(e_1+\eta).
$$
The left side is $C^1$ in $x$. The derivative of the right side with respect to $\eta$ at $x_*$ is nonzero, since the first tangent is constant nearby and the second is rotating with nonzero curvature at a nonzero sine of their angle. The scalar inverse function theorem gives a $C^1$ local lift $\eta$. Two independent tangents and their oriented cross product now recover a $C^1$ rotation $R$ and then a $C^1$ translation $b$. This proof applies also when the unlabelled cap-plane normal points in an arbitrary world direction.

In such a lift put $a=R^Tb'$, $R^TR'w=\omega\times w$. The exact mixed coefficient is
$$
 H(x)C'_Z(e)=a\cdot C'(e+\eta)+\omega_Y (C\times C')_Y(e+\eta)+\eta'.
$$
On every shifted maximal straight interval the right-hand side is constant: $C'$ is constant and $(C+rC')\times C'=C\times C'$. If $H(x)\ne0$ then the left-hand side is constant on an interval only when it is contained in an original maximal straight interval. This is because on any open portion of a corner the angle varies strictly, and the vertical component of a rotating unit tangent cannot be constant on an open interval of angles. Since each of the four shifted intervals has the full original length $.14$, they agree exactly with the four originals. It follows that the shift class is zero modulo $P/4$. The only zeros of $H$ are $0,1/2,1$, from the strict R767 clock increase and the factor $h(1.5-.5h^2)$. Continuity extends the same class throughout. Absorb a constant quarter rotation of $C$ into $R$ to take $\eta=0$. With this convention the proper rotation and the translation are uniquely determined and the local choices agree globally.

Integrating the mixed equation in $e$ now leaves $\omega_Y\int(C\times C')_Yde=0$. The integral is $2B>0$ in the chosen clockwise $(X,Z)$ convention, where $B$ is the actual enclosed planar area. Hence $\omega_Y=0$. Since the tangents $C'$ span the section plane, the pointwise mixed equation then gives $a_X=0$, $a_Z=H$. The axial unit condition takes the exact form
$$
 (a_Y+\omega_Z C_X(e)-\omega_X C_Z(e))^2=1-H^2=K^2>0.
$$
Its bracket is continuous over the full connected transverse circle and therefore is constant, $+K$ or $-K$. A nonzero linear functional on the section plane cannot be constant on this noncollinear closed curve. Thus $\omega_X=\omega_Z=0$. The rotation is independent of $x$, and $a_Y=\epsilon K$ for one constant sign on the entire axial interval. Integrating gives the asserted Euclidean motion of $C+c_0$, incorporating a possible axial reflection when $\epsilon=-1$.

In particular a continuous family starting at the identity with the full right R772 head fixed cannot obtain the R772 central axial contraction by moving or reparametrizing rigid transverse loops, however the directors twist in space. At the stationary right ring three noncollinear points eliminate every motion except possibly reflection in their common plane, which the fixed axial derivative (or continuity from the identity) excludes. The theorem concerns this material cylinder and the fixed $g_0$. It does not force an arbitrary cap outside the cylinder to be rigid, forbid a genuinely changing transverse curve, or exclude an embedding whose *physical* cross-sections use different axial material labels. The run-48 physical dictionary contains shape-changing and coupled axial/transverse material terms, which are outside this rigidity class.

## Independently flexible plane curves in parallel section planes

There is a further obstruction which does not require congruent sections. Let a $C^2$ immersion with the same R772 metric have the property that every complete transverse ring is a regular embedded plane curve and all these planes are parallel for this configuration. The normal to that common plane may depend on the configuration in a family; choose a unit normal $n$ at that phase. Write the field as $F(x,e)=A(x)n+D(x,e)$, $D\in n^\perp$, with any fixed world origin. The signed perpendicular distances of the section planes must then satisfy
$$
 |A'(x)|=\cos q_0(x)\quad\text{for every }x.
$$
Thus the absolute separation between any two of the section planes equals the corresponding initial axial separation. If the whole right head is held pointwise and the orientation is continued from the identity, the planes are word-for-word the original parallel planes at their original axial positions. This conclusion concerns the planes and leaves possible shape changes inside them unspecified.

Indeed set $T=D_e$, $V=D_x$, $h(x,e)=H(x)C'_Z(e)$. The three prescribed metric entries say $|T|=1$, $V\cdot T=h$, and $|V|^2=1-A'^2=b(x)^2$. Since the original transverse vertical side attains $|C'_Z|=1$, necessarily $b\ge |H|$. Fix an $x$ for which strict inequality holds. For the oriented plane let $N$ be the unit left normal to $T$ and set $T_e=\kappa N$. The normal component of $V$ has a single nonzero sign around the whole connected circle, whence $V\cdot N=\sigma\sqrt{b^2-h^2}$. Differentiating the true unit speed in $x$ gives $T\cdot V_e=0$. Differentiating the mixed row in $e$ therefore yields $h_e=\kappa\,\sigma\sqrt{b^2-h^2}$. Since $b>0$ is constant around this circle, integration gives
$$
 \int_0^P\kappa\,de=\sigma\int_0^P [\arcsin(h/b)]_e\,de=0.
$$
A regular, simple, closed oriented planar curve has total signed curvature $+2\pi$ or $-2\pi$ by the turning-tangent theorem. In fact a nonzero turning number suffices. This contradiction rules out every $b>|H|$, including $H=0$, $b>0$. At $H=0$ one therefore has $b=0$ directly, with no quotient formed. Consequently $|A'|=\sqrt{1-H^2}=K>0$ throughout including $x=0,1/2,1$ by continuity at the ends. The sign of $A'$ cannot change along the axis. A fixed noncollinear right-rim circle fixes the direction of its plane; its held two-jet collar or continuity from the identity selects the actual normal sign. This completes the stated plane-separation conclusion and does not apply to nonparallel planes or spatial section curves.

## What a physical correction must change

These obstructions have a direct differential interpretation. On an exact R772 material cylinder $F_{xe}$ is normal since $\langle F_{xe},F_x\rangle=\partial_e g_{xx}/2=0$ and $\langle F_{xe},F_e\rangle=\partial_x g_{ee}/2=0$. A vanishing mixed second fundamental form would therefore force $F(x,e)=U(x)+V(e)$, with $V$ closed. Any continuous isometric family from the initial field in this last class is rigid even if the transverse loops were initially allowed to become space curves. To see this, write $T=V'$, $S=U'$; both are unit and $T(e)\cdot S(x)=p(e)H(x)$ with $p=C'_Z$. The span $M$ of all $S(x)$ is not one-dimensional since a continuous unit vector on a fixed line is constant and $H$ varies. It is not three-dimensional since some $p=0$ then requires $T\perp M$. Thus $M$ is a plane. At a point where $p=1$ let $u$ be the projection of $T$ to $M$; projecting the equation against the spanning $S$ gives $P_M T(e)=p(e)u$. If $|u|<1$, its complementary component along a fixed normal to $M$ never vanishes and has constant sign, incompatible with $\int T=0$. Hence $|u|=1$, and for a suitable unit $n\perp M$ we have $T=p u+\epsilon(e)|C'_X| n$. Along a continuous configuration branch $M,u,n$ have continuous local choices (two independent actual $S$ at the start of each neighborhood suffice). At every fixed material $e$ with $C'_X\ne0$ the sign cannot change; with the initial identification this recovers the same signed $C'_X$ up to the common choice of $n$. Therefore the whole transverse curve remains congruent to its original. The first theorem now applies. In particular any nonrigid cylindrical phase in a continuous translation-only family from the identity is impossible. A path leaving the translation class and re-entering it can have other disconnected sign choices at such a re-entry.

Even without any a priori section-shape assumption there is a quantitative cost. Suppose the right axial material head remains fixed including its axial derivative and some label on the left rim moves inward in the world $Y$ direction by at least $.30$. Then on the exact cylinder $\sup|\mathrm{II}_{xe}|>.31$. Put $m=\sup|F_{xe}|$ and $a=(1+P/4)m$. Integrating from the stationary right edge shows $|F_e-C'|\le m$. Along the transverse material circle $F_x$ varies by at most $m$ times the shorter parameter distance. Each label is at distance at most $P/4$ from a horizontal tangent station with $C'=\pm e_X$ (use opposite side midpoints), and likewise from a vertical tangent station with $C'=\pm e_Z$. The mixed metric and $|F_x|=1$ at the respective stations therefore give $|F_{x,X}|\le a$ and $|F_{x,Z}-H(x)|\le a$ at the arbitrary label. If $a^2+(a+.01)^2<.51$, the whole field satisfies $|F_{x,Y}|>.7$ and takes the positive sign by its right collar. A full unit material axial line has world axial advance $>.7$. Its original advance is at most $1$, so inward motion by $.30$ at the specified left label is impossible. Since $P=4(.14+.27\pi/2+.001)<2.27$, $m\le.31$ would give $a^2+(a+.01)^2<.49<.51$. The normality already shown identifies $|F_{xe}|$ with the absolute covariant mixed second fundamental coefficient. This is a necessary property of an exact solution with the stated central contraction; it is not assumed as a test on numerical fields.
