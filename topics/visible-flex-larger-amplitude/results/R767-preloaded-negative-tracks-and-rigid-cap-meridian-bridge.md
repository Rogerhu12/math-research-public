# R767. Two preloaded negative tracks and a closed meridian bridge with rigid full caps

This is a new fixed initial metric on a material sphere. It is independent of R757. Its entire initial signed normal tube has radius at least $.08$, and $D=\operatorname{diam} F_0(M)<2.57$. For the complete new motion the central cylinder, the two outer rounded annuli and both entire nonplanar caps retain their full initial tensor. Each material meridian of the two remaining bands has its initial unit speed. These facts and a whole-image held witness $>.55$ continue to hold under arbitrary smooth even polarization fields described below. The fixed grip has diameter $>.528$. The source gives actual continuous finite-phase $C^2$ maps and their six spatial jets. The two angular/mixed rows on the remaining bands, whole positive-phase embedding and thickness and rigid-quotient separation have not been established.

## The closed moving section

In its profile plane use $T_\alpha=(\cos\alpha,\sin\alpha)$, $n_\alpha=(-\sin\alpha,\cos\alpha)$. Put $a=.08,b=.88,\delta=.01,l=.13$, and $t\in[0,\pi/2],\ \theta=.78\sin t$. Let $P$ be the unit integral of the following smoothed constant speed. On $0\le z\le1$ the unnormalized speed is $S_5(\min(z,1-z)/.06)$, with $S_5(u)=10u^3-15u^4+6u^5$ between 0 and 1 and constant outside. Its integral is $.94$; extend $P$ by zero and one. Thus $P(1-z)=1-P(z)$, $0<P'\le1/.94$ inside and its first two derivatives vanish at the two outer endpoints. With $B(x)=x-\sin(2\pi x)/(2\pi)$ write
$$
 P_L=P(x/l),\quad P_R=P((x-1+l)/l),\quad
 \alpha_\theta(x)=-a+2\delta B(x)+(a-\delta+\theta)P_L+(b-\delta-\theta)P_R,\qquad 0\le x\le1.
$$
The excess heading $\psi=\theta(P_L-P_R)$ is even about $1/2$ and its mean is $\theta(1-l)$. The entire *open* track has strictly increasing angle: $2\delta B'(x)>0$ in the open interval and the two pulse coefficients are positive, the smaller right coefficient at the last phase being $.09$. In the convention $n'=kT$ both tracks have $k_\theta=-\alpha_\theta'<0$ everywhere in their interiors. All angles are in $[-.08,.88]$ and the absolute curvature is below $.04+.87/(.13\cdot.94)<7.17$. The ratio also satisfies
$$
 0<k_0/k_\theta\le .87/.09<9.67
$$
on the controlled open tracks and extends at the isolated endpoint zeros by its one-sided limit. The lower bound on $|k_\theta|$ need not be uniform at an endpoint. There is no open straight track, including the long central part between the pulses.

Use two identical tracks with opposite spatial orientations. Set $\Gamma_\theta(x)=\int_0^x T_{\alpha_\theta(u)}du$, $J_\theta=\Gamma_\theta(1)$. Join their ends with two congruent smooth clockwise heads. Starting with heading $b$, a head turns by $\Theta=\pi+a+b$ at curvature at most $1/r$, $r=.23$. Ramp the curvature from 0 to $1/r$ with $S_5$ over length $e=.001$, keep it constant, and ramp to 0 symmetrically over length $e$. Its length is $r\Theta+e$. Its chord points along $-N$, where $E=T_{.4}$, $N=n_{.4}$, by midpoint symmetry of the turn. Write its integrated chord as $-2hN$. The upper track runs from $hN-J_\theta$ to $hN$, the right head to $-hN$, the lower track to $-hN-J_\theta$ with displacement $-\Gamma_\theta(x)$, and the left head returns to the starting point by the negative of the original head displacement. All angles and curvatures match at the four seams. The heads have strictly positive signed curvature in their interiors and the only angular curvature zeros of this full loop are those four joining stations. The right head is fixed in world coordinates; the left translates by $-(J_\theta-J_0)$.

For the comparison with an exact circle one may use the actual turn angle as variable. Arclength measure equals $r\,d\alpha$ plus a nonnegative measure of total mass $e$, because the curvature never exceeds $1/r$ and the full length is $r\Theta+e$. Every corresponding partial displacement differs from the circular one by at most $e$. The centered head endpoint error is at most $e/2$. Thus $|h-r\cos(.48)|\leq e/2$. The entire centered head is within $1.5e$ of the corresponding circle arc of radius $r$ about $r\sin(.48)E$. This comparison includes both smoothing intervals.

## All-phase planar normal coordinates

Both tracks are strictly monotone in their $E$ projection with tangents within $.48$ of $E$ in their left-to-right orientations. At equal left-to-right $x$ their separation vector is $2hN$ plus an error of length $<.072$. To verify this, its derivative is the difference of two unit vectors with angle difference $\alpha_0(1-x)-\alpha_0(x)$: the two excess headings are equal. For $0\leq x\leq1/2$ this difference in absolute value is at most $2\delta+(a+b-2\delta)(1-P_L)$ on the first pulse and at most $2\delta$ afterwards. The integral is bounded by $\delta+(a+b-2\delta)l/2=.0711$. For $x>1/2$ integrate backwards from the equal endpoint separation. For unequal track labels their difference includes an additional signed integral of tangents in the same cone. Project on the normal of its mean direction (any direction in the cone if that integral is zero). Hence the two full tracks are separated by at least $2h\cos(.48)-.072>.288$. The two end chords are perpendicular to $E$; the projected coordinate on each head first increases then decreases (or the reverse on the left). Each head extends outwards from its own chord. The two heads are therefore separated by at least $J_\theta\cdot E\geq\cos(.48)$. These arguments give a simple closed loop.

For the normal tube also check possible double-normal pairs. On one track the chord has nonzero projection on either endpoint tangent, by cone width $.96<\pi/2$. On one head distinct parallel tangent lines entail a turn of exactly $\pi$; projecting the connecting arc on its middle tangent gives length at least $r\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\cos u\,du=2r$. For a right-head point whose tangent line is parallel to a track, it lies either on the entrance portion with tangents in the track cone or on the exit portion with the reversed tangents in that cone. On the adjoining track and the corresponding head portion the continuous chord integral has nonzero projection on the track tangent. For the opposite track the chord equals the full end chord plus a signed integral of vectors in the cone: on the entrance portion add the entrance arc to the segment from the lower-track point to its right endpoint, both with rightward orientations. On the exit portion reverse both orientations. Projecting on the normal to that mean direction gives at least $2h\cos(.48)$. The left head is the central reflection and translation of the right. Together with the inter-track and inter-head bounds, every doubly normal chord has length $>.276$.

For completeness the planar tube threshold follows directly from the tangent-ball quotient $|d|^2/(2|n_p\cdot d|)$, $d=q-p$ (Federer, *Curvature Measures*, 1959, Theorem 4.18, p.449). A minimizing pair below $1/7.17$ cannot approach the diagonal by the Taylor expansion with bounded curvature. Differentiating at $p$ forces $d\cdot T_p=0$ since the alternative equality has quotient $1/|k(p)|$; differentiating at $q$ then gives $d\cdot T_q=0$. The quotient would be half a doubly normal chord. Thus every section in the family admits signed planar normal coordinates of radius at least $.137$. This argument applies to the profile curves themselves; a bent positive-phase bridge needs extra three-dimensional estimates.

## A genuinely curved initial bridge

Extrude the closed section between $y=\pm Y,\ Y=.13$. On each side use bridge length $L=.66$ and normalized transverse coordinate $u=v/L$. Set
$$
 p(u)=P(4u)P(4(1-u)),\qquad B_1(u)=\sin(2\pi P(u)),\qquad \gamma_0=.02 .
$$
The drift envelope $p$ is even, $0\le p\le1$, its mean is $.75$, and $p=p'=0$ at each outer endpoint. The preload $B_1$ is odd about $1/2$; it has no nontrivial constant open interval, and $B_1=B_1'=0$ at both outer endpoints. The initial positive-side tangent is
$$
 T_0(v)=\cos(\gamma_0 B_1(u))\,e_y-\sin(\gamma_0 B_1(u))\,n_0(s).
$$
The negative side is its axial reflection. Write $p_0(v),d_0(v)$ for the two arclength primitives of the cosine and sine. Then $d_0(L)=0$, $0\leq d_0(v)<.0066$, and the terminal axial advance is $P_0=p_0(L)\geq L\cos .02$. The entire true initial bridge tensor and curvature are
$$
 dv^2+(1-d_0(v)k_0(s))^2ds^2,\qquad K_0=\frac{k_0(s)d_0''(v)}{1-d_0(v)k_0(s)} .
$$
In particular $d_0''=\gamma_0\cos(\gamma_0 B_1) B_1'(u)/L$ has only two interior zeros; it is positive near each true bridge end and negative around its midpoint. Both hard boundaries of every active open track therefore have genuine negative-curvature material directly inside the bridge. The four isolated angular zero stations and the transverse zero curves are retained; no open rectangular flat band separates a moving track from its outer component. This is a different $g$ and a different Cauchy problem from R752/R756.

At the outer edge of each bridge attach an actual clockwise quarter turn of radius $r_y=.092$ with a $.001$ smoothing interval at each end, using the same curvature clock as on the heads. Its arclength is $L_o=.092\pi/2+.001$. Inward distance and axial advance are $d_o,p_o$, integrals of sine and cosine. Its field from the edge is $(q_0-d_o n_0,\pm(Y+P_0+p_o))$. At the terminal edge put $d_b=d_o(L_o)$, $p_b=p_o(L_o)$, both at most $.093$. Fill the full bounded parallel-curve disk $\Omega$ by the graph of height
$$
 H(X)=10^{-5}[1-|X-C|^2/.012^2]_+^4
$$
above the constant upper cap plane (reflected on the lower one). Here the marked center is $C=q_0(s_R)-.127n_0(s_R)$ on the normal at the rightmost $E$-projection station. Its distance from the original profile is $.127$ by the planar tube, so its distance from the terminal boundary is at least $.127-d_b>.034$. The support lies inside $\Omega$, both caps are nonplanar, and they are exactly planar near their boundaries. There is no rigidly imposed straight region of *bridge* in the new metric. The whole initial sphere is the actual fixed target $g=F_0^*\mathrm{eucl}$, including the entire nonflat graph on both sides.

## The initial full three-dimensional tube and diameter

For a meridian with tangent $(-\sin\alpha,\cos\alpha)$ in inward-normal/axial notation with spatial point $(q-dn,y)$, an outward signed offset $z$ has projected inward parameter $D=d-z\cos\alpha$ and axial coordinate $y+z\sin\alpha$. Use the axial reflection on the lower side. On the cylinder and bridge the transverse parameter stays in $(-.08,.087)$ for $|z|<.08$. On the exterior turn $d\leq r_y(1-\cos\alpha)+.001$ by its circular measure comparison, giving $-.08<D<.093$. All projections thus use one and the same injective planar tube. At fixed $z$ the axial coordinate increases strictly through the upper cylinder half, bridge and outer turn until the last rim:
$$
 \frac{d}{dv}(y+z\sin\alpha)=\cos\alpha(1+z\alpha')>0 .
$$
The bridge has $|\alpha'|\leq.02(2\pi)/(.94L)<.204$; the exterior turn has $\alpha'\le1/.092$. Where the axial height is fixed the derivative of $D$ with respect to $z$ is $-1/\cos\alpha<0$, by the two-dimensional Jacobian. For a fixed axial height the admissible $z$ interval is cut only by the fixed equator height and by $Y+P_0+p_b+z$ at the terminal rim. Thus distinct signed layers cannot coincide, including stationary joins and the center. Limits at the last rim are unique: $D=d_b$ there; for fixed $z$ the exterior portion has $D'= \sin\alpha(1+z\alpha')>0$ after its entrance, and in the bridge $D<.087<d_b$ (the lower circular bound gives $d_b\geq.092$). The same holds after reflection. This handles cross-patch as well as same-patch normal pairs.

For the cap graph its planar normal-offset map is $X\mapsto X-z\nabla H/\sqrt{1+|\nabla H|^2}$. Its global Lipschitz correction on the whole plane is at most $|z|(8+48)10^{-5}/.012^2<.312$. It fixes the exterior of the support disk, is a global diffeomorphism onto the plane by contraction, and preserves $\Omega$ and its boundary. For fixed projected point its axial height increases strictly with the outward parameter, with derivative $\sqrt{1+|\nabla H|^2}>0$ in graph coordinates. The side annuli project strictly outside $\Omega$ except at the common rim; both extreme signed cap layers remain on their own side of $y=0$. This proves global injectivity of the full signed normal map for $|z|<.08$, hence initial reach at least $.08$ (any closest-point displacement to a smooth embedded compact surface is normal).

At phase zero the entire planar projection lies in the original profile disk. For the track displacement, $0\le (J_0)_z<.07$: the sum of the initial headings at $x,1-x$ is nonnegative and their differences have magnitude $<\pi$, so the sine sums are nonnegative. An upper bound follows by integrating on the plateau with $|\alpha_0|\le\delta$, on the left ramp with $\alpha_0\le\delta$, and on the right with $\alpha_0\le\delta+(b-\delta)P_R$; this is at most $\delta+(b-\delta)l/2<.067$. Also $|(J_0)_x|\le1$. The head-circle comparison bounds each centered head in the radius $.338$ disk about its chord midpoint, since $r+r\sin .48+1.5e<.338$. Tracks have monotone $x$; for $z$ their only interior extrema are achieved when $\alpha_0=0$. The original $\Gamma_z$ ranges from at least $-\sin .08>-.081$ to $J_{0,z}$, since $\alpha_0$ increases. These track bounds lie inside the global head-based $z$ interval $[-.338-J_{0,z},.338]$: $h<.206$, and $h\sin(\pi/2-.4)+.081<.288$. Thus the initial box has side lengths $<1.677,.747,1.767$ including the crown heights. It gives $D<2.57$ and $.03D<.0771$. The right head contains an exact constant-radius arc of angular extent $>\pi$ (the two ramps consume less than $2e/r$ angle). Its open central extrusion is pointwise fixed at every phase; two opposite circle points at opposite axial ends of the closed grip give diameter at least $\sqrt{.46^2+.26^2}>.528>.2D$. Equivalently take the closed right-head strip including those points; its interior is open in the material sphere.

The initial lowest world $z$ is attained on the left head. Its minimum is minus the right head maximum minus $J_{0,z}$. The right head has $\max z+\min z>0$: its corresponding circular maximum and minimum both occur inside the swept angle (the horizontal tangent times are within the head), their sum is $2r\sin(.48)\sin(.4)>.08$ and the approximation error in their sum is at most $.003$. The lower track has $z\ge-hN_z-J_{0,z}$ as seen directly from monotonicity of $\alpha_0$; the upper one is higher because $2hN_z>.37>.081$. The cap and roll projections are inward. Hence a material point of the left head which attained the initial minimum now drops by $\Delta J_z$. At $\theta=.78$,
$$
 \Delta J_z=\int_0^1[\cos\alpha_0\sin\psi-(1-\cos\psi)\sin\alpha_0]\,dx>.55.
$$
Indeed the first integral is at least $[(1-2l)\cos\delta+l\cos b]\sin(.78)$ by concavity of sine and the two symmetric ramps. For the negative term use the plateau bound $\delta$, the nonpositive left-ramp angle (or the upper bound $\delta$), and convexity of $1-\cos\psi$ giving a trapezoidal factor $1/2$ on each ramp. Its upper bound is $(1-\cos .78)[(1-2l)\delta +l(\delta+\sin b)/2]$. Substitution gives $>.56$. Consequently every final whole image has Hausdorff distance $>.55>.2D$ from the original, independently of all choices of the two bridge interiors that maintain this common moving center. This is a set distance; no claim about a rigid quotient uses it.

## Exact spherical-velocity endpoint solution

In every positive phase translate both full exterior turns and their entire caps by the same profile-plane vector $-(J_\theta-J_0)/2$ and the side-dependent axial vector $\mp c\sin t\,e_y$, $c=.18$. They retain all three metric entries. Relative to the moving incoming point $q_\theta(s)$ the required bridge horizontal displacement is
$$
 h(s,t)=q_0(s)-q_\theta(s)-(J_\theta-J_0)/2,\qquad y_*(t)=P_0-c\sin t .
$$
At each track station the displacement is a difference of a partial and half a total integral of unit tangent changes; at either head it is plus or minus half that total. Thus $|h|\le\frac12\int_0^1 2\sin(\psi/2)\,dx \le .5\cdot .78(1-l)\sin t<.340\sin t$.

For a vector $b_h$ in the horizontal profile plane let $\beta=|b_h|$, and let $R_{b_h}(p)$ rotate $e_y$ toward $b_h$ by angle $\beta p$. Explicitly use Rodrigues with world axial vector $(b_{h,z},0,-b_{h,x})p$; its entire formula includes $b_h=0$. Pick any smooth even horizontal unit vector family $U(s,u,t)$ with $U(s,u,0)=-n_0(s)$. In the source the direction is $-\cos\chi\,n_\theta+\sin\chi\,T_\theta$ with any periodic even phase-dependent physical angle $\chi$ vanishing at zero. Use as meridian velocity
$$
 V=R_{b_h}(p(u))\{\cos(\gamma B_1(u))e_y+\sin(\gamma B_1(u))U(s,u,t)\}.
$$
It has unit norm. Integration of its odd term over $0\leq u\leq1$ is identically zero for any such $U$, since $R_{b_h}(p)$ is even. The two endpoint moment equations in the direction of $h$ and in the axial direction are exactly
$$
 X(\beta,\gamma)=\int_0^1\cos(\gamma B_1)\sin(\beta p)\,du=|h|/L,\qquad
 Z(\beta,\gamma)=\int_0^1\cos(\gamma B_1)\cos(\beta p)\,du=y_*/L .
$$
A unique solution exists in $0\leq\beta<\pi/2,\ 0<\gamma<\pi/2$. For fixed $\gamma$, $X$ strictly increases with $\beta$; $X_\gamma\le0$, $Z_\beta\le0$, $Z_\gamma<0$ when $\gamma>0$. At $\gamma=0$ attain the prescribed $X$: $\sin(\beta p)\geq p\sin\beta$, so $\beta\leq\arcsin(.340/(.75L))<.761$. The axial loss is at most $|h|\tan(\beta/2)<.137\sin t$, strictly below $L-y_*$ (also at $t=0$ as $P_0<L$). Increase $\gamma$ at fixed $X$, so $\beta$ is nondecreasing and $Z$ strictly decreases. If the branch reaches $\beta=\pi/2$, its axial value is at most the total nonplateau width $2(.25)=.5<y_*/L$. If it reaches $\gamma=\pi/2$ first, the axial value is at most $\int\cos[(\pi/2)B_1]$. Under the clock change $z=P(u)$ one has $du\ge .94\,dz$. The sets $|B_1|\ge 1/2$ and $|B_1|\ge\sqrt3/2$ therefore have measure at least $.94(2/3)$ and $.94/3$. They bound that last mean by
$$
 1-.94(2/3)(1-\cos(\pi/4))-(.94/3)[\cos(\pi/4)-\cos(\pi\sqrt3/4)]<.67,
$$
whereas $y_*/L\geq\cos(.02)-.18/.66>.72$. The root therefore occurs strictly inside the admissible upper boundaries. When $h=0$ fix $\beta=0$ and use the same scalar vertical argument.

The smooth actual unknowns are the two components of $b_h$ and $G=\gamma^2$. In the vector horizontal equation $b_h$ is multiplied by $\int p\,\operatorname{sinc}(\beta p)\cos(\gamma B_1)\,du>0$. The two radial scalar equations have nonzero Jacobian by the strict monotonicity above, and the transverse angular eigenvalue is the same positive integral. At $h=0$ these two horizontal eigenvalues coincide, the axial derivative in $G$ is negative, and the off-diagonal first horizontal derivatives of $Z$ are zero. The integrands are smooth functions of $\beta^2,G$ there. This proves regularity in $s,t$ by the implicit function theorem throughout the compact domain, with zero phase exactly $b_h=0,\gamma=.02$. Integrate $V$ from its actual initial position to obtain the full three-dimensional positive-side bridge, reflecting only the axial coordinate on the negative side with its own even polarization. At both ends $p=B_1=p'=B_1'=0$; hence $F_v=\pm e_y$ and $F_{vv}=0$ in the axial convention, and mixed derivatives vanish. Differentiating the exact vector moment with respect to $s$ gives both terminal tangential jets. This verifies the four physical joins including the true two end-cap edges. Every such closed map retains the true complete $g_{vv}=1$; only the $ss$ and $sv$ bridge components remain to be solved. General even polarization is a real nontrivial physical freedom and preserves the same whole caps and all endpoint data, not a relabeling.

## Fixed cap material atlas and scope of the source

The parallel cap boundary at $d_b$ remains a simple closed curve, with speed factor $1-d_b k>0$. Each head extreme in the $E$ direction lies on a true circular portion. The two tracks have positive $E$ speed, and the right/left heads each have precisely one $E$ extremum. The cap domain is therefore between two true graphs $Z_\pm(X)$ in $(E,N)$, meeting only at their extreme abscissae. If $X=C_h+R_hx$ normalizes these abscissae to $x=\pm1$, the closed unit disk maps onto the material domain by
$$
 \Psi(x,z)=(X,\ (Z_++Z_-)/2 + z\, (Z_+-Z_-)/(2\sqrt{1-x^2})).
$$
In the interior this is a regular diffeomorphism with positive vertical Jacobian. At either extreme the two graphs are on the same actual circle of radius $r-d_b$, so their mean is constant and the halfheight quotient is $\sqrt{R_h[2(r-d_b)-R_h(1\mp x)]/(1\pm x)}$. This extends regularly with positive derivative in the vertical variable. The source switches to this expression for $|x|>.99$: $R_h<.84$ and a departure in $X$ below $.0084$ corresponds to an angle $<.36$ on that circle since $r-d_b>.137$; both arc endpoints and the smoothing intervals are farther than one radian from the extreme. The noncircular portions are $C^2$ in the plane. Thus the two disk centers and all boundary material points have regular charts.

The source's polar chart is $\Psi(r\Psi^{-1}(q_0(s)-d_b n_0(s)))$ and the straight normal collar is $q_0(s)-(d_b+w)n_0(s)$. The material transition is computed by applying $\Psi^{-1}$, taking radius and inverting the strictly monotone lifted boundary angle. The source differentiates this actual change twice. In general $w$ is not $1-r$. The Cartesian cap chart uses the original world-plane $x,z$ point. Both cap heights and all chart changes are frozen at phase zero. The scripts use split Gaussian integration, monotone root inversion and implicit first/second derivatives. Their floating-point output is not an interval proof for a particular optimized field.

The construction pays all initial global geometric estimates and the exact partial metric/strong motion claims above. Its new $g$ has genuine curvature of both signs on the physical bridge. It leaves a full two-dimensional nonlinear metric problem and all potentially close positive-phase space pairs involving either bridge. Without those costs and convergence on the fixed new $g$, this is an intermediate result.
