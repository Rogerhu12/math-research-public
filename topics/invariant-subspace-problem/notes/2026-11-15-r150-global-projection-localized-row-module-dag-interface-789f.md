## 2. The ring and the only allowed polynomial divisions

Put

$$
\begin{aligned}
F_+(s)&=5s^3-25s^2+32s-8,\\
F_-(s)&=5s^3+135s^2+864s+864,\\
E_8(s)&=5s^8+50s^7+153s^6-936s^5-2888s^4\\
&\qquad +15936s^3-3392s^2-25472s+9600,\\
\Sigma_0(s)&=s(s-2)(s+6)F_+(s)F_-(s)E_8(s),\\
S&=\mathbb Q[s,\Sigma_0^{-1}].
\end{aligned}
$$

For a polynomial row $v$, the algorithm may divide all its entries by
a common power of one of the six displayed factors. Every such division
is checked to be exact in $\mathbb Q[s]$. It may also multiply the row by
a nonzero rational number to clear and then remove constant content.

Thus every normalization is exactly

$$
v\longmapsto
\frac{c\,v}{s^{e_0}(s-2)^{e_1}(s+6)^{e_2}
F_+^{e_3}F_-^{e_4}E_8^{e_5}},
\qquad c\in\mathbb Q^\times,\quad e_i\ge0.
$$

It preserves the row module over $S$. It can enlarge the row module over
$\mathbb Q[s]$: if $v=fv'$ with $f\mid\Sigma_0^\infty$, then $v$ belongs
to the polynomial row module generated by $v'$. No unknown factor is
divided out. In particular the final certificate does not claim equality
of the unlocalized polynomial row modules.

## 4. Complete polynomial quotient and strict decrease

For a nonzero row $v$, order its leading term by the pair

$$
(\deg v,\operatorname{lp}v),
$$

where the largest column index breaks ties in degree. Suppose that
$current$ and a basis row $b$ have the same leading position $p$.
If necessary they are exchanged so that

$$
\deg current_p\ge\deg b_p.
$$

Use the complete polynomial quotient and remainder

$$
current_p=q\,b_p+r,\qquad \deg r<\deg b_p,
$$

and replace the whole row by $current-qb$, followed by the allowed
normalization. The remainder $r$ is retained and checked exactly.

Write $d=\deg current$ and $e=\deg b$. For $j>p$ one has
$\deg b_j<e$ and $\deg current_j<d$, so column $j$ has degree below $d$
after subtraction. For $j<p$, the new entry may have degree $d$, but
its position is smaller than $p$. The $p$-th entry has degree below
$e\le d$. Consequently every nonzero new row has strictly smaller
leading degree-position pair. Known-factor division can only decrease
its degree further.

This is the standard polynomial row-reduction step with the full
quotient. It discards no nonzero remainder and makes no heuristic
coefficient truncation.

The actual old pending row is the first current row used. The input
counter remains $20$ until that remainder is resolved. No original
row is skipped because it had merely been started in the earlier run.

## 5. The two containment checks

Let $B$ be the final $22\times22$ weak Popov basis, with one nonzero row
at each leading position. The forward DAG verification proves

$$
\operatorname{row}_S(B)
\subseteq \operatorname{row}_S(A).
$$

For the other direction, every one of the $56$ original rows is reduced
by $B$ using complete polynomial quotients. **This reverse reduction does
not divide by any polynomial factor.** It must terminate at the zero
row. Each successful original row is saved separately.

These checks give the stronger reverse inclusion

$$
\operatorname{row}_{\mathbb Q[s]}(A)
\subseteq \operatorname{row}_{\mathbb Q[s]}(B),
$$

and hence

$$
\operatorname{row}_S(A)=\operatorname{row}_S(B).
$$

Reverse reduction remains legitimate because every allowed common-factor
division enlarges the current polynomial row module. The computation
nevertheless performs all $56$ checks; it does not infer them solely
from the old prefix counter.

A candidate basis is not a verified basis until both inclusions have
passed. In particular no divisor is reported from an unfinished state.

## 6. The determinant and the outside divisor

Over the PID $S$, equality of the row modules implies equality of their
maximal-minor ideals:

$$
I_{22}(A)S=(\det B)S.
$$

A subsequent exact row-Hermite computation uses polynomial quotient and
remainder operations, row exchanges, rational rescaling, and the same
checked known-factor divisions. These are all invertible row operations
over $S$. Thus its triangular diagonal product differs from $\det B$
only by a unit of $S$.

Remove every factor supported on $\Sigma_0$ from that product and make
the remainder monic. The program additionally checks that its gcd with
each of the six factors has degree zero. The result is exactly the outside part $\Omega$
of the complete original projection divisor. It is not the divisor of
one arbitrarily selected original minor.

This conclusion relies on the already proved core reduction over $S$
and on the new forward, reverse, and triangular checks. The code reports
it only after all of them succeed. The current outside-degree bound

If a later complete computation reports $d=\deg\Omega$, the established
