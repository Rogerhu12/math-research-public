## Chalendar, Esterle and Partington

Primary author PDF: [Boundary values of analytic semigroups and associated norm estimates](https://www.math.u-bordeaux.fr/~jesterle/boundary-ba09v9.pdf). The introduction and Sections 2–3 through Remark 3.1 were personally read in this session.

Definition 2.2 requires a commutative Banach algebra with no nonzero annihilator and an element generating a norm-dense principal ideal. Definition 2.3 imposes a common denominator and a norm bound for scaled powers. Lemma 2.4 constructs a Banach module only after this pseudoboundedness input. Section 3 starts with an existing analytic semigroup; it does not construct fractional powers of an arbitrary given operator.

For the current Haar family, Hilbert-space quasiaffinity and contractive strong units alone have not supplied these algebra-norm hypotheses or an analytic semigroup with the prescribed element $C$. No theorem from this paper was imported to claim a root of $C$.

## Sarason

Primary journal PDF: [Unbounded operators commuting with restricted backward shifts](https://files.ele-math.com/articles/oam-02-36.pdf), Operators and Matrices 2 (2008), 583–601. The retrieved original text through Section 5.6 was personally read; the full final classification was not adopted.

The setting is the restricted backward shift on a model space $H^2\ominus uH^2$ with a nonconstant inner function. No identification of the original Haar operator, or of an unknown invariant restriction, with that model has been proved here. The paper therefore supplies no presently justified closed-root or invariant-subspace conclusion for our operator.

## Çağlar and Ercan

Primary publication: [Invariant subspaces for positive operators on locally convex solid Riesz spaces](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0019357707800309), Indagationes Mathematicae 18 (2007), 417–420. The [author-posted original article text](https://www.academia.edu/61692303/Invariant_subspaces_for_positive_operators_on_locally_convex_solid_Riesz_spaces) was read through Theorem 2.2.

The standing assumption immediately before Theorem 2.1 is that the Riesz space is discrete and Archimedean. The theorem's proof chooses an atom and its rank-one order projection. The natural Haar order on $L^2(\mathbb Z_2)$ is nonatomic, so the abstract alone cannot justify applying this theorem there. No alternative atomic positive representation has been established by this reading, and no new invariant subspace is claimed.
